Chain on the bench-calibrated plant

Every chain rollout before today ran on the template’s actuator model, which the bench calibrated on 2026-09-02 as about 60× too stiff. Re-run on the calibrated plant, the chain’s grasp lifts at a fifth of the tool’s weight, the fingers turn the tool 0–10° at any travel budget, and five of eight hands still carry it to the countersink because the arm does the reorientation. The loss is now the handover and the gait, and it has the same cause as the turn.

511 rollouts in four sweeps plus two instrumented runs · calibrated plant kp 0.5, force ceiling 0.35 N·m, frictionloss 0.0035, measured masses (docs/experiments/20260902-servo-sysid) · scored on the signed cosine with a load test at 0.240 N · simulation only

The chain scenes are generated from the base template and inherit its finger actuators: kp 30, forcerange ±10, no friction, capsule-volume masses. The servo system identification fitted the real gain at kp 0.5 against the bench’s own hold deficits and cross-validated it on retention (0.94 sim, 0.92 bench) and alignment (0.638, 0.593). The 2,214-rollout record of 2026-09-08 never went through that model. --plant corrected rewrites each hand’s arm scene through apply_measured_plant.py, cached beside the shipped one.

Grip force at the lift falls from 4–25 N to 0.16–1.43 N. D8 does not close on the tool. D7, the hand that holds 10 of 10 on hardware, lifts at 0.16 N — below the tool’s 0.240 N. Force on a position servo is gain times position error, and the chain closes 2 mm past contact because at kp 30 the plans’ 10 mm produced ~30 N and ejected the tool. At kp 0.5 the same 2 mm is ~1 N.

Element 1: the grasp, at the plans’ own squeeze

--squeeze-mm 4,6,8,10 --plant corrected · 127 rollouts

At 8–10 mm every hand lifts on three or four pads at 2.5–4.5 N, comparable to the shipped plant’s grasps and to the bench, whose exported plans carry exactly this 10 mm. The chain’s 2 mm was a shipped-plant number. Element 1 is set at 10 mm for the rest of the page.

Through the turn the fingers sit 25–36° behind their commands and no actuator is on its ceiling. They are not tripping the torque limit; at kp 0.5 they do not develop the torque to roll the tool against the thumb. The bench recorded the same state on 2026-08-29 — “yaws stall; index yaw does not move at all” — and +11.7° of middle-yaw deficit at a static hold. The shipped plant reads 0.0° on every finger at every seam, which is why it never showed this.

The pivot-height result of 2026-09-06 was therefore a rigid-actuator artifact. Which pole the tool turns to is decided by a finger motion that does not happen on the real plant.

The arm’s iterated rigid transfer stands the held tool up: D3, D4, D5, D6 and D7 reach staged at cos +0.999–1.000 on three pads at 1.3–2.1 N, and set it into the countersink 17–20 of 20. D7 and D4 are the hardware 10/10 and 9/10 hands. D2, the other 10/10 hand, loses the tool between regripped and staged; D1 and D8 lose it at the regrip. The handover then costs each surviving hand 2–8 of 20, and the gait turns the tool 0.00–0.02 revolutions and leaves it at 8–75°.

The handover and the gait fail the way the turn does. At handover_thumb the thumb is 27° behind its command; at handover_index the index is 42° behind; through the gait the middle finger runs 14–38° behind. Both maneuvers are position-command sequences composed on the rigid plant, and on the calibrated plant the fingers execute a fraction of each move, so the tripod never forms and the stroke turns air.

What this settles, and the next measurement

  • Element 1 is set. 10 mm squeeze on the calibrated plant, which is the squeeze the hardware plans already carry. Every hand lifts.
  • Element 2 is an arm reorientation. On the calibrated plant the fingers contribute 0–10° and the UR5e’s iterated rigid transfer contributes the rest, on a grip that holds. Five hands reach the countersink. This is the deployable maneuver; a finger-only reorientation is not on this plant at this gain.
  • Elements 4 and 5 are the open loss, and the deficit that causes it is the one quantity the hardware measures directly: every servo reports its own position at 111 Hz on the bus, and the calibrated plant shows 14–42° of commanded-minus-achieved through the handover and gait.

The next measurement, named: close a loop on achieved joint angle — command = target + k(target − achieved), the integral term the SCS0009 lacks (i_coefficient 0) added on the host — and re-run the handover and gait on the calibrated plant. Accept if q_err_deg through handover_* drops under 10° on D6 and the gait turns ≥ 0.5 revolutions in 8 cycles. Falsify if the loop drives the fingers into the torque ceiling (sat > 0) before the error closes. This loop reads no object pose and no contact force; it is the same signal the bench regrip of 2026-08-29 used.

Two things this page does not settle. D2 holds 10/10 on hardware and loses the tool at the arm transfer here on every seed; the regrip that precedes it is a 0.3 mm re-squeeze from the commanded pose, and on the calibrated plant that is the phase to instrument next for that hand. And the calibrated plant has been validated on the bench maneuver, not on the chain: its 0.35 N·m ceiling is the protective-torque plateau, which the real servo only reaches after sustained overload.
