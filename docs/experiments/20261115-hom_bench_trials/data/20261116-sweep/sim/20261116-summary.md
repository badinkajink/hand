# Sim-real reversal of ground-clear vs low (film) twist grasps, 2026-11-16 13:40-14:30 (workstation study)

Real (corvus, 2026-11-16): film grasps (contacts 18-20 deg below the equator) rose 14-27 mm into the hand, tilted, and
tracked the commanded twist poorly (1 - err/ref 0-0.19); ground-clear grasps (2-5 deg below) rose 3 mm, tilted 13 deg
and tracked 0.38-0.40. Simulation's grid scores: film 0.756 / 0.643, ground-clear 0.153 / 0.172.

Files here: trials/ (merged real trials), rev_features.py, rev_sweep.py (open-loop replay sweeps), rev_stem.py
(scenes/sph040_stem_block.xml: the ball 21.0 g + stem 4 mm x 36.9 mm 0.6 g + block r 15 mm x 17 mm 6.5 g, a table under
the block until the lift in place of the post), rev_joints.py, rev_fit.py (fit_stem.json), rev_closed.py (closed-loop
HOM, hom_bench.simulate, seeds 0-2; the controller keeps its bare-ball model), closed_*.json.

1. Open-loop replay at the default plant (bare ball, mu 1.0): the sim ball over-twists (24-95 deg vs real 10-42) and
   tilts (16-124 deg vs 5-14); the (70,110) film trials drop; film push-up under-predicted (5-11 vs 14-27 mm).
2. Open-loop sweeps (mu 0.3-1.0, tau_max, kp, dead band): no single change reproduces both behaviours open loop; low mu
   gives the film push-up scale (12-36 mm) but drops every trial. The real joints stall far from their targets (thumb
   MCP 25 deg, middle yaw 20 deg short in the ground-clear run) where the sim's joints follow.
3. Open-loop fit (mu, kp, tau_max, damping, dead band; stem scene; 4 train / 2 test): cost 65.8 -> 36.8 train, 64.6 ->
   47.6 test, but at the bounds (mu 2.45, kp 0.30, damping 0.080, dead band 1.27): a sluggish sticky hand that moves the
   object less, an artifact of replaying closed-loop commands open loop; closed loop it does not flip the ranking.

5. The grid's low J for the ground-clear grasps is mostly J's contact factor, not the twist: their tracking is .62-.74 at
   every mu, but the index pad carries > 0.05 N only 20-39 % of the task (thumb and middle 97-100 %).

Explanation: the scenes' friction (1.0, standing for grip tape) is about twice the real pad-on-taped-sphere friction
(TPU on PLA measured 0.37-0.44); below ~0.6 contacts 18-20 deg under the equator cannot hold the squeeze (tan 18 deg =
0.32 of each normal force pushes up; HOM's force-by-offset squeezes harder as the fingers lag), so the ball climbs and
the object tilts. The real object's stem and block (centre of mass ~15 mm low, ~8x the ball's rolling inertia) keep the
ground-clear grasps upright at that friction; the bare ball rolls. Discriminating experiments: measure pad-on-tape
friction (UR5 + HEX-E press-and-drag); repeat the film grasps with the sphere in grip tape (sim at mu 1.0 predicts rise
~5 mm and track ~.75); record the fingertip boards to see whether the index floats in the ground-clear grasps.
