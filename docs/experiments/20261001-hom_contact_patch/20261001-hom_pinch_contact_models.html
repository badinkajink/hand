Pinch friction torque in Drake hydroelastic and MuJoCo contact models

Wang, Oh and Pollard load a screwdriver by loosening a two-finger pinch until gravity turns the tool about the pinch axis, and they used Drake’s hydroelastic contact because a point contact has no friction torque about that axis. On a two-pad rig with the real_v1 fingertip and screwdriver, Drake’s hydroelastic pad at E = 10 MPa resists that rotation with μN·r̄ per pad, r̄ = 1.00 mm·(N / 1 N)0.25. MuJoCo reproduces the torque two ways: condim 4 with the torsional coefficient rescheduled every step from the pad’s normal force, within 1 %, and fingertip pads packed with 0.75 mm spheres at 0.5–1 mm spacing, within 3–7 %, with stiffness set from E and nothing fitted to Drake. With a short contact relaxation time, lowering the pinch from 6 N to 0.2 N lowers the tool to vertical along the quasi-static brake law in both simulators: 45° is passed at 1.20 N in Drake and 1.23 N with the sphere pads, against 1.22 N predicted. At Drake’s default relaxation of 0.1 s the brake gives way as the tool speeds up and the tool swings past vertical, to 123°. With point contact in either simulator the tool swings over the top as soon as its weight comes on.

The pinch in the paper and the rig

what the load step asks of the contact model

The paper’s pinch sub-assembly acts as a fixed joint when its two contact forces are large and as a revolute joint about the pinch axis when they are small. The load step lowers the force until the tool turns under gravity. The torque a pinch resists about its own axis is friction spread over the contact patch, μ∫p ρ dA, which a single contact point does not produce. The paper also takes the centre of pressure of each hydroelastic contact surface as the instantaneous contact point from which it builds its generalized contact frames.

The rig isolates that hinge. Two spheres of the real_v1 fingertip radius ride on rails along the pinch axis and push with a set force N each. They squeeze the real_v1 screwdriver cylinder, whose axis starts horizontal and perpendicular to the pinch axis with its centre of mass d from the pinch line. Masses, geometry, friction and the time step are the same in both simulators; only the contact model changes.

Friction torque against pinch force

gravity off · the tool is spun about the pinch axis at 2 rad/s by a stiff drive · r̄ = steady drive torque / (2 μ N)

Drake’s torque arm follows the elastic-foundation law of a sphere pressed into a cylinder. Drake’s pressure field for a compliant sphere is linear in depth, p = E·depth / R. Pressed on a rigid plate (exp_plate, 0.25–4 N), the pad’s penetration gives F = πEδ² to within 3 % and a pressure-weighted arm of 0.527–0.530 of the patch radius, against 8/15 = 0.533 for a paraboloidal pressure field. On the cylinder the arm is 1.00 mm at 1 N, against 0.97 mm from the foundation integral, and it grows as N0.25, so a fourfold force widens it by 41 %.

Both point contact models give zero torque at every force. condim 4 with a constant μt equal to Drake’s arm at 1 N is exact there and 42 % high at 0.25 N and 30 % low at 4 N; rescheduling μt every step removes that error. The sphere pads come within 7 % of Drake at 1 mm spacing (9 spheres in contact at 1 N) and 3 % at 0.5 mm (37). At 2 mm spacing one or two spheres touch below 1 N and the arm falls to 0.30 mm at 0.25 N.

Rate dependence and the contact relaxation time

torque arm per pad at 1 N (4 N where marked), spun at 0.5 and 2 rad/s

Drake’s hydroelastic torque is independent of the spin rate only when the contact’s relaxation time is short. At SAP’s default of 0.1 s the arm at 1 N drops from 0.97 mm at 0.5 rad/s to 0.77 mm at 2 rad/s, and the contact surface loses about half its faces. Turning the tool about the pinch axis moves the cylinder’s surface along the pad normal at ω·y·z / Rc inside the patch, toward the pad in two quadrants and away from it in the other two. The Kelvin–Voigt term adds force where the surface approaches and removes it where it recedes; the receding quadrants leave the patch and the arm shrinks. The sphere pads with a 0.1 s relaxation lose 10 % the same way. The sphere pads at 0.02 s and Drake at 0.01 and 0.02 s change by 1 % or less between the two rates.

MuJoCo’s friction is also regularized. At impratio 10, the value in the calibrated real_v1 plant, condim 4 at 4 N delivers 25 % of its torque at 0.5 rad/s and the sphere pads 66 %; at impratio 100 both are rate-independent.

The gravity brake

gravity on · centre of mass d from the pinch line · pinch held at 6 N, then lowered geometrically to 0.2 N over 4 s · φ = rotation of the tool axis about the pinch axis, 0 horizontal, 90° hanging

Every model with friction torque lowers the tool to vertical as the pinch force falls. From about 45° on, the angle follows the quasi-static brake law cos φ = 2μ N r̄(N) / (m g d), corrected for the friction the pads spend carrying the weight. For d = 15 mm it places 45° at 1.22 N and 80° at 0.41 N. Drake at 0.01 s passes them at 1.20 and 0.39 N, the 0.5 mm sphere pads at 1.23 and 0.43 N, and the rescheduled condim 4 at 1.20 and 0.41 N.

Near horizontal, cos φ is flat, so the equilibrium angle moves quickly with N; every model breaks away there and sticks past the quasi-static angle before the falling force catches up. A 12 s ramp lowers Drake’s peak swing rate from 293 to 138 deg/s and the sphere pads’ from 250 to 86 deg/s.

With the 0.1 s relaxation the brake weakens as the tool speeds up. Drake drops the tool from 45° to 80° between 1.13 and 1.07 N and swings it to 123°, past vertical; the sphere pads with the same relaxation swing it to 114°. At 0.02 s Drake still falls through 80° at 1.09 N on the 4 s ramp, while the sphere pads at 0.02 s pass 80° at 0.43 N, so per second of relaxation the weakening is stronger in Drake than in MuJoCo. On the 12 s ramp the swing is slower and Drake at 0.02 s follows the law to 80° (0.41 N).

With point contact there is no friction torque. The tool swings through 179° in Drake and 175° in MuJoCo as its weight comes on at 6 N and keeps swinging. At impratio 10 the condim 4 pinch creeps from the start and passes 10° before the ramp begins.

Centre of pressure

one pad at 0.21 mm penetration (about 1 N), rotated about its own centre through 20° in 0.25° steps · tangential distance of the centre of pressure from the analytic contact point

For a sphere against a cylinder the centre of pressure stays at the closest point, so any tangential motion of it under a pad rotation is error in the generalized contact frame’s origin. MuJoCo’s single sphere–cylinder contact is the analytic point. Drake’s contact surface stays within 2.5 µm. The sphere pads’ penetration-weighted centroid stays within 0.015 mm at 0.5 mm spacing and 0.07 mm at 1 mm, and wanders 0.71 mm at 2 mm, jumping 0.29 mm between neighbouring poses.

What a MuJoCo port of the load step needs

  • For the pinch hinge, condim 4 with an elliptic cone and impratio 100, μt rescheduled every step from the pad’s normal force as μ·r̄(N), r̄ = 1.00 mm·(N / 1 N)1/4 for the real_v1 fingertip at 10 MPa. The arm scales as E−1/4R1/2. One contact per pad; the reschedule is a write to geom_friction (or pair_friction) before each step and costs nothing measurable.
  • Where the patch itself matters (a flat or mesh pad, a centre of pressure off a non-primitive finger), sphere pads: 0.75 mm spheres at 1 mm spacing, 126 per 35° cap and 9–22 in contact, stiffness from E. The 4 s brake run takes 0.2 s of CPU for 5.5 s simulated, against 0.1 s for condim 4 and 10.1 s for Drake.
  • The calibrated real_v1 plant runs impratio 10, at which both MuJoCo pinches creep; a brake needs 100.
  • A Drake reproduction of the paper should set the hydroelastic relaxation time explicitly: 0.02 s already turns the lower half of the swing into a fall.

What this does not settle

  1. The pad modulus. E = 10 MPa is Drake’s default, not a measurement of the real_v1 pads; at a given force the torque scales as E−1/4, so 1 MPa gives 1.78× of it. The bench measurement open since 2026-09-16 fixes it: pinch with thumb and index, middle open, step the pinch load down and read the tag angle at each step. The law is falsified if the held cos φ does not scale as N5/4.
  2. The hand. The rails here are force-controlled. The same brake on SR2 (D8 gantry, calibrated position servos, pad force commanded through JT) is on its own page, SR2 pinch-brake loading (docs/experiments/20261001-hom_hand_brake/20261001-hom_hand_brake.html): Drake hydroelastic and MuJoCo rescheduled condim 4 agree within 3 % at the 45° and 80° crossings. Still open there: the grasp picked off the table inside the chain, where the tool already hangs on two pads when the lift ends.
  3. The controller. The paper’s generalized contact frames, least-squares relative-velocity planner and complementary filter (its Eqs. 1–6) are not implemented here. Their inputs, body twists and Jacobians, closest points from mj_geomDistance and centres of pressure from contacts, are all available in MuJoCo 3.6.
  4. MuJoCo Warp. Sphere pads multiply each pad’s contact count by 10–50; their cost at 4096 parallel environments is unmeasured.
