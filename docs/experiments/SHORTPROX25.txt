## 4. FROM-SCRATCH B — 0 for 4, UNRESOLVED

b33's own config has init_actor_checkpoint=None: it was trained FROM SCRATCH at 20M ts, with
a10 driving the live-A reset rather than seeding the actor. So the apples-to-apples length
comparison is a from-scratch B, and it did not produce one.

  t1  COLLAPSED  oscillated 0.02-0.12 for 55 iters, watchdog at iter 55   (genuine draw failure)
  t2  CRASHED    NaN in actor observation group at iter 24
  t3  CRASHED    NaN in actor observation group at iter 42
  t4  COLLAPSED  oscillated, watchdog                                     (genuine draw failure)

The NaN cause is NOT identified. Ruled out:
  - shared Warp kernel cache (each run gets its own mktemp -d)
  - relaunching before the GPU drained (gpu_idle now runs before EVERY attempt; crashes persist)
  - palm interpenetration / clearance. The mcp_frame<->palm contact sits at -11.00 mm and ~677 N
    in EVERY baseline scene including m05 — it is pre-existing, not introduced here — and over
    4000 random joint poses per hand the penetration is -11.00 mm in BOTH, invariant to joint
    angle. Identical in the two hands, so it cannot explain a difference between them.
The control that makes this a real asymmetry: b33 IS a successful from-scratch B on m05.

CAUTION, and it nearly went into this file as a result: the first survival check tested only for
the collapse sentinel, so it reported the iter-24 NaN crash as a SURVIVING run. A crashed run and
a converged one are indistinguishable from the metrics alone. train_B_sp25_attempts.sh now fails
an attempt on a Traceback, a missing checkpoint, or a newest-checkpoint of model_0.pt.


## 5. WHAT IS AND IS NOT ESTABLISHED

ESTABLISHED — the 25 mm hand is VIABLE and grips BETTER. Cleaner CEM grasp on every stability
term, better A health than a10 on force / jitter / de-centering, and a PERFECT hold rate through
the handoff (1.000 vs 0.969). These are low-variance measurements.

NOT ESTABLISHED — that 25 mm is worse for reorienting. The measured gap (0.94 vs ~0.5) is about
1.5x the known per-design draw sd of 0.3-0.5, on n=1 A draw and n=1 B draw per length, with the
evaluator itself moving +-0.16 on repeats of one checkpoint. The partial-turn-then-unload
signature is mechanistically suggestive and worth a look, but it is not a length verdict.

A real length verdict needs several independent A->B draws per length — days, not hours. This is
the same resolution ceiling the morphology program hit and closed out on (morph_sweep_STATUS.md:
per-design draw sd 0.3-0.5, gate-invisible, A-grade even anti-orders outcomes).
