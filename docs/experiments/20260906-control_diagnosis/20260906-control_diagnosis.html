Why the deployed hands stopped reorienting the screwdriver

One configuration has ever run this task end to end, and it still does. Four things changed on the way to the deployed plans; ablating them one at a time shows the pivot height decides which end of the tool points down, and the grasp fitter spends the finger travel the turn is paid for in.

Ablation off the reproduced 2026-09-03 chain · 492 rollouts · scored on the SIGNED cosine with a load test · no hardware run

The reference, and why it had to be recovered first

results/phase1/real_v1/rv05_manual_stored · 169/169 on 2026-09-03

The fingers take the shaft from horizontal to 4.8° off vertical in the air, on three pads, tip down, then the arm sets it into the countersink and the hand gaits it eight cycles:

lifted      cos -0.0005  z 0.1121  3 pads   5.55 N
turned      cos +0.8404  z 0.1145  3 pads  22.32 N
reoriented  cos +0.9939  z 0.1129  3 pads  10.47 N
upright     cos +1.0000  z 0.0499  2 pads          tip down
gaited      cos +0.9946                    8/8 cycles   OK

On 2026-09-06 that configuration would not execute at all. GantryPalm has no worst_pos, which probe_real_v1_chain reads unconditionally when reindex is "full", so the entire floating-palm path raised AttributeError. Nothing had noticed, because every run since the arm work went through the arm path. One line in palm_driver.py, and it reproduces.

It is now pinned by tests/test_chain_reference.py, which asserts the signed cosine above 0.90 at reoriented, at least two pads carrying at least the tool's 0.240 N, floor-free, and 8 of 8 gait cycles. Verified to discriminate: at the deployed pivot height it fails with cos 0.0008.

The pivot height is the 180°. axis_k sets the height of the point the finger anchor is rotated about, in half-straddles above the contact plane. At 0.05 the lift is bit-for-bit identical to the reference — three pads, 5.55 N, z 0.1121, because the pivot only enters after the lift — and the turn then carries the tool to cos −0.988: within 9° of vertical, standing on its handle, and it drops. Every deployed plan carries 0.05 to 0.15.

The clip bleeds the grip. At 0.85 the tool reorients correctly, cos +0.970, and the pad force falls from 5.55 N to 0.15 N across the same 1.1 s against a 0.240 N tool. It arrives vertical in a hand that is no longer holding it, and is lost on the way down. The plan angle of −60° simply under-rotates and still chains 3 of 4.

The two pad arms are not evidence about pads. Both fail at the lift, at z 0.0124 with zero contacts: the stored CEM anchor replayed on a flat pad whose contacting face sits 3 mm further out, and the fitter's anchor replayed on spherical pads. Flat pads with a grasp fitted for them — the last row — lift on three pads at 3.37 N and turn to cos +0.755. The pad shape is not the blocker.

What the fitter spends

mount-to-pad distance at the closed grip, against the 68.11 mm chain

The reference grasp comes from a CEM search over rollouts. The deployed hands are Sobol-sampled and have no CEM run, so their grasp comes from probe_real_v1_carry._grip_from_fit, a geometric IK fit. Its own docstring names the problem it creates:

GRIP DEPTH IS A VARIABLE, NOT AN OPTIMUM. `fit` normally takes the DEEPEST reachable
palm height, for clearance over the shaft's upper half once it stands up. On this hand
that lands every design at 52-65 mm of a 68.11 mm mount-to-pad chain -- 95% extension, the
boundary of the reach shell, where the fingertip has no radial workspace left and so
cannot carry the object anywhere.

Measured, it is worse than the comment says. The turn is paid for in finger extension, and the fitted grasp arrives at the turn with between 0.44 and 1.26 mm of chain left, against the reference grasp's 2.74 mm.

The 4 mm cell is the one that matters. It lifts on three pads at 8.79 N — more grip than the reference's 5.55 N — and is on the floor by the next seam. Grip force at the lift is therefore not the missing quantity; travel left in the fingers is.

Lowering the palm is the direct fix and the fitter will not do it: asked for 62, 58, 54, 50 and 46 mm of grip depth on this hand, _grip_from_fit returns no pose at every one. Its clamp searches only [depth − 8 mm, depth], and on this hand the only depth that solves is the deepest. The feasible set of the fitter lies entirely inside the reach shell, so this is not a flag to turn down.

Two shapes, and the load test separates them. sv1_u0060_b75 reaches cos +0.989 on 0.3 pads at 0.09 N — a tool that has left the hand and happens to be vertical. rv05_manual_b85 holds three pads through the turn and reaches +0.83 at 0.41 N, which is real and 34° short, on a grip 25 times weaker than the reference's 10.47 N.

What this does not settle

  • The fix to the fitter is not written. It needs an extension-headroom constraint — reject a pose leaving under about 2.5 mm of the 68.11 mm chain — and a palm-height search wider than the current 8 mm clamp, since the constraint is unsatisfiable inside it. Falsified if a headroom-constrained fit on rv05_manual_stored does not reproduce the reference chain.
  • 2.74 mm is one hand's number, not a threshold. It comes from a single CEM grasp. Measure headroom against reorientation outcome across the eight deployed hands before treating it as a gate.
  • The pivot height has no per-hand value for the chain. 0.25 is the reference hand's. The 432-rollout sweep found no hand for which any of 0.15/0.25/0.35 completes, so the right value is either outside that range or conditional on the grasp being fixed first. Re-run it after the fitter changes, not before.
  • Nothing here is hardware. The bench record for these eight hands is 251 runs in docs/experiments/20260902-cb1-log-archive, all at the deployed pivot and clip. No corrected grasp has been closed on a real tool.
