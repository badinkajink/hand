Finger identity and handedness of the cad_v3 map, adversarial check (2026-11-04)

Outcome
The map survives. R_P_from_A = Rz(+90 deg) has determinant +1. z_A points from the fingers to the
robot (base plate z_A 0..3, robot mount 3..28, fingertips down to z_A -157 mm), as z_P does in the
simulator. Two independent tests put the hardware's index at gantry_v3 <1> with Finger v3 <2>
(x_A -110..0 mm), the middle at gantry_v3 <2> with Finger v3 <1>, and the thumb at gantry_v3_thumb <1>
with Finger v3 <3>. An ICP registration of the cad_v1 carriage reproduces the three yaw centres
within 0.015 mm.

Test 1: servo rotation sense against the measured aa signs (servo_predict.py, servo_sense.py)
Each finger has three 6673 mm^3 SCS0009 bodies. The output spline is the convex r = 2.0 mm cylinder
beside the r = 6.05 mm boss, and the mounting-hole line (r = 1.15 mm, 28.5 mm apart) shows which
link each body is fixed to. The aa body is vertical at yaw 17.4, -23.0 and -12.6 deg, so it is fixed to
the carriage. The MCP body lies along the yaw axis, so it is fixed to the yaw link. The PIP body is
turned by the MCP angle, so it is fixed to the MCP link. Each horn drives the next link. The aa
spline points dorsal, to -y_A on the pair and +y_A on the thumb. The MCP and PIP splines point
along -y_F. cad_v1's assembly.json gives the same layout (spline_top 30.2 mm beyond boss_top
27.0 mm along the axis that runs from the bearing wall to the servo).
servos.py commands zero + relative with no per-servo sign, and plan.JOINT_SIGN has fe1 and fe2 at
+1. A positive fe command is flexion: every bench grasp closed the hand with positive fe, and the
2026-08-29 manual sweep has the larger side positive on all six fe servos (index fe1 -50.4/+64.8,
middle fe1 -42.8/+69.7 deg). With the horn on the child, flexion fixes the servo sense at c = -1:
a positive command turns the horn clockwise seen from the horn side. The MCP and PIP servos give
c = -1 on all three fingers.
The same sense predicts the aa signs. verify_frame_mapping.py measured them as "toward +y_P (index
side)": thumb +1, index -1, middle -1.

  finger            positive aa swings the tip    claim's identity    mirrored identity
  Finger v3 <2>     -x_A                          index  -1 (=)       middle +1 (measured -1)
  Finger v3 <1>     -x_A                          middle -1 (=)       index  +1 (measured -1)
  Finger v3 <3>     +x_A                          thumb  +1 (=)       thumb  -1 (measured +1)
  agreement                                       3/3                 0/3

The test needs only the module's internal layout, the fe sign and the aa measurement. Take z
toward the base plate and x from the thumb toward the pair: the measured signs then put the index
at +y. That is the simulator's convention. In A, x_P = -y_A, so the index is at +x_A.
The claim's sentence "the aa signs ... do not discriminate" is therefore wrong. They are uniform
only when the servo's rotation sense is ignored; with it they discriminate 3/3 against 0/3.

Test 2: home corners at the motor ends (the claim's argument, rechecked)
kinematics.py homes thumb bottom-left, index top-right and middle bottom-right, with firmware x
along local y. The 11194 mm^3 motors are the thumb X motor at x_A -239..-210, the thumb Y motor
at y_A 248..276, the gantry <1> X motor at x_A 19..48, and the gantry <2> X motor at x_A -270..-241
(part 062, top level). The pair Y motors sit at y_A -36..-7. Each pair gantry keeps one 4927 mm^3
motor mount, on its outboard side. Five axes home at their motor end under either identity. The
thumb's J0 homes at its motor end only under the claim's identity. Stepper_SetInvertDir is never
called in firmware/, so all six axes run with the same direction polarity. Motor cable pinout and
screw hand are not recorded in the repository.
Correction: the base plate (002) is mirror-symmetric about x_A = -111 mm. It has thumb motor-mount
holes at both (-243.3, 230.375) and (21.3, 230.375) mm. The +x_A hole is used by the sensor plate
000. The claim says the motor sides are "the only sides with motor-mount holes in the base plate".
That is true for the pair, whose outboard motor sides are also forced by the adjacent gantry. For
the thumb, the -x_A motor side is set by the assembly and the sensor plate, and a build with the
thumb's X frame turned 180 deg would still bolt on. Test 1 does not depend on the motor sides.

Test 3: carriage registration and yaw centres (register_carriage.py, resguard --mem 3G)
The cad_v1 MJCF meshes carriage.stl and yaw_link.stl (body frames, metres, scaled to mm) were
registered onto each v3 instance. Each fit used 25000 surface samples, initial poses from the 8
principal-axis sign choices, and point-to-point ICP with a proper or forced-improper Kabsch.

  instance        carriage RMS proper / mirrored   p99 proper / mirrored   yaw link RMS proper / mirrored
  Finger v3 <1>   0.36 / 1.80-1.88 mm              0.77 / 9.45 mm          0.29 / 1.69-1.73 mm
  Finger v3 <2>   0.36 / 1.80-1.88 mm              0.77 / 9.45 mm          0.29 / 1.69-1.73 mm
  Finger v3 <3>   0.36 / 1.80-1.88 mm              0.77 / 9.45 mm          0.29 / 1.69-1.73 mm

All three fingers are proper copies of the cad_v1 part, so none is mirrored. The carriage fit maps
the F origin (the yaw centre) to these points in A:
  Finger v3 <2> (-76.721, 83.149, -75.897)   claim (-76.732, 83.141, -75.900)
  Finger v3 <1> (-153.604, 80.092, -75.896)  claim (-153.614, 80.083, -75.900)
  Finger v3 <3> (-126.690, 159.616, -75.896) claim (-126.688, 159.624, -75.900)
The fitted yaw link gives the same points within 0.02 mm. The foot of the common perpendicular from
the registered MCP axis lies 0.008 to 0.014 mm from the carriage-borne centre, and the MCP centre is
20.743 to 20.750 mm below it. x_F is (0, -0.99987, 0.0159) on the pair and (0, 0.99987, 0.0158) on
the thumb. y_F is +x_A on the pair and -x_A on the thumb. The yaw angles about x_F are 17.36 (index),
-23.06 (middle) and -12.56 deg (thumb, +12.56 about +x_P). The claim gives 17.38, -23.04 and 12.54.
