Gantry travel, box centres and heights of the SR2 hand CAD (hand v3, 2026-11-04): adversarial check

Outcome. The travel ranges, box centres and heights under test were reproduced by two other methods, and none of
them changes by more than 0.007 mm. The strokes are 64.80 mm on the index and middle X stages, 114.80 mm on the thumb
X stage and 62.58 mm on every Y stage. The home-anchored boxes are centred at thumb (-56.181, -2.400), index (56.181,
58.400) and middle (56.181, -58.400) mm in P, with the result's t = (121.4375, 110.9998, 75.9003) mm. No box centre or
size needs a correction over 0.5 mm.

Terms. A: the hand assembly frame of file "hand" (mm). P: x_P = -y_A + 121.4375, y_P = x_A + 110.9998, z_P = z_A +
75.9003 (the result's map). Displacement: translation of a stage from its pose in the CAD (mm). Home stop: the hard stop
at the motor end, where the firmware stalls and zeroes (kinematics._home_one_axis calls zero() after the stall).

Method 1, B-rep faces (parts.json, no meshes; pj.py, yaw_centres.py).
- The pair frames' X-screw bearing blocks have inner faces at x_A -94.9 and -15.1 (index) and -206.9 and -127.1
  (middle), 79.8 mm apart. The thumb frame's are at -175.9 and -46.1, 129.8 mm apart. Each beam's main body is 15.0 mm
  wide and centred on its yaw centre. The X strokes are therefore 79.8 - 15.0 = 64.8 mm and 129.8 - 15.0 = 114.8 mm.
  The X-nut flange is flush with one beam face, so the flange and the beam touch the block together.
- The Y home stop is the Y-nut flange against the beam's motor-end Y bearing block. The flange plane is tilted 0.926 deg
  with the carriage, and the contact is on the block's lower edge (z_A -51.5). This gives -47.8837 (index), -44.8259
  (middle) and +45.4943 mm (thumb).
- Yaw centres (common perpendicular of the carriage's 10.2 mm yaw bore and the yaw link's 10.2 mm MCP bore): index
  (-76.7320, 83.1407, -75.9003), middle (-153.6135, 80.0828, -75.9003), thumb (-126.6881, 159.6238, -75.9003) mm in A,
  20.750 mm from the MCP axis in all three. The yaw plane is z_A = -75.9003 mm.

Method 2, mesh casting (contact.py; travel_check.py; travel_corners.py; test_contact.py).
Vertices and points every 0.2 mm along every edge were moved 0.01 mm into their solid. A line was cast from each point
along the motion axis, against the front faces of every other solid, in both directions. On test boxes the method is
within 0.01 mm, with a +0.006 mm bias. The stops agree with the result within 0.007 mm: index X +54.137/-10.674, middle
X +19.019/-45.792, thumb X +73.094/-41.717 (thumb on the holes), index Y +14.703/-47.891, middle Y +17.761/-44.833,
thumb Y +45.501/-17.093. The Y stops are unchanged with the X stage at either X stop, and the X stops are unchanged with
the Y stage at either Y stop.

Thumb gantry offset. The thumb frame's four corner holes are at y_A 140.375 and 235.875. The base plate and robot mount
holes are at 142.875 and 238.375, and the standalone base_plate.step has the same holes, turned 180 deg about z. The
thumb X screw is at y_A 227.875, its motor and motor-mount hole at 230.375. The standalone gantry_v3_thumb.step has the
same 2.5 mm offset between motor and screw. As exported, the thumb X block meets the middle frame's rail-end tab after
+1.644 mm and the middle X block after -1.931 mm. With the thumb moved +2.5 mm in y_A (except its X motor, mount and
shaft), no thumb part meets a pair part inside any stroke. The pair frames sit exactly on their holes.

Limits that were checked and do not stop a stage. The nuts stay at least 10.6 mm (X) and 8.3 mm (Y) from their screw
ends. The rail blocks stay at least 3.35 mm from their rail ends and 4.55 mm from their own frame's tabs. With the index
Y stage at its far stop, the index carriage would reach the sensor bracket 005 at X +57.34 mm, which is 3.2 mm past the X
home stop. With both fingers at their CAD postures and the thumb X at its far stop, the thumb DIP link (part 009) meets
the index tip (part 091) after 10.0 mm of thumb Y travel toward the pair. This contact is between two fingers and depends
on their postures.

Heights (z_A, and z_P above the yaw plane): every value in the result agrees with the B-rep. They include flange face
28.0 (+103.90), robot mount underside 3.0 (+78.90), base plate underside 0.0 (+75.90), frame plate underside -4.0
(+71.90), X rail blocks -11.0 (+64.90), frame bearing blocks -22.0 (+53.90), X motors -24.0 (+51.90), the middle's X
motor -27.5 (+48.40), beam underside -28.5 (+47.40), beam Y bearing blocks -51.5 (+24.40), beam motor end and Y motors
-54.0 (+21.90), sensor bracket 005 -57.0 (+18.90) and carriage -88.693 to -10.871 (-12.79 to +65.03). The result omits one
part: under each beam, between its Y bearing blocks, the 5 mm Y lead screw spans z_A -46.8 to -41.8 (z_P +29.10 to
+34.10). It is the lowest part mounted on the beam over the finger travel. The flange axis is at (5.4375, -0.0002,
103.9003) mm in P; the adaptor's M6 holes fit a 25.000 mm radius circle centred on (-111.000, 116.000) in A.

Wording. The result names the nut alone at the middle X far stop (+19.013 mm). The beam's +x face touches the frame
block at the same displacement, because the flange is flush with it.

Runs. Both mesh runs went through resguard.sh run --mem 3G. travel_check took 64 s with a peak RSS of 0.99 GB, and
travel_corners took 82 s with 2.2 GB. A first travel_check with 2 mm buckets and 200k-point chunks grew to 1.4 GB. I
stopped it at 18:26 with a SIGTERM, which resguard logged as signal_end rc 143, and reran it with smaller chunks.
