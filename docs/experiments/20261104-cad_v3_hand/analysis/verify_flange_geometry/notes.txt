Check of the UR5-flange-to-hand transform (cad_v3), geometry lens, 2026-11-04

Outcome. The four T_E_from_A matrices are confirmed. My matrices, built from the STEP text with my own reader,
differ from the claimed ones by at most 4.9e-7 per element, which is the claim's 6-decimal rounding. Each claimed
matrix puts the adaptor's four M6 holes within 1.1e-4 mm of the flange's bolt positions at 45, 135, 225 and 315 deg.
Two statements around the matrices are wrong or not reproducible (items 1 and 2 below).

Method. stepread.py is a small ISO 10303-21 reader written for this check. It uses no FreeCAD and none of the other
agent's scripts. It composes each instance as A(item_2) A(item_1)^-1 from ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION. It reports
cylinders, planes and face vertices in mm (the files are in metres). verify.py, t_standalone.py and t_extra.py
run the checks. verify.json and standalone.json list the numbers. Peak RSS was 123 MB, run time about 1 s, and no
mesh was loaded. The hand STEP has 31,840 cartesian points.

Measured, all in frame A (mm) unless stated:
- Adaptor = hand solid 4, robot mount = solid 1. The solid order is the same as in parts.json.
- T_A_from_D, from the STEP text: R = [[-0.406737,0,-0.913545],[0.913545,0,-0.406737],[0,-1,0]] and
  t = (-111.000003, 115.999998, 6.000000). This is 134.6979 deg about (-0.4173, -0.6426, 0.6426). It agrees with
  FreeCAD's placement to 1.4e-14 deg and 0 mm.
- The adaptor solid inside the hand file equals UR5 Adaptor.step face for face, in the same local frame.
  robot_mount.step and base_plate.step also equal their solids in the hand file. For both,
  T_A_from_file = Rz(180 deg) with t = (-111, 142.875, 0).
- The four 6.4 mm M6 axes are parallel to z_A to 0 deg. Fitted at z = 28, they lie on a 50.000000 mm circle
  (rms 1.9e-13). The centre is (-111.000003, 115.999998), 3.4e-6 mm from the claimed (-111, 116). The holes lie at
  -111 (= 249), -21, 69 and 159 deg.
- The 11 mm counterbores span z 6..13 and the head seats are at z 13, so the screw heads are on the z = 6 side.
  The 6.4 mm bores span z 14.03..28. The adaptor face toward the flange is therefore z = 28.000000, and the tool
  direction z_E is -z_A.
- The flat sides face 24, 114, 204 and 294 deg, at a half width of 30.2 mm.
- The 4.4 mm holes are at (-105.5, 100), (-133, 117), (-116.5, 100) and (-89, 117). They pass through the whole
  adaptor (z 6..28) and lie 0.000 mm from the robot mount's 4.4 mm openings (z 5.8..6.0, below 8.5 mm
  counterbores at z 3..5.8).
- Robot mount pocket: four walls with inward normals at 24, 114, 204 and 294 deg, each 30.500 +- 3e-6 mm from the
  M6 centre. Four R20 corners lie 14.849 mm from the centre, and the floor is at z = 6. The floor has no openings
  other than the four 4.4 mm holes.
- No part of the hand rises above z = 28. The robot mount and the adaptor reach z = 28. The lowest point is
  z = -157.511, the bottom of a 3 mm sphere on solid 12 in Finger v3 <3>, which is 185.511 mm below the flange
  face. The volume centroid of the hand is at z_E = +38.4 mm.

Mate uniqueness. Of the 24 orderings of the four points, only the identity maps the trapezoid onto itself by a
rotation. The next best has 7.778 mm rms. The one reflection that maps it onto itself (0 mm) has its mirror line
along y_A, through x = -111. I tested the eight placements of the rounded square in its pocket: four 90-degree
turns, each with and without turning the adaptor over. Only the as-designed placement lines up the 4.4 mm holes,
4 of 4. Every other placement leaves each hole 1.2 to 29.7 mm from the nearest robot-mount hole.

Turning the adaptor over about the trapezoid's mirror line would turn its flats to 66, 156, 246 and 336 deg,
42 deg off the pocket walls. Turned that far, the 60.4 mm R20 square reaches 34.405 mm from the centre, against
the pocket's 30.5 mm, so it does not enter the pocket. The result rules out the flip for a weaker reason (the
counterbores would face the flange).

The result omits four radial holes in the robot mount. Each is 6.8 mm in diameter and 55 mm long from the pocket
wall at z = 17, opening into a 9.8 mm counterbore toward the outside. They are coaxial with the adaptor's four
8.6 mm side holes (10 mm deep, chamfered entrance, at mid-thickness on each flat). The pattern repeats at every
90 deg. With radial fasteners alone, the adaptor would therefore fasten in any of four turns in its pocket.

The pocket is centred on the M6 circle to 3e-6 mm. Such a turn therefore composes to one of the same four
T_E_from_A and only changes which k matches a given connector direction. The set of four transforms is complete.

Transforms, with the convention as stated (flange M6 at 45 + 90 j deg, z_E out of the flange):
- Every claimed matrix has det 0.99999945 and orthogonality error 5.5e-7. The nearest rotation is 2.5e-7 away,
  which moves a point 300 mm out by 7e-5 mm.
- Each claimed matrix maps c_A to the origin of E. The adaptor's flange face is at z_E = 0, its other face at
  z_E = 22 and the head seats at z_E = 15.
- The adaptor's flats face 0, 90, 180 and 270 deg in E in all four clockings. In every clocking, one flat side
  faces the tool connector squarely, which can be checked by eye.
- The claimed quaternions match the claimed matrices to 3e-5 deg. The claimed positions in metres match t to
  4e-5 mm.
- The hand occupies z_E 0..185.511 in every clocking, on the tool side.
- No adaptor hole lies on the flange's 6 mm pin hole in any clocking. The nearest is 8.3 mm away, so no dowel can
  be added through an existing hole.
- Thumb, index and middle gantry centres lie at 90, -45 and -135 deg from the flange axis in A (exact). The
  directions in E for each k and the connector offsets (24, 21, 21; 66 and 69 and 159 deg) are reproduced.

Discrepancies:
1. k_rule says "x_E is 90 deg counterclockwise from y_E seen from the tool side". In a right-handed frame seen
   with z_E toward the viewer, x_E is 90 deg CLOCKWISE from y_E. The sentence is true only when the flange is seen
   from the robot side, looking along +z_E. Read as written, it puts x_E 180 deg wrong at mount time. The
   matrices and the by-eye descriptions in k_descriptions do not use this sentence and are correct.
2. standalone_face_check_max_mm.base_plate_translation_only = 247.64 mm could not be reproduced. The metric that
   reproduces the robot mount's 315.53 mm gives 399.73 mm for the base plate. That metric is the largest
   displacement of a parts.json plane centre when the centres of mass are aligned by translation. Cylinder points
   give 389.98 mm and vertices 412.30 mm. The conclusion, that a translation alone is wrong by hundreds of mm, is
   unaffected.
3. Not over the threshold: the bolt-circle centre is 3.4e-6 mm from (-111, 116).

Not checked here: the flange convention itself (M6 at 45 deg, connector on -y_E, the URDF wrist3 mesh), which
another task in this workflow covers. The index and middle labels follow the result's handedness argument
(x_P = -y_A, y_P = x_A, z_P = z_A), which is sound for right-handed frames. No physical label in the CAD confirms
them.
