Code map, grasp synthesis, evaluation protocol and simulator backends

The package src/morphohand/ contains the simulation, grasp, learning and bench code. The studies and page builders that call it are in scripts/. A real_v1 hand goes through five stages. It starts as a Sobol sample of six gantry coordinates and becomes a frozen MuJoCo scene. A grasp comes from the geometric fitter. A residual PPO policy is trained in MuJoCo-Warp through mjlab. An evaluation over 64 rollouts stores its per-step traces. A bench plan then passes a 5 mm clearance gate. MuJoCo on the CPU runs grasps, chains and plans; Newton and Drake run the contact comparisons in separate environments.

Terms

Morphology run
A directory under results/phase1/ with frozen_scene.xml, summary.json and best_rollout.npz; trainers and evaluators read a hand from it.
Frozen scene
A scene with the hand’s layout baked into fixed geometry, without the nine morphology joints.
open_ik
The keyframe of the open fingers before the grasp: the CEM seed and the RL reset pose.
Grasp fitter
fit_real_v1_pose.fit: the palm pose and finger angles that put the pads on the tool.
CEM
Cross-entropy method over the nine finger controls (phase1_optimize_grasp.py).
Held lift
Tool height after the hold minus its height before the lift, in m.
Residual policy
A PPO actor. Its output is scaled by 0.5 rad and clipped, and it is added to the scripted finger set-points from control step 58.
Recipe
configs/recipes/*.yaml holds the trainer defaults of one policy type. Command-line flags override them.
Held, signed cosine
Held: more than 0.5 N of pad force with the tool centre above 0.06 m at the last step. Signed cosine: of the tool axis with world +z, +1 upright.
Traces
Per-step arrays of one evaluation, stored in traces/<run>.npz beside the page that plots them.

Code map

The directories of src/morphohand/ divide the code as follows.

  • sampling/: the morphology dataclasses, REAL_V1_WORKSPACE and freeze_scene_for_eval.
  • tools/: the scene generator and keyframe IK.
  • optimization/: the grasp evaluator and CEM.
  • rl/: the mjlab environment terms, the recipes, the deploy helpers and the trajectory-health scorecard.
  • bench/, geometry/ and driver/: the bench replay, the geometry and the servo firmware with the manta_hand host.

scripts/README.md maps the active scripts, and superseded ones are in scripts/archive/. assets/mjcf/real_v1/real_hand.xml is the hardware model; assets/mjcf/ has one folder per hand topology plus generated/ and dated experimental/ folders. Runs go to results/, which git ignores; pages go to docs/.

Path of one hand from a sample to a bench plan

  1. Sample: real_v1_design_search.sobol_designs draws a prefix-stable Sobol population over the six gantry slides (thumb x ±30 mm and y ±55 mm, index and middle ±30 mm, link lengths fixed). real_v1_sobol8192.sh runs 8,192 layouts.
  2. Scene: generate_morphology_xml.py --base-hand-xml assets/mjcf/real_v1/real_hand.xml writes a rigid scene without the morphology joints.
  3. Grasp: the grasp fitter, called through probe_real_v1_carry._grip_from_fit, plus a commanded squeeze.
  4. Screen: real_v1_design_search.py scores the open-loop carry, and real_v1_promote_designs.py checks export, servo range, gantry travel and clearance.
  5. Policy: make_cal_morphology_run.py --hand <tag> --squeeze <mm> writes a morphology run on the calibrated plant. rl_train_cube.py --recipe b_liveA --morphology-run <dir> trains the policy through resguard.sh run. hands_tranche_queue.py runs a queue of such jobs.
  6. Evaluation: policy_eval_suite.py, then rl_render_rollout.py and policy_filmstrip.py for a film and its frames.
  7. Bench plan: real_v1_export_plan.py --design <id> writes <id>_plan.json, _traj.csv (50 Hz, degrees), _build.txt and _poses.txt; real_v1_trajectory_clearance.py --plan <id> checks the chord and the CSV against 5 mm. Plans are copied to the bench computer by hand.

Grasp synthesis

The eight deployed hands hold the tool with the fitter’s pose plus a 10 mm squeeze (real_v1_export_plan.py --squeeze-mm), without CEM. fit_real_v1_pose.py places the pad targets on the shaft’s equator, at the shaft radius plus the 10.55 mm pad and a 1 mm gap. It then searches palm heights from −30 to +60 mm in 2.5 mm steps and takes the deepest one at which every tip comes within 4 mm, with the joints 0.05 rad inside their limits. It keeps the pose if a hold probe leaves the tool at least 20 mm up. It writes open_ik and, with --also-open, an open pose 8 mm back.

The CEM lane, real_v1_pipeline.py --stages grasp, runs phase1_optimize_grasp.py --keyframe open_ik. It runs 24 iterations of 40 candidates over the yaw, MCP and PIP controls of the three fingers. Each candidate closes for 240 steps, lifts the palm 50 mm and holds for 140 steps. The score’s lift term rewards the peak height, which a lift followed by a drop also reaches. The pipeline therefore passes a design on a held lift of 0.03 m or more. Contact-target patches, a force-closure energy and synergy (eigengrasp) CEM remain in the code with zero weight, and no current pipeline enables them. retarget_keyframe_ik.py retargets the keyframes of the earlier nine-parameter hands, which are kept as historical controls.

Evaluation protocol

Every grasp and policy evaluation runs on a frozen scene. freeze_scene_for_eval bakes the keyframe’s layout into fixed geometry, because the morphology joints drift during a rollout. It takes the real_v1 base scene from 31 to 22 position coordinates. policy_eval_suite.py steps 64 rollouts in parallel under the run’s own timing and tool contact class, both read from the run’s config.yaml. It reports the align rate (signed cosine of at least 0.9) and the hold rate. A single deterministic rollout on the GPU does not repeat, since contact solves reduce in a varying order, so every claim rests on the 64. Each evaluation stores its traces, --check recomputes the summary from them, and policy_reeval_traces.py re-runs a set of evaluations and compares them with the originals.

The trajectory-health scorecard (trajectory_health.characterize_trajectory) grades late and idle fingers, a drop, jitter, de-centring and over-clamp. policy_filmstrip.py tiles phase-aligned frames of a rollout. The frames are looked at before the rollout’s numbers are explained.

Simulator backends

MuJoCo 3.6.0 on the CPU, pinned in pyproject.toml, runs the CEM evaluator, the grasp fitter, the open-loop carry, the UR5e chain, the plan export and the clearance gate. MuJoCo-Warp through mjlab 1.2.0 (uv run --extra rl --extra gpu) runs PPO training, policy evaluation and the batched contact benchmarks. Newton (hydroelastic contact under its MuJoCo solver) and Drake (SAP hydroelastic contact on the CPU) run in their own environments for the comparisons of the fingertip contact overview. Drake also runs the SR² planning prototype (drake_sr2_hand.py). MJX runs on the CPU in the project environment and is used by two non-default CEM optimizers and the DiffMJX scripts of 2026-10-07, whose runbook is the README of docs/experiments/20261007-diffmjx/.

Future work

  1. Give rl_render_rollout.py and policy_transfer_probe.py the run’s tool contact class, as policy_eval_suite.py --object-contact run has it. Today they render and probe a policy trained before 2026-09-20 on the scene’s class, where 11 of 16 such policies drop the tool at the trained pose. A render of D7 that holds the tool through step 249 shows the change.
  2. Store traces from the UR5e chain probe (real_v1_chain_policy.py) and the CPU transfer probe in the format of traces/<run>.npz, so their figures can be redrawn without a rerun.
  3. Run each unused grasp objective (contact targets, force closure, synergy CEM) and morphohand.backends once in a test on the real_v1 base scene. An objective that fails the test moves to scripts/archive/ with its code.
