Data paths, rates and clocks of the SR2 hand bench

The SR2 hand bench runs on three computers joined by HTTP, UDP over Tailscale, USB, RTDE and Ethernet. Of the 14 failures recorded from 2026-11-15 to 11-17, 8 sat in the devices, the power or the sensing, and 6 in the plumbing between processes. Figure 1 maps every stream with its rate, its age when used and its clock; Table 2 places each failure on the map. The owner asked on 2026-11-17 whether the bench should move to ROS, after repeated trouble with servo telemetry, tag detection and fingertip streaming. The last section sorts the failures by what a middleware changes.

Terms

  • Rate: messages a second on a link.
  • Age when used: the time from a measurement to the control loop's use of it.
  • Clock: the computer whose time stamps a stream's samples.
  • Read-back: after the station writes a frame of nine goals, it reads the nine positions and returns them with its answer.
  • Lease: the time the station waits for the next frame before it ends a stream and keeps the last goals.
  • Plumbing: the processes, threads and links between the devices, as opposed to the devices, their power and the sensing itself.

Failures and a middleware

Failures 1, 2, 4, 9, 11, 14 sat in the plumbing. Two came from one Python process doing six jobs at once (1 and 9): a job that blocks the interpreter stalls every other job in it. Three came from the HTTP link to the station (2, 4 and 14), and one from UDP replies that never arrived (11). ROS 2 runs each node as a separate process and stamps every message with its time. Several nodes can read one topic, and one bag records all topics. Separate processes share no interpreter (1, 9), and a topic monitor shows a stream that stops (11). Leases and timeouts (2, 4, 14) are settings of the transport under ROS 2 as well.

Failures 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12, 13 sat in the devices, the power and the sensing. The servo bus reads nine positions in about 9 ms whatever runs above it (3, 5). The potentiometers leave their track near their ends (8, 10). The supply sagged under the grip's current (6, 12), and the tags were seen twice or too few times (7, 13). A middleware changes none of these.

The bench is moving toward the structure ROS has without adopting ROS: since 2026-11-17 the fingertip estimator runs in a process of its own. The next steps on that path are the tracker in its own process, one publish-and-subscribe transport with time stamps for every stream, a recorder of all streams and a time service on all three computers. Each has a counterpart in ROS 2: nodes, topics, bags and a shared clock.
