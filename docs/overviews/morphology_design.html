Finger-layout search for the SR² hand and the transfer of its ranking

The program sampled 8,198 finger layouts with a Sobol sequence, screened them in simulation for a grasp that keeps the tool through a perturbed open-loop turn (227 passed), and built eight. On the bench the eight held the tool in 48 of 77 trials, and simulated and measured alignment rank them at (); they lie close together, a median 43.6 mm apart. Where the thumb grasps the tool predicts whether a hand turns it; the mount layout does not. For a cylinder or sphere the best layout puts the mounts 35–37 mm outside its surface.

Terms

Layout
The six gantry coordinates of the three finger bases (the hand’s morphology).
Sobol sample
A low-discrepancy sequence over the six-dimensional box of gantry travel.
Retention screen
A simulated grasp, 60 mm lift and 5 s hold with no more than 10 mm of slip, repeated under perturbations.
Alignment
Cosine of the tool axis with vertical at the end of the turn; +1 is upright.
Spearman rank correlation.
AUC
Area under the curve of a score used as a classifier of an outcome; 0.5 is chance, 1 perfect.
Thumb moment arm
Moment arm of the thumb’s contact about the pinch axis of the other two fingers (tau_thumb).
KaRMA
A kinematic score of the object poses a hand can reach (KaRMA-T: translations, KaRMA-R: rotations).

Search funnel and bench ranking

The population covers the six gantry coordinates; all 8,198 layouts fit the gantry travel. Half fail the grasp fit, 535 pass the retention screen, 227 are confirmed under perturbations, 19 were exported as plans and eight built as D1–D8, numbered by simulated rank. On the bench the eight held the tool in 48 of 77 scored trials, and the simulated and measured alignment rank them at (, bootstrap interval −0.29 to 1.00) (program record). The eight are concentrated: their median pairwise separation is 43.6 mm against 66.9 mm for eight layouts drawn at random, and seven of them sit in the compact quadrant.

A design is evaluated in a frozen scene (freeze_scene_for_eval): its mounts become fixed body transforms and the morphology joints, which drift under contact, are removed. The funnel and the deployed plans take their grasp from a geometric fitter (fit_real_v1_pose.py) that solves for a palm height and nine finger angles with three pads at or below the shaft’s equator. The older CEM search (phase1_optimize_grasp.py) optimises the hold, which on real_v1 trades against the turn: rv04_mid with the thumb offset turned the tool to +0.997 from its fitted grasp and to +0.625 after CEM (design search).

What predicts a turning hand

Among 108 designs searched with a fixed-contact turn, 49 reorient the tool, all by a two-pad pinch-roll that sheds the descending pad. The grasp decides more than the layout: sliding the thumb 20 mm along the shaft takes rv04_mid from 0 of 8 to 8 of 8, and the thumb’s moment arm about the pinch axis separates turning from non-turning designs at , AUC 0.821, over 80 graspable designs. Above 55 mm of thumb-to-pair separation the mount coordinates stop ordering the designs ( −0.015 and −0.092) (design search).

Kinematic scores predict which designs keep the tool and barely which turn it. KaRMA-T separates retained from discarded designs at AUC 0.802 but correlates with the screened turn at only (kinematic rolling-pinch metric), and an earlier dexterity score ranked the grip and missed the turn.

Finger spacing against object size

On a family of symmetric tripods with the bench servo model and 1 mm pad fingertips, the layout with the largest fixed-contact workspace grows one for one with the object: the mounts sit 37.2 mm outside a sphere’s surface and 34.7 mm outside a cylinder’s, about half the 68.1 mm finger. The task moves the optimum: a cylinder is held most robustly 25.4 mm out and turned furthest 31.3 mm out, and for spheres robustness peaks at the compact edge of the feasible band for 8 of 10 objects and the turn at the wide edge for 10 of 10. A bench protocol of 47 cells (17.3 h) is written and has not been run (finger spacing and object size). A size-7 basketball needs the widest layout, friction of at least 1.0 and MuJoCo’s elliptic friction cone to be held (basketball grasp).

Earlier design studies in simulation

Before the hand was built, designs had nine parameters: the x and y of each finger base and its proximal length. morph_pipeline_sweep.py scored a design by baking it into fixed geometry, retargeting the grasp keyframe by inverse kinematics, searching the grasp by CEM, training a lift policy from scratch and a turn policy on the lift’s deliveries, and grading the hand-off with the trajectory-health scorecard. The policy draw dominated: the held cosine of one design varied with a standard deviation of 0.38 between from-scratch seeds; a fixed lift policy, a shared warm start and fingertip imitation in the object frame cut it to 0.02, and one design win (H06_04, 0.748 over four draws) replicated (program record). Of two finger arrangements, the opposed pair turned the tool to 0.995 and dropped it in every rollout, while the inline pair held at 0.884 (two finger arrangements).

Future work

  1. Score a fresh Sobol sample by the thumb’s moment arm and run the open-loop turn on it; the claim fails if the AUC on new designs falls toward 0.5.
  2. Bench the within-family contrast already on the control station (g12 at four clips, u0060 and u0100 at two), twenty trials in one sitting. If the bench does not reproduce an ordering within one family, an ordering across families means little.
  3. Choose the next hands to build where the retention screen and the bench-schedule replay disagree most, and keep the eight built hands as the held-out set.
  4. Train one policy over randomised mounts that observes the six mount coordinates, and test first whether its behaviour changes with the design vector alone; if it does not, the policy ignores the design and cannot rank it.
  5. Run the 47-cell bench protocol of the object-size study (17.3 h; core set of 8 objects in 7.1 h) after printing the objects and exporting the plans with hand_object_scale_export.py.
