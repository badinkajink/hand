Stiffness, printability and magnetic force sensing of the printed TPU fingertips

The SR² fingertip is a 17 by 14.8 by 22 mm block of 95A TPU printed with two walls and gyroid infill. Finite elements on the tip’s own G-code give an approach of 63 µm under 1 N at 20 % infill; eFlesh lattices of 8 mm cells sink 381–613 µm, and 1 mm sphere pads at one modulus miss the modelled approach by 20–28 % over 0.5–3 N. A 4.76 by 1.59 mm magnet 5 mm under the face, read by ReSkin’s five-chip MLX90393 board, resolves 0.044 N per sample at a known contact point; with the contact point unknown, four magnets hold 0.14–0.16 N over the face. No tip or board has been measured yet.

Terms

Approach
Travel of the 12.5 mm screwdriver shaft into the tip from first touch under a given normal force, in µm.
Contact modulus
, the modulus of the pads’ pressure law that best reproduces the finite-element approach at 0.5, 1 and 3 N, in MPa; the RL scenes use 10 MPa.
Twist arm
Torsional friction torque over the friction coefficient times the normal force, in mm: the radius at which the contact patch resists spin.
Gyroid infill
PrusaSlicer’s triply periodic infill; the percentage is the volume fraction of the core.
eFlesh
An open-source printed sensor: cells of a microstructure pattern cut to the part’s shape, with magnets in printed pouches over a magnetometer board.
Five-chip board
ReSkin’s 5X board: five Melexis MLX90393 three-axis magnetometers, U1 under the contact point and U2–U5 7 mm from it in a plus.
Force noise
Standard deviation of the normal force estimated from one 141 Hz sample of the five chips, computed from finite-element magnet travel, a dipole field and the datasheet’s chip noise, in N.
GAIN_SEL
The MLX90393’s gain register; at 7, the most sensitive setting, its guaranteed range is 4.8 mT.

Stiffness of the printed gyroid tip

The finite-element models resolve the tip’s two walls, its four top and bottom layers and the gyroid beads as voxels of 0.1 mm, pressed by the 12.5 mm shaft. On those models the pads’ contact modulus is 13.0 MPa at 20 % infill, 6.6 MPa at 10 % and 3.4 MPa at 5 %. One modulus misses the approach by 20–28 % over 0.5–3 N, because the tip’s force grows as the approach to the power 1.07 and the pads’ to the power 1.5; the walls carry the edges of the face, and the pads’ twist arm falls 7–23 % short of the finite elements’ at 3 N, depending on the print (finite elements of the printed tip).

The tip’s shape was screened on the hand’s tasks: of 24 printable side and end fillets, the three finalists turn the tool no further than the as-built tip on fresh placements, and the best, a 6 mm end fillet, gives 11.7 µm/N of magnet travel at 95A and 20 % gyroid, short of the 12 µm/N the sensor needs. On every shape the pads’ sliding torque grows with spin rate, 1.25 times the law at 1 rad/s on the printed 2.7 mm fillet (fingertip co-design). Capsule, half-ellipsoid and quarter-ellipsoid tips in the same envelope do no better on fresh placements, and the quarter-ellipsoids with the board on their flat back move the magnet 0.6 µm/N and put 214 mT on the nearest chip. Their finite elements, and those of the block, give a nearly uniform surface, 136–170 µm at 1 N under a 2 mm sphere, because the solid walls and skins spread the load over the gyroid core; neither Drake’s field nor the pads reproduce it (curved fingertips).

Solved from its own G-code, the 20 % tip with its magnet is softer than these models: 63 µm at 1 N against 41 µm, a contact modulus of 7.3 MPa against 14.6 MPa, because the stacked beads overlap only partly (eFlesh printability). A magnet 5 mm under the face stiffens the tip by 15 %, one 3 mm under it by 31 % and one 1.5 mm under it by 99 %.

Printability and stiffness of eFlesh lattices

eFlesh’s cut-cell tool crashes in OpenVDB on every input, and its notebook’s fallback tiles whole cells, which gives only boxes. scripts/eflesh_build.py builds a lattice of any shape, with the pouches and the pause, in about 9 s by stitching cells and cutting them with mesh booleans. In a 24 mm cube at the modulus parameter of the owner’s print, 3, 28 and 71 % of the islands of a layer are less than half supported at 8, 6 and 4 mm cells on a 0.4 mm nozzle, where the owner’s prints delaminated; the gyroid tip prints each layer as one island (Figure 2).

From their G-code, an eFlesh tip of 8 mm cells sinks 613 µm at 1 N when open on every face, as eFlesh prints it, 381 µm with a 0.9 mm palmar skin and 81 µm with a skin on every face (Figure 3). At 3 N the open lattice sinks about 1.5 mm, deeper than 1 mm sphere pads represent. The skin closes the open cells that catch on a tool and costs sensitivity: the magnet travels 250 µm/N in the open tip and 26 µm/N with a skin on every face (eFlesh printability).

Embedded magnets and the five-chip readout

With the chips against the tip’s back face and the Earth’s field fitted as an unknown, one magnet 5 mm under the face gives 0.044 N of force noise per sample on the gyroid tip (0.060 N from U1 alone) and 0.005 N on the open eFlesh tip. The owner’s v17 mount sets the board under 2.5 mm of solid TPU and doubles the noise to 0.090 N (Figures 5 and 6). The disturbances are larger than the noise: a 10 °C change drifts the gyroid tip’s estimate by 0.47 N, and the facing finger’s magnet moving from 25 to 30 mm shifts it by 5.2 N, which a fitted field gradient reduces to 2.0 N at 0.079 N of noise; the hand’s kinematics give that magnet’s position, so its field can be subtracted (magnetometer readout).

With the contact point unknown, one magnet gives 0.08–1.48 N over 15 contact points on the face. Four magnets of the same size, 6 mm apart across the face and 7 mm along it, give 0.14–0.16 N everywhere, stiffen the face by 12 % and raise the field at the chips to 10.6 mT, which needs GAIN_SEL 3 (magnetometer readout).

On the SR² finger as built from its CAD parts the DIP link’s 3.0 mm plate covers the tip’s back, so the board sits on the plate’s far side, its chips 3.42 mm from the tip. A tip printed in Siraya Tech 85A at 30 % gyroid with four 3 by 2 mm magnets then resolves 0.35 N per sample at the contact point, with the contact position and a uniform background field unknown, and 0.14 N with a fifth magnet over U1 (deployment readiness).

Print plan and magnet pockets

The printed tip is whole, without the insert’s mounting slots; the owner designs the attachment separately. Each pocket is 90 % of the magnet’s diameter, for a press fit, and one layer deeper than the magnet, so the nozzle never prints onto it. At 0.2 mm layers the 4.76 mm magnet 5 mm under the face sits in nine layers from 10.40 to 12.20 mm of print height under 0.21 mm of air, and the print pauses before layer 62. The tip page holds the STL and pause sheet of each print and a calculator for magnet depth, chip distance, TPU modulus, magnet grade and output rate (finite elements of the printed tip). Fourteen PrusaSlicer projects print the 3 by 2 mm magnet tips and two seat coupons in Siraya Tech 85A on the Prusa XL’s second extruder, at 30 and 50 % gyroid, with seats of 2.7 mm until a coupon shows which seat keeps a magnet (deployment readiness).

Compliance of the tip and of the servo

At 1 N the gyroid tip sinks 63 µm and the eFlesh tips 0.38–0.61 mm. The servo plant used in training (gain 0.72 N·m/rad, inferred from the load register and never measured) lets the fingertip yield 3.4–4.7 mm per newton toward the tool, so on that plant the servos set the finger’s compliance and the tip adds less than a fifth of it. The servo identification protocol measures the gain on a pendulum and with finger presses on a load cell before the tip’s deformation is modelled further (servo identification protocol).

Future work

  1. Press a printed 95A 20 % tip with the 12.5 mm shaft on a load cell at 0.5, 1 and 3 N and fit it with scripts/tpu_tip_calibrate.py; an approach outside 50–76 µm at 1 N contradicts the as-printed finite elements (63 µm).
  2. Log the five chips at GAIN_SEL 7 for 10 min with the tip unloaded, then over a 10 °C change; a per-sample force noise above 0.066 N (1.5 times 0.044) or a drift above 0.7 N per 10 °C contradicts scripts/mlx90393_model.py.
  3. Fit sphere pads with a lumped normal spring to the eFlesh tip’s approach and twist arm in docs/experiments/20261013-eflesh_printability/fem.jsonl; if no spring brings both within 15 % over 0.5–3 N, eFlesh needs a distributed model.
  4. Rerun the magnet-depth finite elements with the grid refined to 0.3 mm from −2 to 7 mm in depth (scripts/tpu_tip_magnet_layouts.py), to test the noise trend of 0.137, 0.121 and 0.098 N at 1.5, 3 and 5 mm.
  5. Print the four-magnet tip print/20261013-sr2_tip_four_d4p76x1p59_depth5.stl and check that the recessed pockets hold the magnets through the rest of the print without glue.
