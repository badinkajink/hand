Calibration of Drake's field and the pads to the finite elements

Calibration to the FE reproduces stiffness and twist arm on the 11/22 half-ellipsoid, and the two simulators still part on its turns; none of the four prints meets every criterion of the plan (docs/experiments/20261018-fe_contact_calibration/plan.json: approach within 25 % RMS and 40 % at worst, twist arm 0.70-1.15 times the FE's, and pads and Drake agreeing on the same iCEM commands: 5 of 6 held outcomes, a same-branch RMS angle within 3 deg or the pads' seed spread, forces within 0.25 N normal and 0.35 N friction).

Approach: the tip's travel toward the palm under a probe, from the FE of the 85A prints at 30 % gyroid with four 3 x 2 mm magnets, under rods of 5, 8 and 12.5 mm radius at 0.5, 1 and 3 N. Twist arm: the pressure-weighted patch radius that sets the torque resisting a turn. M1: one modulus fitted to the approach; M2: one modulus fitted to the twist arm; M3: M2's modulus behind a series spring. One modulus leaves 21-24 % RMS and 41-61 % worst approach error in Drake, because Drake's pressure depends only on the local overlap: its approach grows with the 0.50-0.67 power of the force, the printed shell over gyroid's with the 0.71-0.93 power.

Pads and Drake on the 11/22 half-ellipsoid at M2 (the plan runs trajectories at M2 in place of M3): both hold the tool in 6 of 6 replays of the readiness iCEM commands. On the 16 mm cell the tool axes end 2-9 deg apart (3 pairs on the same branch, median RMS angle 6.1 deg against a 5.0 deg bound) with the normal forces 0.41 N RMS apart; on the 25 mm cell they end 27-57 deg apart. On a plan made for that tip the axes end 26 deg apart in all three pairs. On the bed the pads creep 0.29-0.97 deg/s where Drake creeps 0.004-0.024 deg/s; at impratio 1000 the pads put 4 of 6 pairs on Drake's branch (3 at impratio 100) and consistency still fails. The spring check fails (3.1 deg and 0.22 N against 1 deg and 0.05 N), so M2 cannot stand in for the spring on trajectories. Drake replays took 52-178 s each against the 15 s planned, so the block's and the capsule's consistency runs were cut; they lead the page's Future work with the pads' creep against Drake's and the bench indentation.

DIP-link redesign for the magnetometer board

Two designs are complete and a third nearly so, each with STEP and STL files, a clearance check over PIP -18 to 92 deg and a sensing estimate. Concept a keeps the tip's outer surface: the DIP link's plate becomes a 0.85 mm window plate whose windows let the five MLX90393 chips stand 0.05 mm proud against the tip's back face, and a 3 mm dorsal clamp plate sandwiches the board with two M1.6 countersunk screws, two M2 screws and two locating pins. Concept b replaces the tip's dorsal 6 mm with a rigid cassette: four M1.6 screws and brass nuts clamp the board between a 0.9 mm PLA cover and a 3.6 mm tongue of the DIP link, and an 11 mm 85A pad snaps onto two dovetail rails; the pad keeps the palmar and side surfaces, halves the magnet travel per newton and raises the tip's modulus by 20 %, and the closer chips need GAIN_SEL 0-1. Concept c places the board at a print pause and prints a 0.2 mm PLA cap and the TPU tip over it, so the board is captured by the print with no screw preload; the cartridge (carrier, board, cap, tip) slides onto a separate PLA body and one brass M2 screw holds it. Its evaluation was regenerated from the final clearance check (worst rigid part 0.55 mm from the PIP servo at -13.5 deg) and its renders redrawn (personal job, step 3).

The specification (docs/experiments/20261018-dip_link_board_mount/spec.json) sets two hard constraints. The board's JST SH connector clashes with the MCP link by 1.45 mm at this placement, so the tab is cut to 4.9 mm or less and the cable soldered. One micrometre of board motion toward the magnets reads as 0.16 N of normal force with the chips on the back face, so the clamp must remove play below a micrometre. Both designs leave the 20 mm board 2.6 mm past each side of the 14.8 mm tip (rigid rim 3.1 mm), where the readiness study's board edge touched the cylinder in most HOM runs; a narrower board is the remedy the designs cannot provide.

Next, in the personal job: concept c's checks (step 3), the judge and the final design with a dimensioned drawing (step 4), the DIP page (step 5). The owner chooses the design after the judge's recommendation.
