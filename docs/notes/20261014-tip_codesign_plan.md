## Critic's verdict

The design will not run as written. It overruns in three places, so I would restructure it rather than patch it. First, the code is the critical path. Steps 3-5 put a patch sampler with curvatures, hull/STL/pocket, as_printed, FE hooks, two law modules, builder v2, a replace_tips dispatcher, bed rig subclasses, a spin driver, BedFilm, a scale wrapper and three test files into 0:20-2:15. Meanwhile the deterioration sweep on flat tips needs none of that code: tpu_tip_rig already builds any tpu<r> (tpu_models(radii=...), scripts/tpu_tip_rig.py:302) and block_law2.py already takes r. Second, the crown branch cannot be built. Face-up 0.2 mm layers flatten every crown into a top terrace 2·sqrt(0.2R) mm wide (2.9 mm at R 10.55, 4.9 mm at R 30). eflesh_build.write_3mf (scripts/eflesh_build.py:153-186) writes only the custom-gcode file and no layer-height ranges, so the 0.1 mm rescue route does not exist. The terraced-pad fallback also has no Drake reference, and about 3/4 of the Sobol points would land in that branch. Third, step 12 runs FE (3.7-5 GB, 4 cores) beside six single-core jobs and a 6-thread, about 3 GB iCEM, all inside cores 4-11 and 6 GB.

The references are computable as described. The Drake field E(1−gauge) about the hull's volume centroid can be integrated for any convex hull. The Winkler and local laws need only surface samples. The scratch brake_law.py reruns in under 1 s and reproduces the stated N45 values (0.373/0.364/0.393 N on r 2.7, 0.806/0.796/0.792 N on r 6, 0.243/0.243/0.222 N on r 0). Two parts of the statistics are flawed: the finalist interval pools the stage-2 placements that were used for selection, and J1 takes the best of about 33 grasps per cell. The τ step has two friction levels, and the four bistable placements (D3 seeds 0-2, D8 seed 1; 4 of 64 replays, 6 %) cross its 5 % threshold on their own.

Printing also matters more than the plan allows. Face up at 0.2 mm, the printed palmar contact is a flat terrace whose first step (0.2 mm) is deeper than the 3 N approach (113 µm). The fillet radius therefore reaches the contact only through the terrace width, sqrt(0.2r − 0.01) mm per side (0.77 mm at r 3.0). The edge is close to the sharp-box regime where the pads deteriorate most (spin ratio 1.71 on r 0, edge set back 311 µm). The revised plan runs the sweep on existing code from 0:15, makes the as-printed convex surface the screened geometry, takes crowns out of the screen, scores finalists on fresh seeds and puts the FE in a slot of its own. Compute falls from about 17 to about 9 core-hours, inside cores 4-11, 6 GB and 6 h (resguard at 18:55: 18.0 GB available, PSI 0.00/0.00, load 4.66 on 20 cores, gate open).

### Problems found in the design

- [critical] Steps 3-5 (0:20-2:15) and the overall schedule: About 1,500 lines of new code across six modules plus three test files are due by 2:15, and every compute step from 6 onward waits on them. The deterioration sweep is the part the owner asked for, and its flat members need no new geometry: tpu_tip_rig builds any 'tpu<r>' through tpu_models(radii=...) (scripts/tpu_tip_rig.py:302-313), and pad_usability_bed.py:42-52 patches the missing x0 on import. Step 3 also asks for a legacy mode that reproduces fingertip_geometry.pad_spheres to 1e-9 m, which means reimplementing its round() cell counts and Fibonacci corners (scripts/fingertip_geometry.py:45-151) just to pass a test. Fix: Launch the flat-member sweep at 0:15 through a 40-line wrapper over tpu_tip_rig and pad_usability_bed. It redirects MESH_DIR (tpu_tip_rig.py:38 otherwise writes into assets/mjcf/experimental/20261006-tpu_tip) and scales contact_bed_twist.C_LAW per shape. Promote block_law2.py, which already takes r, as the law module. Route legacy 'tpu<r>' keys to the original fingertip_geometry instead of reproducing them. Write TipShape only for the as-built, as-printed and unequal-radius members.
- [critical] Steps 3 and 9, crown variables and the 0.1 mm layer route: Two problems. Step 3 builds crowns as 'cylinders through the apex'. Two orthogonal cylinders intersect in a Steinmetz cap with creases along the diagonals, and the ψ 45 deg contact line runs along one of those creases. It is also not the spherical crown that steps 6 and 9 assume. Separately, the plan's own terrace formula 2·sqrt(2R·0.1 mm) flattens every crown face up, including the sphere cap (2.9 mm at R 10.55), to a terrace wider than its 1 N patch. The 0.1 mm rescue needs layer-height ranges in the 3MF, but write_3mf (eflesh_build.py:153-186) writes only Metadata/Prusa_Slicer_custom_gcode_per_print_z.xml. That leaves the terraced-pad path, which has no Drake reference. With 1/R sampled uniformly over [0, 1/15], about three quarters of Sobol points have a crown of 30 mm or more on at least one axis. Fix: Take crowns out of the screen. Keep the sphere cap (10.55 mm) and a 15 mm crown as deterioration members only, built as a smooth elliptic-paraboloid or torus cap, never as intersecting cylinders. Screen the flat family over (r_side, r_end), which prints face up. State in Future work that crowns need side-on printing (palmar face as perimeters, 'layers along' recipe 11.1 MPa at 95A 20 %) and a re-designed vertical pocket.
- [critical] Step 12 resources: In 4:25-5:15 the plan runs FE on cores 4-7 (3.7-5 GB measured, 4.4 GB in tip_runs_surf.jsonl), confirmation on six processes and iCEM with --nthread 6 on cores 5-10 (the planner example uses resguard --mem 3G), all at once. That exceeds the 6 GB cap and oversubscribes cores 5-7. resguard --cpu does not cap cores (memory note project_resguard_cpu_cap_ineffective.md). Fix: Run the FE alone, after the confirmation batch drains, on taskset -c 4-7 with --mem 6G. Move iCEM to the autopilot's queue or to Future work. It is first in the plan's own cut order, and at 262 s on 6 threads per run (scale_turn_cad_v1/planners/turn.jsonl median 297 s) it does not fit beside anything.
- [major] Steps 9-12, print realisation: Face up at 0.2 mm, PrusaSlicer slices at mid-layer, so the top layer covers the region within 0.1 mm of the apex. The next step down is 0.2 mm, deeper than the 113 µm approach at 3 N. The printed contact is therefore a terrace with bead-rounded (about 0.1 mm) edges, and the fillet radius enters only through the terrace half-width increment sqrt(0.2r − 0.01) mm (0.77 at r 3.0, 1.21 at r 7.4). The printed tip then sits near the r 0 regime, where the pads err most (1 rad/s spin ratio 1.71, edge set back 311 µm, no sphere touching a 45 deg plane until 60 µm). The G-code FE arm of 4.50-4.78 mm, against 3.0 mm homogenized (20261013-eflesh_printability/fem.jsonl), fits this. The plan treats print deviation as a correction applied to a few candidates. Fix: Define as_printed(layer) as the convex hull of the per-layer top outlines (slice at mid-layer, placed at the layer top). It is convex, so Drake's Convex path still applies, and pads sample it like any other shape. Add the as-built as-printed member to the deterioration sweep and evaluate every screened candidate on its as-printed surface. The design-surface as-built tip stays as a reference.
- [major] Step 12 decision: The bootstrap interval pools stage-2 placements, and those placements chose the finalists. Selecting on the same noise biases the paired difference upward. The scale-study best-grasp turn range has a 75th percentile of 34-35 deg over three placements (hom_postoff_servo/turn.jsonl). Fix: Compute the interval on stage-3 seeds only (HOM D1-D8 seeds 5-9, scale cells seeds 3-5). Use stage 2 for selection only.
- [major] Step 10, score J1: 'The best grasp's held turn' is a maximum over about 33 grasps per cell. Its mean over three placements carries a winner's-curse bias that grows with how noisy a candidate is, which favours tips with erratic turns. Fix: Per candidate and cell, pick the grasp on seed 0 and score its held turn on seeds 1-2. Alternatively, score the mean of the top three grasps ranked on seed 0.
- [major] Step 2, tolerance τ: Two friction levels (±10 %) cannot give 'the smallest change that alters 5 % of runs'. μ also scales translational friction, so τ measures friction tolerance and not pinch-torsion tolerance. The bistable placements D3 seeds 0-2 and D8 seed 1 flip under almost any change: in U2, pads end at −2.0 deg on D3 seed 0 where Drake turns 43.2 deg (servo_mujoco.jsonl, servo_drake.jsonl). They are 4 of 64 replays (6.3 %), so they cross the 5 % threshold by themselves. Fix: Use μ ∈ {0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 1.05, 1.1, 1.2} plus μ 1.0 at seeds 5-7, where U2 has no rows. Count changes on non-bistable placements and report the four bistable ones separately. The μ 0.8 level also tests the rate hypothesis for free, because 0.8 ≈ 3.59/4.55 is the ratio of Drake's sliding arm to the pads'. If D3 and D8 seed 1 turn at μ 0.8, the pads' excess torsion is enough to explain the U2 misses.
- [major] Step 7 M5, pinch spin rate from traces: No row stores the tool's angular velocity relative to each fingertip about the pinch normal. Replay rows keep trace_coarse (t, turn, z, force, pad count), and the HOM loop writes the same five fields (pad_usability_backends.py:271-273). A --traces flag means copying RB.run_mujoco (reorient_backends.py:373-461) and hom_turn3.rollout. The knob sweep on r 3.0 (relaxation, damping ratio, impratio) repeats probes already run on r 2.7, where tr 0.01/0.02/0.04 s gave 4.40/4.55/6.79 mm and impratio 10-10,000 gave 4.545-4.555 mm. Terms. Approach: the tool's displacement into the tip along the pinch axis at pinch force N, in µm. Contact-line angle ψ: the angle between the tool axis and the tip's width direction (the PIP axis). The bench grasp starts at ψ = 0, and a turn about the pinch axis changes ψ. Onset arm: the torque about the pinch axis at which a pinched tool starts to spin under a ramped torque, divided by 2μN, in mm (bed task T2). Sliding arm: the mean torque that drives the tool to spin at rate ω, divided by 2μN; the quasi-static arm is its limit as ω goes to 0. N45: the pinch force at which a tool released horizontal, CG 15 mm from the pinch, passes 45 deg while the pinch falls from 6 to 0.2 N over 4 s (T5). Creep: steady sliding speed at half the slip force, in µm/s (T1). Law integral: the pressure p = Eδ/h integrated over the overlap of the rigid tool and the tip surface, with δ the local overlap. In the Winkler law, h is fixed at 8.5 mm, as in the current builder. In the local law, h_i = n·(x − c_V), the normal distance from the hull's volume centroid c_V, which equals Drake's field to first order. The drake field is E(1 − gauge) on the hull facets about c_V. Quasi-static pads: the builder's spheres and springs K_i summed over the same overlap, without a simulator. n_patch: loaded pads per side. ρ90: the 90th-percentile distance of the loaded pads from the pinch axis. Slip number Π = ω ρ90 t_c / δ, with t_c the pads' time constant (0.01 s). Scallop: the depth by which the sphere envelope sits below a plane tangent to a fillet between two arc rows, (r − rs)(1 − cos(Δθ/2)); it is 37.5 µm at r 2.7. Held turn: the tool's turn at the end of a run if at least two fingers press it with at least its weight, otherwise 0. Three-finger hold: all three fingers press with at least the tool's weight at the end. E*: the pad modulus that fits the finite-element approach of a printed tip at 0.5, 1 and 3 N. Servo plant: kp0.72_kv0_fr0.18_fl0_dp0.0144. τ: the hand's tolerance from step 2.

## Reader maps

### geometry_pads_reference

The pad builder covers one shape family. scripts/fingertip_geometry.py samples the Minkowski sum of a box (17.0 x 14.8 x 22.0 mm, or 21.41 mm on cad_v1) and a sphere of radius r. It puts 0.75 mm spheres on a 1 mm grid with their centres inset by rs (clamped into the box shrunk by rs), leaves out the back face and the mount side, and gives each sphere K = E a / h with h = 8.5 mm everywhere. reorient_backends.replace_tips then gives every pad of a finger the median K (1,199.8 N/m at r 2.7) through one solimp d0 = 1 - 1/(tc^2 K Lambda).

Drake's reference for the same block is a compliant Convex. A pydrake 1.57 probe shows its volume mesh is a fan of 1,164 tetrahedra from the hull's volume centroid, with pressure E(1 - gauge). That equals E delta/8.5 mm on the palmar face, E delta/7.4 mm on the side faces, E delta/11.0 mm on the end face and about E delta/10.1 mm on 45 deg fillet facets. No repository code integrates this field for the block. A scratch integral (2 s per radius) reproduces Drake's bed onset arms within 3 % and its 1 rad/s spin arms within 8 % at r 0, 2.7 and 6 mm.

Geometry costs the pads accuracy in four measurable ways:
1. The fixed h makes the pads 25-37 % stiffer than Drake on the end face and 4-8 % softer on the side faces.
2. On fillets the sphere envelope is scalloped by (r - rs)(1 - cos(dtheta/2)): 37.5 um at r 2.7 and 20 um at r 6, against approaches of 35-113 um at 0.5-3 N. A plane at 45 deg on the 2.7 mm fillet touches no sphere until 37.5 um.
3. At a sharp edge the clamped side spheres double the edge row (+7.5 % static arm) and set the edge back by 311 um.
4. The median K ignores area shares of 0.94-1.11 mm^2.

None of these explains the bed's sliding-arm excess on the 2.7 mm block (4.55 against Drake's 3.59 mm at 1 N): the same pad lattice, integrated quasi-statically, gives 3.58 mm. A traced spin shows the excess grows with spin rate:
- The pad arm is 3.90, 4.24, 4.55 and 7.02 mm at 0.1, 0.3, 1 and 3 rad/s; Drake gives 3.50, 3.59 and 4.14 mm at 0.3, 1 and 3 rad/s.
- The normal force stays at 1.000 N and the tool neither drifts (at most 0.05 mm) nor tilts.
- Each sphere's normal force exceeds K x penetration by 3x near the spin axis and up to 87x at the ends of the contact line.
- The pad relaxation time changes it (4.40 mm at 0.01 s, 6.79 mm at 0.04 s); impratio from 10 to 10,000 does not.

The brake gap has a different cause. Drake's own 45 deg force is not converged in time step (0.452, 0.497 and 0.527 N at 2, 1 and 0.5 ms), and the pads' value does not change with relaxation time.

Finite elements disagree with every pressure law in a further way: approach errors of 20-28 %, a twist arm 3.5-23 % short and a coupling length of 2.9 mm. The CAD insert has 3.0 mm fillets and spherical corners, not the 2.7 mm of the default model.

A new shape end to end needs:
- a TipShape module (samples with curvature, hull and STL, insert-frame inside and surface functions for FE, volume centroid, a key);
- a pad builder with per-sphere h and K;
- a law integral with a tool-rotation angle;
- a shape key in the tpu_tip_rig specs and in replace_tips;
- an FE geometry hook.
Drake's Convex path works unchanged for any convex hull.

### material_print

Print settings reach the simulation through one number, the pads' modulus E*. The finite-element (FE) scripts model four print choices: the TPU grade as an isotropic solid modulus E_s (30 MPa for 95A, 12 MPa for 85A; 95A datasheets span 26-67 MPa), gyroid density (5-50 %), build direction, and the magnet pocket. Walls (2 x 0.45 mm), skins (4 x 0.2 mm), layer height (0.2 mm) and bead width (0.45 mm) are module constants. The fastest proxy is the FE table: the insert's E* is 6.6, 13.0, 22.9 and 34.8 MPa at 10-40 % 95A gyroid, which fits E* = 98 MPa x infill^1.19, and inside the 0.5-3 N fit window E* scales as E_s^1.4. Full FE costs 4 min per tip on four CPU cores (homogenized core), 1-3 min per approach on the RTX 4070 Ti SUPER (0.1 mm voxels), and 9-20 min per tip solved from its G-code.

E* is a golden-section fit of the Winkler law p = E x overlap / 8.5 mm to the FE approach under the 12.5 mm shaft at 0.5, 1 and 3 N. One modulus misses the approach by 20-28 % because the FE force grows as approach^1.06-1.16 and the law's as approach^1.5; the modulus fitted at each force falls from 25.7 MPa at 0.25 N to 7.7 MPa at 4 N. The walls carry 46-66 % of the load on the face's edges against 23-30 % for the law, which shortens the pads' twist arm by 7-23 %, and the tip's shear compliance (24 and 53 um/N) is absent. The FE is uncertain by a factor of two: from its G-code the 95A 20 % magnet tip has E* 7.3 MPa and a 4.6 mm twist arm at 1 N, homogenized 14.6 MPa and 3.0 mm. An effective depth h adds no freedom, since h enters only as E/h. The stored FE data support two better calibrations, neither coded: a per-pad stiffness map that stiffens pads over the walls, and stiff pads on a normal series spring of the FE contact stiffness (15-45 N/mm). The calibration tools scale E* linearly with the bench E_s; a refit gives E_s^1.4, so a print half as stiff as modelled receives an E* 28 % too high.

The SR2 block prints face up without supports as one island per layer (21 min, 3.2 g); the press-fit pocket pauses before layer 62 at 12.4 mm. eFlesh lattices print soundly only at 8 mm cells on the 0.4 mm nozzle. Five-chip force noise is about 1.2 um over the magnet's travel per newton: a softer tip senses finer and sinks deeper.

Printable output exists for the SR2 block (STL, JSON sheet) and for eFlesh lattices of any closed shape (PrusaSlicer 3MF with the pause, 9-12 s); no tool writes a gyroid project with the pause, and the print/ STLs lie in the FE frame. Friction is unmeasured (scenes use 1.0), the servo is 79-278 times softer than the contact, and TPU damping is not modelled.

Seven CPU harnesses can score a fingertip candidate. Fingertip geometry has changed outcomes in two harnesses.
- On the bed, the static friction arm at 0.5 N is 0.79, 1.12, 2.42 and 4.00 mm for the sphere, the 6 mm fillet, the 2.7 mm fillet and the sharp box. The brake passes 45° at 1.23, 0.95, 0.34 and 0.20 N. Drake gives onset arms within 6.5 % of the pads.

Fine geometry effects in the replay are therefore about as large as the disagreement between contact models.

HOM, the scale-study tasks and iCEM have run only on the 2.7 mm block. RL policies on the servo plant keep the tool in 1,527-1,531 of 1,536 rollouts across box, mesh, pad and skin tips. Halving friction or servo gain drops their open-loop replays to 292 and 198 of 384.

The pads' agreement with Drake gets worse as the fillets get sharper, but only in sliding. The pads/Drake ratio of the sliding (kinetic) friction arm is 0.96-0.98 on the sphere, 0.99-1.07 at 6 mm, 1.18-1.67 at 2.7 mm and 1.43-1.74 on the sharp box. The brake force at 45° follows the same trend: −2 %, −32 % and −41 % against Drake. Two features of the builder fit this:
- every pad of a finger gets the median spring;
- at r 2.7 mm, pads on the fillets are 1.38 times (edges) and 1.92 times (corners) denser per unit of tool area.

Design lever (an inference from the bed and the controllers): the friction torque about the pinch axis is mu N rbar, and N is servo-limited.
