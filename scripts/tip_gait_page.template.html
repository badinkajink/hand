Bench partial reorientation: support geometry, plan family and a two-tip gait

The deployed plan’s 50° tip on the calibrated plant (cos 0.638 in simulation against 0.593 on the bench, rv05) ends where the tool’s upper end meets the palm plate. In the scene that produced the match the plate sits on the mounting plane; the built plate is 25 mm higher, and with it in place the same plan tips the tool to {{D7_FAST_PL25}}° from vertical on the template contact model and drops it on the Coulomb-like one. Commanding a larger arc adds nothing, and re-applying the plan’s turn after a regrip returns the tool to flat. An RL environment on the calibrated plant now exists for D6.

The rv05 tip, contact by contact

deployed plan rv05_manual_b85 on cal, plate at 0 · axial position of each pad’s contact along the shaft from the tool’s centre (+ toward the end that rises), normal force

The plan lifts the tool off the post with three pads at 1.4, 1.1 and 0.3 N and 17° of tilt already in the grip. Over the 1.1 s turn the middle pad slides 19 mm along the shaft toward the centre (its yaw and pip flexion carry it there), the index leaves at three quarters of the turn, and the thumb slides 9 mm the other way. From half-way on, the tool is a two-pad pinch between thumb and middle at 1–1.9 N, and its upper end (+50 mm, the end of the 100 mm cylinder) presses on the palm plate at 0.4–0.5 N. That is the rest: a wedge between two pads and the plate, 50° from vertical, retained by the pinch and not by gravity alone.

The plate is the stop, and the built plate is 25 mm higher

eight deployed plans · three seeds (1 mm spawn jitter on seeds 1–2) · degrees from vertical at the end of the hold for every held rollout · bench column: the 2026-09-02 archive, 251 runs

Every bench replay so far, including the plant-correction gate of 2026-09-02, ran with the plate on the mounting plane. Moving it to the measured +25 mm changes the result on every hand that reaches it. On fast, rv05 goes from {{D7_CAL_PL0}}° (plate at 0, cal) and 55–57° (plate at 0, fast) to {{D7_FAST_PL25}}° from vertical, D2 from 46–75° to 16–22°, D4 from 57–61° to 24–36°; D1 and D8, which the plate at 0 kept in the hand, drop the tool during the turn with the plate at 25. The bench measured 57° on rv05 and 42° on D2. So the simulation that matched the bench had a support the built hand does not have at that height, and the simulation with the built plate over-turns.

The 25 mm between the mounting plane and the plate is not empty on the hardware: the yaw servos are bolted to the underside of the palm and sit in that gap. The scene carries no collision geometry for them. The tool’s upper end at 50° is where the old plate was, which is where the servo bank is. This is a hypothesis about what the tool rests on; the bench can settle it by looking (below).

On cal the picture is different again: D1, D2 and D8 lose the tool at the grip on the post (the plan’s 10 mm squeeze at μ 1.0 pops it out before the turn), D6 holds only with the plate at 25, and rv05 holds at plate 0 and drops at plate 25. The two contact models disagree about the grip itself, not only about the turn, and neither has been checked against the bench’s grip forces.

Commanding a larger arc does not extend the tip

rv05 re-exported with the deployed plan’s grasp, pivot (axis_k 0.05) and budget (0.85 rad) at 60, 75, 90 and 110° · plate at 0 · two seeds

The re-export at 60° does not reproduce the deployed plan: its turn_end differs by up to 10° on the middle mcp (the exporter is not bit-reproducible across the palm-plate and generator changes since 2026-09-02), and it tips the tool only to 73–86° from vertical. The 75, 90 and 110° exports saturate the middle yaw and pip at the budget and land at 70–89°. The deployed plan’s 50° is a property of that one trajectory, not of the commanded angle.

Two tips with a regrip between them

rv05 · tip = the plan’s turn deltas applied from the current command · rest 1.5 s · regrip = each finger’s mcp closes 1° per 0.1 s from its commanded pose until its pads carry 0.8 N

The regrip works: on cal it brings the tool from two pads to three at 51° without moving it. The second tip does not. The plan’s deltas are the specific asymmetric opening that lets the tool wedge against the plate from the flat grip; applied again from the regripped command they open the same fingers further and the tool falls back to flat (90° from vertical, held on two pads on cal; lost on fast). A second step needs a second primitive, one that moves the pinch line rather than re-opening the grip, and nothing in the exported plans provides it.

An RL environment on the calibrated plant

scripts/make_cal_morphology_run.py · results/phase1/real_v1/20260916-<hand>_cal/

The trainer reads a morphology-run directory: a frozen scene, the grip, and a reference rollout. The new builder takes the chain’s flat scene for a hand (the fitter’s grasp at 10 mm squeeze as the open_ik keyframe), applies the measured servo (kp 0.5, kv 0.02, forcerange 0.35 N·m, frictionloss 0.0035, real masses), the Coulomb-like contact model (elliptic cone, impratio 10, pad μ 1.0), moves the plate to +25 mm, and stores the Phase-1 evaluator’s own settle–lift–hold as the reference. MuJoCo-Warp compiles the elliptic cone and impratio 10: a 12-iteration smoke run at 1024 environments trained at 3.05 s per iteration (8,000 steps/s).

  • D6 (sv1_u0308_b050) holds the evaluator’s lift on cal: peak 97 mm, three pads at the end, contact persistence 1.00. A 20 M-step run (3072 environments, seed 0, the b_liveA recipe without a live Policy A, the residual active from step 58 on top of the scripted lift, friction DR 0.55–1.15, object pose in the observation) started at 21:44.
  • D8 (sv1_w0099_b100) drops it: peak 86 mm, persistence 0.53, two pads through the ramp. The chain’s arm lift held D8 21 of 34 at 10 mm and 12 of 12 at 12 mm; a 12 mm flat scene has to be built before D8 trains.
  • Not in the environment yet: servo-gain randomisation (kp 0.25–0.5) and torsional-friction randomisation (condim 4, 0–0.005). Both are unmeasured on the real pads and both changed the pinch-and-swing outcome on 2026-09-16.

What this does not settle

  • What the tool rests on at the bench’s 50°. One bench run of rv05_manual_b85 with a look at the handle end at the rest: does it touch a yaw servo housing, the plate, or nothing? If a servo: add the servo-bank boxes to assets/mjcf/real_v1/real_hand.xml from the CAD and regenerate the bench scenes with build_real_v1_scenes.py --keep-keyframes; if nothing, the bench’s 50° has a different cause than the simulation’s and the plant-correction gate needs re-running with the plate at 25.
  • Which contact model matches the bench’s grip. cal loses D1, D2 and D8 at the grip on the post; fast keeps all eight. The bench keeps all eight. Pad μ on the real pads, and whether the pop-out is a μ effect or an impratio effect: replay the grip alone with real_v1_turn_probe.contact_variant over μ 1.0–2.4 and impratio 1–10 on D8.
  • The RL run. Judge it on deterministic held-cos over seeds and plant draws, on the eval video, and on the input-replay test before any claim; the August policies on this hand ignored their observations.
