Program record behind the SR2 Hand workshop paper

The workshop paper reports a sampling funnel, eight hands on a bench and a plan. Of {{N_SAMPLED}} sampled finger layouts, {{N_CON}} passed the simulated retention screen and its confirmation, 19 were exported as plans and eight were built. On the bench the eight held the tool in 48 of 77 scored trials, and the simulated and measured alignment rank them at ρs = {{RHO_ALL}} (p = {{P_ALL}}). No learned controller reached the bench. This page gives the source and definition of each number in the paper and the record of the experiments from April to September 2026.

Terms and metrics

Layout, D1–D8
A finger layout is one placement of the three mounts on the gantries; D1–D8 are the eight layouts built and run on the bench.
Retention screen, confirmation
The simulated test of a layout: a 60 mm proof lift and 5 s of free hold at some residual clip; its repeat and full-error stages.
Plan
A layout’s exported joint trajectory, as served to the hand by the control station.
Retention (bench)
The operator’s verdict that the tool is still in the hand at the end of a trial.
Alignment
Signed cosine of the tool’s axis with vertical over the final hold, from the AprilTag vane (cos_hold); +1 is upright.
Scored trial
A bench trial with both a tag angle and a retention verdict.
ρs
Spearman rank correlation between simulated and measured alignment over the eight layouts.

Discrepancies between the PDF and the record

Places where the PDF and the record disagree, and likely questions the paper does not answer

Read first
  1. Trial count and D1’s hold rate. The PDF reports 80 trials, 51 held, D1 9/10. The corrected analysis of 2026-09-01 (paper/transfer_corrections.tex, docs/experiments/20260901-real_v1-transfer-firstpass/NUMBERS_CHANGED.md) gives 77 trials, 48 held, 28 dropped and 1 unresolved, with D1 at 6/7. The three extra D1 holds come from a 15-trial session in which the operator scored three trials, all holds; they enter D1’s alignment pool (nc = 9) and are excluded from its hold rate because they were kept for the outcome being measured. Use 6/7.
  2. “64 % success rate in reorientation” is the hold rate (51 of 80 held). The paper has no separate reorientation criterion; §3 proposes one that the data supports.
  3. The 6,629 “hardware-feasible” count used rail limits that were later corrected. On 2026-08-30 the driver took J1/J3/J5 to travel 56.2/56.0/54.1 mm; on 2026-09-01 all six axes were measured at their full nominal travel (110 mm, 60 mm). All {{N_REJ}} rejections are on the x axes by 3.8–5.9 mm ({{RAILS_NOTE}}); under the corrected rails all {{N_SAMPLED}} fit. The rejected corner is the compact end of x, which the retention screen favours. Quote “6,629 under the travel envelope measured at the time”.
  4. Table IV’s simulated prediction is the bench-schedule replay. Each “Sim c” is the mean held cosine of four replicates of the exported plan on a fixed palm, with the tool on the 100 mm post and a 9.6 s hold (deploy_plan_bands_all.json). The retention screen that selected the population ranks the same hands differently (D3 first at 0.913; D1 first in the replay at 0.827); §4 tabulates both.
  5. D4’s peak substitution is the most favourable value it could take. Over every value D4’s bench cosine could hold, ρs ranges {{SENS_LO}}–{{SENS_HI}}; the substituted 0.819 gives {{RHO_ALL}}, and dropping D4 gives {{RHO_NO4}} on seven hands.
  6. The eight hands are a concentrated set. Their median pairwise mount separation is 43.6 mm against 66.9 mm for eight random members of the population, below the first percentile of 20,000 random draws of eight; seven of the eight sit in the compact quadrant. A rank correlation over them describes one corner of the design space.
  7. Table I’s yaw range is the servo’s. ±85° is the measured range; the live command cap is ±70° (manta_hand/plan.py), and one promoted design (sv1_w0116) could not be deployed because its turn needed −84° of middle yaw.
  8. “Up to 110 Hz control” is the read rate. Nine position reads run at 111 Hz with the FTDI latency timer at 1 ms, and a write-plus-nine-reads loop at 75 Hz. The deployed policy stream writes without reading the servos, so a residual policy on the current path would see the AprilTag pose and its own commands only.

Design space

A configuration is the palm-frame (x, y) of each finger mount: the thumb inside a 60 × 110 mm box, index and middle inside 60 × 60 mm boxes, centred on the CAD-nominal mounts at (−50, 0), (50, 55) and (50, −55) mm. The fingers are identical CAD parts (links 33.45, 33.45 and 37.16 mm axis to surface, capsule radius 10.55 mm), so a sampled hand is realised by driving three gantries. The earlier simulation program had nine parameters, the extra three being proximal-length slides; REAL_V1_WORKSPACE pins them to zero because the hardware has no such joint. All six slides at zero is the CAD-nominal hand, the widest tripod the machine offers.

Stage A: the population ({{N_SAMPLED}})

The population is a scrambled Sobol sequence in seven dimensions (scipy.stats.qmc.Sobol(d=7, scramble=True, seed=20260830)). Six coordinates map to the mounts. The seventh selects a stratum: points with u7 < 0.25 have their index-x and middle-x transformed by u → u0.75, which shifts them toward the outward end of their travel, and three quarters of the population stays uniform. Tags name the stratum (sv1_u uniform, sv1_w biased). The first N points of a longer draw are exactly the shorter draw, so the 128-point pilot, the 4,096 screen and the 8,192 screen are nested and a design keeps its tag and scene across passes; D5 (sv1_u0060) was promoted from the 128-point prefix. Keeping the stratum choice inside the sequence keeps the 25 % mix even at every prefix.

Six anchor hands are added to every population so that each pass can be compared with the earlier results: rv00_wide, rv01_compact, rv03_narrowy, rv04_mid (the 2 × 2 factorial plus centre of the first real_v1 study), rv05_manual (the hand-authored design) and g12 (the first design with a collision-safe exported plan). 8,192 + 6 = {{N_SAMPLED}}.

Stage A2: the rails ({{N_REACH}})

Each design’s mounts are checked against the gantry travel with the mount_violations call the deployment plan uses, on the design vector itself: seven designs sit within 0.05 mm of a limit, where the manifest’s rounded mounts flip the verdict. The envelope in force on 2026-08-30 was short on three axes (item 3); rv01_compact is among the {{N_REJ}} rejected.

Stage B: the grasp fit ({{N_GRASP}} designs, {{N_CELLS}} rows)

fit_real_v1_pose.py solves, in closed form and then by IK, a palm height and nine finger angles that put three pads on a 25 mm shaft lying on a 100 mm post. The index and middle pads straddle the thumb along the shaft, the thumb sits an axial offset from their midpoint, and every pad sits at or below the shaft’s equator; a pad above the equator wedges the shaft out within 0.6 s of standing still (measured 2026-09-06). The fit runs at four cells, straddle 32 or 40 mm × thumb offset 10 or 20 mm, with the grip depth searched and a 4 mm commanded squeeze. A design admits a cell when all three pads reach their targets inside joint limits without the MCP reaching its −15° stop; compact hands fail because the pads cannot ring the shaft, wide hands because the pair cannot reach across it. {{N_GRASP}} of {{N_REACH}} admit at least one cell; rows by cell: {{CELLS_BY_GRASP}}. The thumb offset is in the grid because in the earlier 108-hand search sliding the thumb 20 mm along the shaft took rv04_mid from held cos 0.000 (0/8) to 0.972 (8/8), by giving the thumb’s normal force a moment arm about the pinch axis.

Stage D: the retention screen ({{N_SEL}})

Every (design, cell) row runs once at each of five residual clips, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.1 and 1.3 rad, for {{N_ROLLOUTS}} rollouts on CPU MuJoCo (2 ms steps, 18 workers, about an hour). One rollout is the bench maneuver:

  1. The tool stands on a 100 mm post at y = −35 mm with the palm fixed; the pads are the built hand’s flat 14.8 × 21.1 mm faces (--flat-pads), since the earlier round-capsule model made point contact and mis-sized every grasp by 2–5 mm.
  2. The fingers close to the fitted grip with a 10 mm commanded squeeze, then hold a 400-step capture (0.8 s) under a load regulator (target 250 load units per finger, as on the bench) and a turn-phase torque ceiling of 0.180 N·m (80 % of the SCS0009 stall torque, where its overload protection fires).
  3. The turn rotates the grasp anchor by −80° about an axis parallel to the pinch axis, raised above the contact plane by axis_k = 0.15 of the half-straddle (5–6 mm). Per-finger IK maps the moving pad targets to joints, and a straight joint-space line from the anchor to the end pose plays over 550 steps (1.1 s), each joint’s excursion clipped to ±clip. The torque ceiling then drops to the rated 0.069 N·m for the hold.
  4. A proof lift raises the palm 60 mm over 700 steps (1.4 s); only a captured tool follows.
  5. A 2,500-step hold (5.0 s) follows, during which the tool may slip at most 10 mm.

A rollout is retained (ok) when at least one hand contact remains, the tool’s minimum height stays within 20 mm of its lifted height, it never touches the post, the proof lift raised it at least 48 mm (80 % of the commanded 60) and it slipped at most 10 mm through the hold. A (cell, clip) row passes when it is retained, its final signed cosine to vertical is at least 0.7 (within 45.6° of vertical), the index–middle clearance along the turn is at least 5 mm and no commanded joint exceeds the servo’s measured range. A design passes when any of its rows does. {{N_SEL}} pass, {{N_SEL05}} of them at 0.5 rad and {{N_SEL_INV}} only at another clip (selected clips: {{SEL_BY_CLIP}}).

The clip is swept because every screen before 2026-08-30 planned the turn at 0.5 rad, the action budget of the reinforcement-learning residual. Re-screening the Sobol-4096 population over five clips showed that each hand keeps the tool only inside a contiguous band of clips, that alignment is not monotone inside the band, and that a single-clip screen misses 14 % of the passing population. One trial per row keeps the screen cheap enough to run {{N_ROLLOUTS}} times; the confirmation repeats the survivors.

Stage 3: confirmation ({{N_CON}})

Each of the {{N_SEL}} designs is re-run at its selected (cell, clip): five nominal replicates (0.5 mm spawn jitter) and four draws from the half-error ensemble of §5. A design confirms when at least three of the five nominal replicates are retained, the mean held cosine (a released replicate scoring 0) is at least 0.7, and the clearance and servo gates still pass. {{N_CON}} confirm, {{N_CON05}} at 0.5 rad ({{CON_BY_CLIP}}). Stage 4 draws twenty full-error rollouts per confirmed design ({{N_FULL}} cells); its outputs, ens_kept and ens_win (retained with cosine ≥ 0.7), rank robustness for the promotion.

Promotion from {{N_CON}} to eight

Promotion is a hand-triggered step per pass. The leaders of each pass by full-error win are exported (real_v1_export_plan.py: three set-points with the clip, a 50 Hz per-joint trajectory CSV, a build sheet and a pose sheet) and gated: every commanded joint inside the servo’s measured range with the ±70° yaw cap, every mount inside the gantry travel, index–middle clearance of at least 5 mm along both the three-set-point chord the station interpolates and the CSV path (a plan can clear one and not the other), and plan validity. The survivors are band-scanned on the bench schedule (33 clips × 4 replicates, 9.6 s hold) and shipped at a clip inside the band, at least 0.10 rad above its lower edge to absorb the measured yaw droop. The retention gate and the bench schedule disagree about which hands work: of the five gate-passers promoted from the 8,192 pass, two had a bench band (sv1_w6689, sv1_u7952), and of sixteen from the 4,096 re-screen, eleven kept the tool at no clip on the bench schedule. Nineteen plans reached the station across all passes.

The eight bench hands are the top eight distinct morphologies among the sixteen exported, clearance-safe plans, ordered by the bench-schedule replay (docs/experiments/20260831-real_v1-transfer-protocol/README.md, §3). Six are Sobol members (D1, D2, D3, D5, D6, D8), D4 is the g12 anchor and D7 the manual hand. The clearance gate excluded three exported plans before the study: g23 (0.8 mm), rv04_mid (−2.6 mm) and g24 (−5.2 mm; the simulator scores it 0.750, which would have placed it second).

Selected region of the design space

Selection moves toward compact tripods. In the plane of thumb-to-pair span and index–middle opening (both linear in the six mounts), the quadrant below the population medians holds 25 % of the reachable population, 65 % of the retained, 71 % of the confirmed and seven of the eight deployed; the screen cuts the spread of those two coordinates to 0.59 and 0.74 of the population’s. Principal components of the 535 retained designs explain 47, 21 and 17 % on three directions and 8, 4 and 2 % on the rest, so the survivors lie near a three-dimensional sheet in the six-dimensional box. The depth of the grasp fit separates retained from discarded designs at AUC 0.80 and does not predict alignment (ρs +0.08 over the 535); the KaRMA rolling-pinch metric behaves the same way (AUC 0.80 for retention, ρs 0.11 against the screened turn).

The funnel trained no policy and scored one maneuver. The retention gate’s proof lift differs from the bench’s fixed-palm hold, the two order hands differently, and the paper tests the bench schedule’s ranking. The simulator retained the tool on all four replicates of seven of the eight deployed plans, so its retention prediction does not distinguish the bench’s 2/10 from its 10/10.

The simulator and the bench differ in one convention. The simulator’s held cosine counts a release as zero, a retention-weighted alignment; the bench cosine averages the trials that held. The pre-registered protocol asked for parity (“a bench trial that released the tool contributes cos_up = 0”); the analysis averaged held trials because the tag cannot tell a turn from a fall. The two conventions rank the hands differently (§4).

Proposed reorientation criterion

Forty-four of the 48 held trials finish between 20° and 60° of net turn, against 4 of the 28 drops. The drops leave that band in both directions: 12 pass 60°, 12 stop short of 20°. The proposal from the record (project_eight_hand_bench_record): a trial is a reorientation when it is held, its net turn is inside 20–60°, and its slip is under 20 mm (held slips run 4.9–10.9 mm; ejections 24–54 mm). Under that rule the eight hands score 44/77, against the PDF’s 51/80, and every count is attributable to a trial.

Restricting each trial to rotation recorded while the cylinder was still rising moves the drop side of the contingency from 4/28 to 17/28: most drops turn as far as the holds before they let go, so the 20–60° band separates outcomes after the fact. What the tag cannot supply is a contact signal, and the transfer sessions logged no servo load (fixed at the source for later sessions: --telemetry-hz 10 --servo-fields load). The two drop modes that remain after the correction are identified by lateral travel and by a shortfall in rotation: D6’s seven drops travel 24–54 mm with no net turn (ejection), and D3’s five drops travel like a hold and stop at 25° against its holds’ 42° (stall, p = 0.040). “Overshoot” was withdrawn: D8’s held and dropped trials are identical to 1.4 s, then the holds finish at 46–59°/s and stop at 52° while the drops rotate at 166–217°/s with the centre descending at about half of g, so the extra 20° is the shaft being flung.

A rotation threshold that the simulator and the bench could share is c ≥ 0.7 on a held trial. Against the corrected per-hand means, D1 (0.834), D2 (0.797), D4 (0.819, peak), D5 (0.938) and D8 (0.773) sit above it and D3 (0.689), D6 (0.614) and D7 (0.553) below; D7, the hand that retains every trial, is 0.15 short of it with sd 0.049. A per-trial count under this rule needs the corrected per-trial table regenerated: data.json in the first-pass folder predates the 2026-09-01 corrections, and real_v1_transfer_figures.py currently fails on D4’s void hold window when asked to rebuild it.

Effect of scoring on held trials

The bench alignment is averaged over held trials, and the survival fraction runs from 0.2 to 1.0 across the eight: D5’s 0.938 is the mean of its three holds, and D6’s 0.614 rests on two. Scoring a release as zero, as the simulator does, gives D1 0.715, D2 0.797, D3 0.275, D4 0.737, D5 0.281, D6 0.123, D7 0.553 and D8 0.309, and ρs = {{RHO_RW}} (p = {{P_RW}}) against the simulated column instead of {{RHO_ALL}}, with D5 moving from first to sixth. Held-only alignment measures how far a hand turns the tool when it keeps it, which the geometric plan predicts; retention-weighted alignment measures how often a trial ends with a turned, held tool, which a downstream task needs. The paper should name its convention and report the other beside it.

Hold rate and alignment are nearly uncorrelated (ρs = {{RHO_HOLD_BENCH}}): the hands that turn furthest when they hold drop most often (D5, D8), and the hand that always holds turns least (D7). One open-loop plan trades the two: in the simulated chain no cell holds the tool past about 59° of turn and every cell that turns 44° or less holds it 4/4 (docs/experiments/20260906-screened_turn/), because the index pad leaves at about half the turn and the total pad force falls from 28 N to 0.3 N by 70 % of it.

The D4 peak substitution

On all nine of D4’s held trials the vane leaves the decodable image near vertical, so its hold-window cosine is void and its peak stands in. Peak and hold alignment agree in rank (ρs = {{RHO_PEAK_BENCH}} over the eight, 0.86 over the 52 complete traces), and the peak is always the larger, by a residual sd of 0.103, so the substituted 0.819 is an upper bound. Removing D4 gives ρs = {{RHO_NO4}} on seven hands, and any value from 0 to 1 gives ρs between {{SENS_LO}} and {{SENS_HI}}; the reported value sits at the top of that range. A vane with the tag 50 mm from the centre instead of 77 mm cuts the swept arc by a third and keeps the tag decodable.

Eight hands

The eight-point coefficient {{RHO_ALL}} has p = {{P_ALL}} and a bootstrap 95 % interval over hands of [{{BOOT_LO}}, {{BOOT_HI}}]; the two-sided 5 % critical value at n = 8 is 0.74. The pre-registered protocol planned sixteen plans at four trials (critical value 0.50); the study ran eight morphologies at ten trials. The secondary correlations are descriptive at this n: contact force {{RHO_FORCE}} (p = 0.09), contact count {{RHO_CONT}}, and nothing simulated orders hold rate (−0.29 ≤ ρs ≤ 0.27). Two further limits apply:

  • Within-eight correlations inherit the selection. The eight were chosen in simulated-alignment order, so any variable correlated with the selection correlates with alignment inside the set: the depth of the grasp fit reads ρs = −0.90 (p = 0.002) over the eight and +0.076 over the 535 retained designs.
  • The set is narrow. The median pairwise mount separation of 43.6 mm (minimum 14.4 mm, D4–D8) is below the first percentile of random eights from the population.

The plant correction of 2026-09-02 (finger gain 30 → 0.5) reproduces rv05’s level (retention 0.94 against the bench’s 0.92, cosine 0.638 against 0.593) and moves ρs over seven hands only from −0.54 to +0.11 in that harness. Its two largest misses are grip cases in opposite directions (D3 slips on the bench and never in simulation; D8 drops in every simulated trial and in some bench trials), so the missing physics is contact.

Repeatability and placement sensitivity

Morphology positioning, trial-to-trial repeatability on one plan, session-to-session drift and the simulated sensitivity to tool placement

Morphology positioning

The six gantries run on T5 × 1 lead screws at 3,216 steps/mm and home sensorlessly against a hard stop (TMC5160 StallGuard2, with a timeout fallback on which J3 and J5 always home). A 45 mm caliper check on J1/J3/J5 found 0.0 % scale error (verify_frame_mapping.py --travel). The home’s repeatability has not been measured (home ten times and read one mount against a dial indicator, half an hour); in simulation a 2 mm mount error changes retention through the turn from 77 % to 80 % over 18 designs, and the nearest two deployed hands are 14.4 mm apart.

Trial-to-trial repeatability

Stage 1 of the protocol ran twelve consecutive trials of D1’s plan with a full re-stage between each; the seven with a hold-window cosine give a mean of +0.881 and an sd of 0.011 (95 % interval on the sd 0.007–0.024), and the other five lost the vane tag, which led to the 40 mm tag (docs/experiments/20260831-real_v1-transfer-protocol/sessions/20260831-1845-sv1_w6689-stage1/README.md). Against the simulated between-plan spread of 0.24, four trials per plan resolve a design difference of 0.02. Re-staging is the variance: three holds from a later session tripled D1’s sd from 0.032 to 0.092, and the same plan drifts ±5° between sessions. Within-design sd for six of the seven measurable hands is 0.027–0.061 against a between-design spread of 0.49–0.94; D3 is the exception at 0.221, a mixture of held grips and partial slips.

Tag tracker

A 40 mm static tag gives the world-up datum, and a tag on a vane along the shaft (30 mm on a 71 mm vane at first, then 40 mm on a 77 mm vane) gives the tool’s pose, read by a RealSense D435 at 30 fps with 0.017° rms angle noise and 0.030 mm rms height noise. The operator’s by-eye estimate was ±5° and missed axial slip: one earlier “90°” was the shaft falling through the grasp.

Simulated sensitivity to manual placement

The operator centres the cylinder on the post by eye. The deployment envelope (scripts/real_v1_deploy_envelope.py) plans on the nominal hand and replays the unchanged plan on perturbed scenes, one factor at a time and as an ensemble at level L: placement N(0, 3 mm · L) in x and y, yaw N(0, 8° · L), mount error |N(0, 1.5 mm · L)|, a servo zero offset |N(0, 2° · L)| per joint, and log-uniform multipliers on mass (0.6–2.0), radius (0.92–1.08), friction (0.45–1.8), contact stiffness, servo gain (0.3–2.5), torque (0.35–1.5) and damping. Half error is L = 0.5, the tolerance a careful operator holds; full error is L = 1.0.

Learned controllers and the derived controller the paper deploys

What was trained, why it is not on the bench, and its state on 2026-09-20 · the pipeline with a decision register is on the robust-tranche page (local · published)

Training setup

The task is the paper’s: a 25 × 100 mm cylinder lying on the floor is grasped, lifted 100 mm and turned to vertical by the nine finger joints. The policy is a residual: at 50 Hz it emits nine numbers, scaled by 0.5 rad and added to a scripted finger set-point that eases from the open keyframe to the fitted grip, while the palm follows a scripted lift. Action zero reproduces the open-loop grasp and lift, so the recipe runs on any hand with a fitted grip. The actor is an MLP (512-256-128) reading 66 numbers: joint positions and velocities, the tool pose in the palm frame, the finger joints and tool pose of a reference rollout at the same step, the previous action, and 1 − cos of the shaft with vertical. PPO (rsl_rl) runs on 3,072 copies of the frozen scene in MuJoCo-Warp through mjlab: 20 M steps take about 36 min on the workstation’s RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, about 10,000 steps/s.

The reward weights progress (300 × the per-step change in cosine) and alignment (100 × exp(−4(1 − cos)²)) most, then lift, contact and tracking terms, with penalties on drift, action rate, joint limits and pad force above 6 N; the reorientation terms and the tip-lost termination switch on at policy step 58 (recipe configs/recipes/b_liveA.yaml). Two policies, A for the lift and B for the turn, replaced a single policy whose rewards conflicted, and the live-A reset, in which a frozen A drives the first 40 steps of every B episode and those steps are masked from the update, closed the hand-off on 2026-06-10. On the real_v1 hand a script takes A’s place.

Results of the learned policies

  • On the earlier m05 hand, b33 holds the continuous hand-off at cos 0.891 on three loaded pads; warm-started draws of its recipe have a seed sd of 0.032 over 16 draws.
  • The deployed geometric plan descends from them: the anchor lerp that became the deployed controller was the policy’s set-point schedule, and the ±0.5 rad clip every screen inherited was the policy’s action budget.
  • They set the evaluation rules the bench work used: score the deterministic held cosine and never the reward, look at the rollout before explaining its numbers, count a hold only after the hold window, and evaluate as a distribution.

Reasons for deploying the derived controller

  1. Draw variance. From-scratch training on one design spans held cos −0.29 to 0.78 across seeds (m05, n = 5, sd 0.38), more than the differences between designs; a derived plan applies the same computation to every morphology.
  2. Ignored observations. Replaying another environment’s values over b33’s whole input leaves its held cosine at 0.900 against 0.895, so it is an open-loop residual indexed by step.
  3. Floor work. 69 % of b33’s alignment gain occurs while the shaft touches the floor; the first floor-free turn in the program was the raised-axis geometric plan of 2026-08-27.
  4. Contact-model dependence. A harder contact (solimp 0.985/0.999) keeps the grasp and collapses the turn (alignment 13 against 48), and domain randomisation did not widen the working band.
  5. New topology. Every policy through 2026-08-26 was trained on m05, with 117 mm fingers and a proximal-length slide; b33 reaches a peak cos of 0.019 on the CAD hand, and fresh real_v1 pipelines turned the shaft 0.9–4.0° until the open-loop probe found the rotational lock.
  6. Missing observations on the hardware. On 2026-08-29 the platform read nine joint positions at 6.95 Hz, with no object pose, contact or current, against the policy’s 66 inputs at 50 Hz. The bus reached 111 Hz and the tag tracker arrived after the plans were exported, and the deployed stream is write-only.
  7. Actuator stiffness. The scenes shipped with finger kp 30 against a bench fit of about 0.5, and every policy before 2026-09-16 trained on the stiff plant.
  8. Unclipped residuals. The D6 finetunes of 2026-09-17 command joints up to 384° beyond their range; clipping the residual at ±1 rad (2026-09-19) gave policies whose commands a plan can contain.

State on 2026-09-20

Policies are trained on the calibrated plant (kp 0.5) with the built palm plate, the screw-tip tool and the object pose observed. D6 from scratch at 60 M steps holds 64/64 at cos 0.933, and a 20 M grip finetune reaches 0.969 at 10.8 N (page). Sixteen warm-started jobs across D1–D7 with the residual clipped at ±1 rad hold 64/64 at the trained pose; with the spawn jittered the hold ranking runs from D5 at 57/64 to D7 at 35/64 (page). In the UR5e chain D5 and both D6 policies hold the turn and keep the tool through the handover and eight gait cycles, while the gait turns the screw −0.002 revolutions because the servo sits on its torque ceiling at the calibrated gain. Nothing learned has run on the hand; a bench controller that feeds a policy the tag pose at 50 Hz is still to be built.

Future work

The six items of the paper’s Discussion and the five of its Appendix D, each with its first measurement

Yang et al. (RA-L 2024; TEVC 2026) optimised the 37 hand-set parameters of a reconfigurable hand along hardware gradients of a force-closure fitness, and evolved three- to five-fingered designs with morphology-aware learning before building them. This platform differs in two ways: its morphology is motor-driven, so a design change is a 95 s gantry move, and its object of study is the sim-to-real error Δ(m) as a function over the design space.

  1. Automated reset. All 251 archived trials were staged by hand, and re-staging is the main variance (sd 0.011 within a session, ±5° between sessions). Fit a fixture that returns the cylinder to a repeatable pose (a V-block on the post with a stop at the shaft end), let the UR5e re-stage, and repeat stage 1 of the protocol; the fixture works if the between-session drift falls below ±5°.
  2. Servo torque and stiffness. The servo is the largest sim-to-real term on record: a 0.018° per load-unit spring and a protective-torque cutoff that trips on 42 % of middle-yaw runs. Raise protective_torque (a writable register; the 70 °C thermal cutoff stays), or fit a stiffer servo in the yaw positions only, and measure the free-air and loaded arrival tables (real_v1_bench_arrival.py) on one hand before and after.
  3. Servo speed and a residual policy on the bench. A free-air 20° step per joint read at 111 Hz gives the servo’s time constant, which was never measured. The six bench drops turn the shaft to a mean peak of +0.61 and then let go, which a residual policy that sees the tag pose could catch. Build the station loop that streams a residual clipped at ±1 rad with the tag pose as input, and compare one hand (D5 or D6) with its open-loop plan, ten trials each in one sitting.
  4. Fingertips and palm. The rolling turn favours a convex tip of 5–6 mm radius with 1–3 mm of deflection at load, and the gait a 13 mm radius, because the pad ring acts as a gear. Print tips of radius 5, 8 and 13 mm in two durometers and run the ten-trial protocol on two hands; a force-sensitive resistor or a barometric pad per tip gives the contact signal the transfer sessions lacked.
  5. Motorised proximal-phalange length. Add a seventh design coordinate (length 25–45 mm) to the Sobol funnel and run the same retention screen. If the retained designs do not separate on length, the joint is not worth a motor for this task.
  6. Design-conditioned policy. Train one actor and critic across per-environment randomised mounts with the six mount coordinates observed; MuJoCo-Warp already indexes model fields per world. Test first whether the behaviour changes when only the design vector changes, since b33 ignored its inputs.
  7. Local agreement (Appendix D i). Bench the within-family contrast already on the station (g12 at four clips, u0060 and u0100 at two), twenty trials in one sitting. If the bench does not reproduce an ordering within one family, an ordering across families means little.
  8. Sparse hardware queries (Appendix D ii). Choose the next hands to test where the retention screen and the bench-schedule replay disagree most, and keep the eight built hands as the held-out set. The one plant correction tried so far reproduced one hand’s level and left the ordering unchanged, because the missing physics is contact.
  9. Warm-started controllers (Appendix D iii). Move the gantries between trials with one policy loaded and count the trials on the new hand until its held rate matches a per-hand policy. In simulation a shared warm start cut the draw sd from 0.3–0.5 to 0.032 and transferred its source hand’s grip into every design.
  10. Task specialisation (Appendix D iv). Run the cylinder and a 90–120 mm ball on the compact layout and on the widest one, ten trials each, scored on retention; the basketball page gives the simulated prediction, and the pad friction on a real ball is unmeasured.
  11. Robustness against nominal performance (Appendix D v). Run the nominal leader and the robustness leader of one pass, ten trials each under perturbed staging (±3 mm along the tool, ±8° of yaw), scored on retention. If the robustness ranking predicts the bench and the nominal ranking does not, select on ensemble win in real_v1_select_budget.py.

Of the bi-level plan (Section III-B), Jsim(m) exists for one task and one controller, Jreal(m) for eight hands, and Δ(m) is the eight signed differences of Table IV; one iteration of its first step has run.
