Terms and metrics

{{GLOSSARY}}

Presliding displacement of an elastic tip

An elastic fingertip deflects before it slips. Under a tangential force \(Q\) below \(\mu N\), the Cattaneo–Mindlin solution for a sphere on a flat keeps a central disc of the patch stuck and lets an annulus at its edge slip, and the contact moves by

\[\delta(Q)=\frac{3\mu N(2-\nu)}{16\,G a}\Bigl[1-\Bigl(1-\frac{Q}{\mu N}\Bigr)^{2/3}\Bigr],\qquad k_t=\frac{8Ga}{2-\nu},\qquad k_\theta=\frac{16Ga^3}{3},\](1)

where \(G\) is the shear modulus, \(\nu\) Poisson’s ratio and \(a\) the contact radius; \(k_t\) is the initial tangential stiffness, the slope of \(\delta(Q)\) at \(Q=0\), and \(k_\theta\) Lubkin’s initial torsional stiffness. A reversed load retraces the curve at twice its scale from the turning point (the Masing rule), so a load cycle below slip closes a hysteresis loop whose area is the energy dissipated by the partial slip. At 1 N per pad on the bed (\(E\) = 10 MPa, \(\nu\) = 0.45, Hertz radius 0.775 mm) the tool moves 40.3 µm at half the slip force and recovers 38.0 µm when the force returns to zero.

The sphere pads and Drake store no tangential displacement. A sphere’s friction rows hold the tool through MuJoCo’s regularised friction (the contact overview’s step 6), so under a held load the tool creeps at a constant speed, keeps creeping while the force is positive and recovers nothing when the force is removed (Figure 2).

Pad spheres on a sprung skin

The skin moves each fingertip’s pad spheres onto a child body, joined to the fingertip by two slides tangent to the pad and a hinge about its normal (Figure 1). Each joint has the elastic contact’s initial stiffness at a reference force \(N_\text{ref}\) and critical damping:

\[a=\sqrt{A(N_\text{ref})/\pi},\qquad k_t=\frac{8Ga}{2-\nu},\qquad k_\theta=\frac{16Ga^3}{3},\qquad c=2\sqrt{k\,(m+m_a)},\](2)

with \(A\) the contact area at \(N_\text{ref}\), \(m\) the skin’s mass and \(m_a\) the armature on its joint. {{SKIN_SIZING}}

Armature makes the skin work in MuJoCo. A contact’s friction rows are softened in proportion to its inverse weight \(\hat\Lambda\), and a 2 g body has a large one: on the bare skin the tool slid 49 µm over the spheres during each force ramp. Armature on the skin’s joints lowers \(\hat\Lambda\) and leaves the static compliance unchanged, and a tool that sticks to a skin moves that armature whenever it moves relative to the fingertip. Held between the two bed skins, the 24.5 g tool moves 64.5 g along its axis. This is a 104 Hz mode (169 Hz on Mindlin’s springs alone) with a damping ratio of 0.8. On the hand each slide has 0.26 kg of armature, 11 times the tool’s mass. The dampers are critical for the skin’s own inertia, which is 40 times smaller than the tool’s about the pinch axis, so the held tool’s twist on the two hinges is a 4 Hz mode with a damping ratio of 0.2. At the bed’s 1 ms step the skin’s loop is a step error at any damping: 0.29 against 2.58 µJ at 0.025 ms with critical dampers, and −9.2 against +7.1 µJ at eight times critical.
