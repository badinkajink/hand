Ground-supported gaiting

On rv05_manual, an open-loop cycle of twist, release, return and regrasp turns a screwdriver standing on the floor {{TURNS}} revolutions about its axis in 40 cycles (SD {{TURNS_SD}}, 3 rollouts), and the shaft ends 0.5° from vertical after 0.3 mm of drift. With the floor removed after the grasp, no hand keeps the shaft (0 of 6 rollouts each). Five of the ten hand scenes cannot reach the gripping ring; rv04_mid cannot release it, so its return stroke undoes its twist. A 13 mm fingertip turns the shaft 52.6° per cycle, against 45.0° for the shipped tip.

Terms and metrics

Gait cycle
One twist, release, return and regrasp of the three pads; a rollout runs 8 cycles, an endurance run 40.
Ring
Circle of pad-centre targets around the standing shaft, thumb at 180° and the other two fingers at ±60°; 21.05 mm in radius when gripping, 6 mm wider when released.
Commanded interference
Depth of the gripping ring inside the shaft surface, in mm (2 mm unless stated).
Grip force
Summed normal force of the pads on the shaft at the start of the gait, in N.
Ring IK residual
Largest distance from a commanded pad position to the nearest one its finger reaches, in mm, on the gripping and the released ring.
Stroke and pad arc
Azimuth the ring advances per twist, in degrees (30° unless stated); the pad arc is the stroke times 21.05 mm.
Press
Distance the closed grip is driven down after the grasp, in mm; positive loads the shaft onto the floor, negative lifts it.
Rotation per cycle
Rotation of the shaft about its own axis per cycle, in degrees, mean over a rollout; accumulated rotation is the sum, in turns.
Slip-free ceiling
Rotation per cycle if the pads did not slip: the stroke times the gear ratio, the gripping-ring radius over the 12.5 mm shaft radius (1.684 for the shipped 10.55 mm pad, 50.5° for a 30° stroke).
Transmission
Rotation per cycle over the slip-free ceiling; 1 means no slip.
Held
Rollouts, of n spawn-jittered repeats, that end with the shaft less than 14° from vertical, touched by the hand and standing on the floor; in the air, the floor is removed once the grasp is established.
Final tilt
Angle of the shaft axis from vertical at the end of a rollout, in degrees.
Hand contact
Mean number of hand-shaft contacts during a phase of the cycle.
Hand tags
real_v1 designs from the design search, such as rv05_manual_stored; the suffixes _sp30 and _sp40 mark two stored scenes of one layout.

Gait on a standing shaft

Driving a screw needs unbounded rotation about the tool's own axis, which three fingers with about 12 mm of tangential stroke deliver only as a gait: advance the contacts, let go, return and regrasp. A 100 mm shaft of 12.5 mm radius standing on its end face stays up until it tilts past 14.0°, so the hand can let go of it. The gripping ring is commanded 2 mm inside the shaft surface because the position servos make grip force from the commanded-minus-actual error.

  1. 01TwistAll three azimuths advance by the stroke and carry the shaft.
  2. 02ReleaseThe pads retract radially by 6 mm; the shaft stands on the floor.
  3. 03ReturnThe azimuths run back to the start while retracted.
  4. 04RegraspThe pads close onto the ring again, on new material.

The joint targets are solved once into a table indexed by ring radius and azimuth and played back as a set-point list of the shape the bench runs, with no policy and no object sensing. The mounts are asymmetric about the palm origin, so the ring is offset 4 mm in +x, which brings every grip target within 0.7 mm; the pads approach radially at ring height, since a ramp from the open keyframe lifts the shaft 8 mm off the floor.

Floor removal across ten hand scenes

The floor is removed once the grasp is established, so grip, press and commands stay fixed and only the support during the release changes.

The table separates two failures. rv00_wide, rv01_compact and rv02_narrowx cannot reach the gripping ring (13 to 20 mm residual, grip force under 0.05 N) and never touch the shaft. rv04_mid reaches it to 1.78 mm and holds the shaft for 40 cycles at 34 N with a final tilt of 6°, but its released ring has a residual of 9.85 mm: the pads stay on the shaft through the return and drag it back almost as far as the twist advanced it, for 2.2° per cycle.

The ability to withdraw the pads at the end of the stroke is therefore a design criterion of the gait, beside the thumb's moment arm and the extension budget that decide the turn.

Rotation by gait phase

Rotation is integrated at the 500 Hz physics rate. A free shaft spun at 10 rad/s coasts 0.6° and stops within 12 ms, so nothing accumulates while the fingers are off the tool; the +5.1° that rv05_manual gains in the release phase at zero hand contact is the servos still completing the commanded twist.

Operating window of press, stroke and grip force

Press is a finger command, so a hand without a wrist can deliver it. The shaft is kept from −6 to +6 mm, with transmission varying by less than 15 %, and tips over at +8 mm, where the downward drag of the pads levers it off its footprint at a ground normal force of 0.58 N against its 0.24 N weight. At −6 mm the shaft is lifted only about 7 mm and falls back to the floor during the 0.12 s release.

Fingertip radius and the gear ratio

Without slip, a pad moving along the ring moves the shaft surface the same tangential distance, so the shaft turns by the stroke times the gear ratio. The ring radius is the shaft radius plus the pad radius minus the commanded interference, so the pad radius is the term of the ratio the hand's design sets. rv05_manual delivers 0.89 of the shipped pad's 50.5° ceiling.

A 13 mm pad turns the shaft 52.6° per cycle against 45.0° for the shipped pad, 17 % more with the same commands. The 2026-08-18 fingertip study of the learned reorientation on the earlier m05 hand found compact convex point contact best, with an optimum radius of 5 to 6 mm: the turn rolls the shaft between the pads and a larger patch resists the roll, while the gait drives the shaft tangentially with a lever arm that grows with the pad radius.

Limitations

  • The shaft starts upright under the hand; the chain from the turn into this gait ran 6 of 6 seeds on 2026-09-03 (carry-to-gait seam and countersink seating).
  • No gait plan has been exported or passed the trajectory-clearance and servo-limit gates.
  • The load is about 7 mN·m of rim friction on a flat floor. Loaded in a countersink (wrist-geometry page), the relay gait starts losing seeds at 8 mN·m.
  • Pad friction, shaft diameter and contact stiffness were held fixed; a 0.5 mm spawn jitter is the only randomisation. Five scenes of three layouts grip the ring.

Future work

  • Bench gait plan. Export a 40-cycle plan (about 90 s of bus time at 50 Hz) and pass it through scripts/real_v1_trajectory_clearance.py and the servo-limit check, including the family's clip ceiling of about 1.34 rad. The gait fails on the bench if the shaft turns less than one revolution in 8 cycles or a servo reaches its protective cutoff.
  • Release residual in the design screen. Compute the released-ring IK residual (one IK table, no rollout) for the eight deployed hands and run the premise arm of scripts/real_v1_gait_study.py on each; the criterion fails if the residual does not separate the hands that turn the shaft from those whose return undoes the twist.
  • One fingertip for both tasks. Sweep the pad radius from 5 to 16 mm (probe_real_v1_gait.py --pad-radius) on a hand that also runs the fixed-contact turn, scoring both; the trade holds if no radius keeps both tasks within 10 % of their optima.
  • Contact sensitivity. Vary pad friction, shaft diameter and contact stiffness in the premise arm over the ranges the turn tolerates; the gait is the more fragile task if it loses the shaft inside them.
