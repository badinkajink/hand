Problem and hypothesis

a pinch brake needs a contact area, and MuJoCo resolves contact at points

The paper’s load step turns the thumb–index pinch into a brake. The tool hangs between the two pads and turns about the line through them. Only friction spread over each contact area resists that turn: \(T=\mu\int_A p\,\rho\,dA\), with \(p\) the contact pressure and \(\rho\) the distance from the contact normal. A point contact has no area, so it transmits no torque about its normal. With point contact the pinched tool is a free hinge, in MuJoCo and in Drake alike; the point-contact films below drop it into a hang as soon as it leaves the posts. The paper uses Drake’s hydroelastic contact for this reason. The question here is whether MuJoCo can carry the same controller without it.

Drake’s compliant-hydroelastic fingertip carries a pressure field \(p=E\,\delta/R\), with \(\delta\) the depth below its surface. Drake evaluates the field on the rigid tool surface wherever the two overlap (Figure 1, left). For a sphere on a plane the integrals give the elastic-foundation laws \(F=\pi E\delta_0^2\) and \(T=\mu F\cdot\tfrac{8}{15}a\) with \(a=\sqrt{2R\delta_0}\), so the friction arm \(T/\mu F\) grows as \(F^{1/4}\) (derived step by step in the next section). Take the real_v1 fingertip (\(R\) = 10.55 mm) on the screwdriver (\(r\) = 12.5 mm) at Drake’s default \(E\) = 10 MPa. At 3 N the integrated field covers {{HI_AREA}} mm² and gives an arm of {{HI_ARM}} mm, against {{HI_RIG}} mm measured in Drake on the two-pad rig.

The hypothesis is that springs can replace the field. MuJoCo resolves each overlapping pair of geoms as one soft contact. At steady state that contact acts as a linear spring of stiffness \(1/\bigl(t_c^2(1-d_0)\Lambda\bigr)\), for solref \((t_c,1)\) and a constant solimp \(d_0\); \(\Lambda\) is MuJoCo’s inverse-inertia estimate for the contact. Cover the fingertip with small spheres and give each the foundation’s stiffness over its share of the pad, \(k_s=\frac{E}{R}A_s\bigl(\frac{R-r_s}{R}\bigr)^2\). The spring forces then sample \(\int p\,dA\) and their moments sample \(\int p\,\rho\,dA\) (Figure 1, right). Contact area, centre of pressure and pinch torque come out of ordinary point contacts, with no change to MuJoCo. The spacing has to be fine compared with the contact patch (Figure 2).

  • At 3 N: every spacing gives the field’s arm within {{HI_DEV_MAX}} %.
  • At 0.5 N, the force at which the brake sets the end of the swing: the 2 mm pad touches with only {{LO_N2}} spheres and its arm is {{LO_DEV2}} % long. The 1 and 0.5 mm pads stay within {{LO_DEV_FINE}} %.

A second, cheaper surrogate keeps a single contact point and supplies the torque directly: condim 4, with a torsional coefficient \(\mu_t=\mu\,\bar r(N)\) recomputed every step from the measured law (step 3 of the next section). Earlier results:

  • Two-pad rig: the rescheduled condim 4 matched Drake’s torque within 1 %; the 1 and 0.5 mm pads matched it within 7 and 3 %.
  • Hand load step: all three agreed with Drake within 3–9 %.

This page asks whether the surrogates carry a controller through a whole task with one code base driving Drake and MuJoCo, and what each costs. Figure 3 follows the thumb contact through the nominal chain in five of the models.

Friction torque of a soft pinch and the force that supplies it

a derivation from contact pressure to servo targets, with the numbers of the nominal trial

The brake needs two quantities at every 10 ms tick: the friction torque the pinch must transmit about its axis, and the pad force at which the pads transmit it. Steps 1 and 2 derive the torque a soft pad can carry and show that it grows faster than the normal force. Steps 3 and 4 show how each MuJoCo surrogate reproduces that law: condim 4 by rewriting its torsional coefficient every step, the sphere pad by geometry. Step 5 turns the swing’s equation of motion into a torque demand and inverts the law for the pad force. Step 6 turns the pad force into joint torques and servo targets. The numbers are for the nominal trial: fingertip radius \(R\) = 10.55 mm, \(E\) = 10 MPa, \(\mu\) = 1, and a 24.5 g tool pinched \(d\) = 15 mm from its centre of mass.

1Friction torque of a contact patch

A pad pressed with normal force \(F\) spreads it over a patch \(A\) as a pressure \(p\). When the tool turns about the contact normal relative to the pad, every area element slides on a circle about the patch centre, and its friction \(\mu p\,dA\) acts at lever \(\rho\):

\[F=\int_A p\,dA,\qquad T=\mu\int_A p\,\rho\,dA=\mu F\,\bar r,\qquad \bar r=\frac{\int_A p\,\rho\,dA}{\int_A p\,dA}.\](1)

\(T\) is the largest torque the contact carries before it spins; below it the contact sticks. The friction arm \(\bar r\) is the pressure-weighted mean distance from the patch centre. A point contact has \(\rho=0\) and transmits no torque, so a pinch between two point contacts is a free hinge.

2The hydroelastic fingertip: an arm that grows as \(F^{1/4}\)

Drake’s compliant-hydroelastic fingertip carries a pressure field that rises linearly with depth below its surface, \(p=E\,\delta/R\). In contact, Drake evaluates the field on the rigid tool’s surface inside the overlap. For a sphere pressed \(\delta_0\) into a flat face, the depth at distance \(\rho\) from the patch centre is \(\delta(\rho)\approx\delta_0-\rho^2/2R\) for \(\delta_0\ll R\). The patch is a disc of radius \(a=\sqrt{2R\delta_0}\), and

\[\begin{aligned} F&=\int_0^a \frac{E}{R}\Bigl(\delta_0-\frac{\rho^2}{2R}\Bigr)\,2\pi\rho\,d\rho=\pi E\,\delta_0^2,\\ T&=\mu\int_0^a \frac{E}{R}\Bigl(\delta_0-\frac{\rho^2}{2R}\Bigr)\,2\pi\rho^2\,d\rho=\frac{2\pi}{15}\,\frac{\mu E\,a^5}{R^2}=\mu F\cdot\frac{8}{15}\,a . \end{aligned}\](2)

Substituting \(\delta_0=\sqrt{F/\pi E}\) gives the law used throughout this page:

\[\bar r(F)=\frac{8}{15}\sqrt{2R}\,\Bigl(\frac{F}{\pi E}\Bigr)^{1/4}=c\,F^{1/4}.\](3)

A flat face gives \(c\) = 1.035 mm·N−1/4. On the 12.5 mm cylinder the patch is narrower across the tool axis, and a fit to Drake’s torque on the two-pad rig gives \(c\) = 0.996 mm·N−1/4 with exponent 0.2498. The arm grows with force because the patch does: \(\bar r\) = 0.84 mm at 0.5 N and 1.31 mm at 3 N. A pad therefore resists spin with \(T=\mu c\,F^{5/4}\), which is not proportional to \(F\).

3MuJoCo condim 4: a torsional coefficient rewritten every step

A MuJoCo contact with condim 4 carries a normal force \(N\), two friction forces \(f_1,f_2\) in the tangent plane and one torque \(\tau\) about the normal. With the elliptic cone used here they are bounded together:

\[\frac{f_1^2+f_2^2}{\mu^2}+\frac{\tau^2}{\mu_t^2}\le N^2 .\](4)

The torsional coefficient \(\mu_t\) is the second entry of the geom’s friction and has units of length. With no tangential load the contact carries up to \(\mu_t N\), so it reproduces the patch of (1) when \(\mu_t=\mu\bar r\). By (3), \(\bar r\) depends on \(N\), and MuJoCo stores one \(\mu_t\) per geom. A fixed value is therefore right at one force only. Set for the 3 N hold, it makes the brake 57 % too strong at the 0.5 N end of the swing, the ratio \((3/0.5)^{1/4}=1.57\) of the two arms. The plant therefore rewrites the coefficient before every 1 ms step from the normal force the solver returned on the step before:

\[\mu_t[k]=\mu\,c\,N[k-1]^{1/4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tau_\text{max}[k]=\mu_t[k]\,N[k]\approx\mu\,c\,N^{5/4}.\](5)

The code is one line in MjChainPlant.step: geom_friction[g, 1] = mu * c * max(N, 1e-3) ** 0.25. The one-step lag has no measurable effect. The command changes over 0.25 s ramps or in 10 ms brake ticks, so \(N\) moves by well under 1 % between steps. On the two-pad rig the rescheduled contact matched Drake’s torque within 1 %. The law is fitted to Drake, so it has to be refitted when \(E\), \(R\) or the tool radius changes. The contact still has one point, so its centre of pressure cannot move across the pad.

4Sphere pads: the same law from geometry

The sphere pad replaces the field with \(n\) spheres of radius \(r_s\) whose outer surface is the fingertip. Each sphere is an ordinary condim 3 contact that carries force only; the torque is the moment of the friction forces spread over the patch:

\[F\approx\sum_i N_i,\qquad T\approx\sum_i \rho_i\,f_{t,i}.\](6)

Each sphere gets the foundation’s stiffness over its share of the pad. The 45° cap has area \(2\pi R^2(1-\cos 45^\circ)\) = 205 mm², so each of the \(n\) spheres covers \(A_s\) = 205 mm²/\(n\). The centres lie on radius \(R-r_s\), which packs them \((R/(R-r_s))^2\) more densely on the tool than on the fingertip:

\[k_s=\frac{E}{R}\,A_s\Bigl(\frac{R-r_s}{R}\Bigr)^2 .\](7)

MuJoCo does not accept a stiffness directly. For a contact at rest with solref \((t_c,1)\), a constant solimp \(d_0\) and penetration \(x\), the solver returns the force \(f=a_\text{ref}/R_\text{reg}\), with reference acceleration \(a_\text{ref}=x/(d_0t_c^2)+2\dot x/(d_0t_c)\) and regularizer \(R_\text{reg}=\Lambda(1-d_0)/d_0\). So each contact is a spring and a damper,

\[k=\frac{1}{t_c^2\,(1-d_0)\,\Lambda},\qquad \frac{c}{k}=2t_c ,\](8)

where \(\Lambda\) is MuJoCo’s estimate of the contact’s inverse inertia, here the fingertip’s and the tool’s inverse weights, 47.2 kg−1. Matching Drake’s relaxation time \(t_r\) = 0.02 s fixes \(t_c=t_r/2\) = 0.01 s, and (8) then gives \(d_0=1-1/(t_c^2k_s\Lambda)\).

  • 1 mm pad: \(n\) = 205, \(k_s\) = 817 N/m and \(d_0\) = 0.741.
  • 0.5 mm pad: \(n\) = 819 and \(k_s\) = 205 N/m. It needs \(t_r\ge\) 0.03 s to keep \(d_0\) above 0.05.

Nothing is rescheduled. As \(N\) rises, more spheres touch, the patch spreads and \(\bar r\) grows with it, which is the comparison in Figure 2.

5The brake: the torque demand and the pad force that meets it

About the pinch axis the tool obeys

\[I_u\,\ddot\varphi=m g d\cos\varphi-\tau_f,\qquad I_u=I_\text{cg}+m d^2 ,\](9)

with \(\varphi\) measured from horizontal (90° hanging), \(\tau_f\) the friction torque of both pads opposing the swing, and \(I_u\) = 2.69×10−5 kg·m². The closed-loop brake takes a minimum-jerk reference \(\varphi_\text{ref}(t)\), 0 to 90° in 1.8 s, adds PD feedback for the acceleration it wants, and reads the required torque off (9):

\[\begin{aligned}\ddot\varphi_\text{des}&=\ddot\varphi_\text{ref}+k_d(\dot\varphi_\text{ref}-\dot\varphi)+k_p(\varphi_\text{ref}-\varphi),\\ \tau_\text{des}&=m g d\cos\varphi-I_u\,\ddot\varphi_\text{des},\end{aligned}\](10)

with \(k_p\) = 64 s−2 and \(k_d\) = 24 s−1. Gravity dominates the demand. The reference’s peak acceleration, 2.8 rad/s², takes 7.5×10−5 N·m, which is 2 % of the 3.6×10−3 N·m the weight exerts at horizontal.

The pads also carry the weight. The pinch axis is horizontal, so \(W=mg\) lies in both pads’ tangent planes and each pad carries \(W/2\) tangentially. Putting \(f_1^2+f_2^2=(W/2)^2\) and \(\mu_t=\mu\bar r(N)\) into (4) gives the torque the two pads can still carry together:

\[\tau_\text{cap}(N)=2\,\mu\,N\,\bar r(N)\sqrt{1-\Bigl(\frac{W}{2\mu N}\Bigr)^2}.\](11)

\(\tau_\text{cap}\) is zero at the slip limit \(N=W/2\mu\) = 0.12 N and rises monotonically above it, so bisection finds the pad force that meets the demand:

\[N^\ast=\min\bigl\{N\in[N_\text{floor},N_\text{max}]:\ \tau_\text{cap}(N)\ge\tau_\text{des}\bigr\}.\](12)

Holding the tool still (\(\ddot\varphi_\text{des}=0\)): at 0° the demand is 3.61×10−3 N·m and \(N^\ast\) = 1.61 N. At 45° it is 2.55×10−3 N·m and 1.22 N; at 60°, 0.93 N; at 80°, 6.3×10−4 N·m and 0.41 N; at 85°, 0.25 N. At 90° only the 0.12 N slip limit remains. These are the forces of the 10-01 load step, where Drake passed 45° at 1.26 N and 80° at 0.40 N.

The floor starts at twice the slip limit, 0.24 N, so the tool cannot slide out along its axis near vertical. It rises 50 % when the tool slides and decays while the tool stalls off vertical. \(N_\text{max}\) = 6 N keeps the yaw joint at 0.28 N·m, inside the servo’s 0.35 N·m. The same three moves carry over to other grasps. Write the object’s equation of motion about the direction the grasp leaves free. Compute the torque or force the contacts must supply. Then invert the contact law for the normal force.

6From pad force to joint torques and servo targets

A pad presses with force \(N\) along \(-n\), where \(n\) is the contact normal from the tool to the finger. At rest the finger’s joints must supply

\[\tau_\text{joint}=J^\top(-N\,n)+\tau_g ,\](13)

with \(J\) the 3×3 Jacobian of the pad’s contact point with respect to yaw, mcp and pip, and \(\tau_g\) the torque that holds the finger’s own weight (MuJoCo inverse dynamics at zero velocity). The servos are position servos, \(\tau=k_p(q_\text{cmd}-q)-k_v\dot q\), so at rest the command that produces (13) is

\[q_\text{cmd}=q_\text{touch}+\frac{J^\top(-N\,n)}{k_p}+\frac{\tau_g}{k_p}.\](14)

\(q_\text{touch}\) is the posture at which the pad just touches. It is recorded at the switch to force mode and corrected by the gap still open there, \(\Delta q=J^{+}(-\text{dist}\;n)\).

Thumb at the pinch posture: \(J^\top(-n)\) = (46.7, 12.1, 5.2) mN·m per newton for yaw, mcp and pip. At 4 N the yaw joint needs 0.19 N·m, a 0.37 rad offset at \(k_p\) = 0.5 N·m/rad; 6 N needs 0.28 N·m. The finger’s own weight needs \(\tau_g\) = (−0.6, 4.2, 1.9) mN·m. At the 0.12 N slip limit that is nearly three times the 1.5 mN·m the pad force puts on mcp. Without \(\tau_g\) the pads pressed 0.12–0.15 N less than commanded.

Controller and chain

the paper’s generalized contact frames and least squares, a pad force through the servos, a closed-loop brake

Each fingertip gets a generalized contact frame on the tool. For the fingertip sphere (centre \(c\), radius \(r_\text{tip}\)) against the tool cylinder (axis point \(p\), unit axis \(a\), radius \(r\)), every element has a closed form. Take the nearest point on the axis segment, \(s^\ast=\operatorname{clamp}\bigl((c-p)\cdot a,\,-h,\,h\bigr)\). Then

\[\begin{aligned} n&=\frac{c-p-s^\ast a}{\|c-p-s^\ast a\|}, &\qquad y&=\frac{(I-nn^\top)\,e_\text{flex}}{\|(I-nn^\top)\,e_\text{flex}\|},\\ p_\text{obj}&=p+s^\ast a+r\,n, & z&=n\times y,\\ p_\text{fing}&=c-r_\text{tip}\,n, && \end{aligned}\](15)

where \(n\), the gradient of the tool’s signed distance, is the frame’s x axis and points from the tool to the finger, and \(e_\text{flex}\) is the finger’s flexion axis. The relative contact twist is the fingertip’s witness point relative to the tool’s, written in \(E=[n\;y\;z]\). It is linear in the finger’s three joint rates \(u\):

\[\begin{aligned} \begin{bmatrix} v_\text{spin}\\ v_\text{roll}\end{bmatrix}&=E^\top\bigl(J_\omega u-\omega_o\bigr),\\ \begin{bmatrix} v_\text{sep}\\ v_\text{slide}\end{bmatrix}&=E^\top\bigl(J_v u-v_o(p_\text{obj})\bigr), \end{aligned}\](16)

with \(J_\omega,J_v\) the fingertip’s Jacobians at \(p_\text{fing}\), \((v_o,\omega_o)\) the tool’s twist and \(v_o(p)=v_o(c_o)+\omega_o\times(p-c_o)\) its velocity at a point. Stacked, with the linear rows divided by \(\ell\) = 20 mm so that 20 mm/s weighs like 1 rad/s, the six rows are \(A\,u+b\) (paper Eq. 1, with the sign set so that \(v_\text{sep}>0\) opens the gap). Pass the tool’s measured twist and the controller follows the tool. Pass a reference twist and the fingers move the tool, as in the tasks of the paper below. The joint-rate reference is the bounded least squares of the paper’s Eq. 2:

\[\begin{aligned} u^\ast=\arg\min_u\ &\sum_i w_i\bigl(v_{i,\text{ref}}-v_i(u)\bigr)^2+w_\text{damp}\|u\|^2\\ &+w_\text{h}\bigl\|k_h(q_\text{h}-q)-u\bigr\|^2\\ \text{s.t.}\ \ &q_\text{min}\le q+\Delta t\,u\le q_\text{max},\qquad |u|\le u_\text{max}, \end{aligned}\](17)

with \(w_\text{h}\ll w_\text{damp}\ll w_i\) (\(10^{-5}\), \(10^{-3}\) and 1), \(u_\text{max}\) = 2 rad/s, solved by bounded-variable least squares and integrated into the servo targets at 100 Hz, \(q_\text{ref}\leftarrow q_\text{ref}+\Delta t\,u^\ast\). For a sphere against a cylinder the frames of (15) are exact and stay smooth through contact, so the paper’s geodesic filter on the hydroelastic centre of pressure is left out. The controller runs on a kinematic MuJoCo copy of the scene, so the same code drives the MuJoCo and the Drake plant.

The chain has seven steps.

  • Pick: the palm descends with thumb and index 8 mm open. They close along their contact normals (\(v_\text{sep}\) = −15 mm/s, slide held at zero) and switch to force at contact. The pad force enters the servo targets through (14).
  • Lift: the palm rises 90 mm at F = 4 N.
  • Brake: the swing about the pinch axis runs open loop (4 → 0.5 N over 3 s) or closed loop. The closed-loop brake tracks a 1.8 s minimum-jerk profile to 90° through (10)–(12), with the nominal mass and \(\mu\). Its force floor starts at twice the slip limit \(W/2\mu\), falls while the tool stalls off vertical, and rises 50 % when the tool slides along its axis. It accepts the swing within 2.5°.
  • Hold firm: F rises to 3 N.
  • Transport and aim: the palm carries the tool’s lower end, aimed from its measured pose, 8 mm over the hole and corrects once.
  • Insert: the palm descends 30 mm at 15 mm/s.

The pinch line is the thumb–index mount line turned 45° about the palm normal, 50 mm below the mounts. There both fingers keep 0.55 rad of joint range from 4 mm squeeze to 12 mm open. The 2026-10-01 brake study’s line through the mount midpoint at 45 mm puts the index on its mcp and pip limits at contact, and the pinch cannot open there.

Object twist, pusher and wield tasks of the paper on D8

Exp 2, Exp 3 and the wield of arXiv 2609.25619 under the same controller; MuJoCo 1 mm sphere pads against condim 4, MuJoCo point contact and Drake hydroelastic

{{PAPER_LEAD}}

The setup for each task is as follows; every reference enters through (16) and (17).

  • Exp 2, object twist. The pick and lift are the chain’s; then the palm stays still. In (16) the tool’s reference twist replaces its measured twist, and both pinch fingers hold their separation and slide rows at zero: sticking contacts, roll and spin free. Six components are stepped in turn, + then − for 0.4 s each, at the paper’s 20 mm/s and 40 °/s: translation along the pinch axis, the vertical and the tool axis, and rotation about the same axes through the pinch midpoint.
  • Exp 3, pusher and tripod. The middle is D8’s only free finger. By inverse kinematics it reaches the top of the tool 35 mm past the pinch, on the side away from the centre of mass, over ±20° of rotation about the pinch axis. It misses the hanging tool by 13 mm or more. The pinch therefore takes the tool 10 mm from its centre of mass and the middle closes on the far end. The pinch then relaxes to 0.8 N per pad. By (11) that is a friction hinge of 1.5×10−3 N·m, below the 2.4×10−3 N·m of gravity, so the tool always leans on the pusher. The middle tracks the pinch velocity \(s\), the paper’s object twist \(\omega_o=s\,u\) about the pinch axis \(u\), through two ±10° cycles at 20 °/s. Gravity brings the end back when the middle retreats. Then the tripod: pinch force to 3 N with the middle in place, and the palm lifts 30 mm.
  • Wield. The tool stands in the chain’s peg hole on the table (1 mm clearance). It is gripped 15 mm above its centre of mass, as at the end of the chain. Each cycle has five steps. Thumb and index close to 2 N. A reference twist of 40 °/s about the tool axis for 0.5 s (20°) rolls the tool between the pads. The pinch releases and opens 6 mm along the normals. The fingers return to the open posture, and the cycle closes again. Six cycles.

Simulation cost

the nominal chain under the closed-loop brake, one core, {{REP_RANGE}} repeats per model

Physics is the time spent inside the simulator’s step, in ms of one core per simulated second. One simulated second is 1000 steps of 1 ms, so 10.5 ms per second is 10.5 µs per step. The scene is the whole task: 19 degrees of freedom (hand, four-axis palm stage, free tool) and 114 collision geometries before the pads, 96 of them the peg block. The controller is the same code in every model: state read-out, mirror kinematics, contact frames, least squares and brake. It costs {{CTRL_MJ}} ms per simulated second against MuJoCo and {{CTRL_DK}} ms against Drake, whose state read-out is slower. Repeats agree within {{REP_SPREAD}} %.

  • Against condim 4: the 1 mm sphere pad costs {{X_S1_P4S}}× as much.
  • Against Drake hydroelastic at its default 1 mm mesh: the 1 mm pad is {{X_DHY_S1}}× cheaper and the 0.5 mm pad {{X_DHY_S05}}× cheaper.
  • What scales the cost: a sphere pad’s cost follows its spheres in contact, {{NC_S1}} in the hold for the 1 mm pad and {{NC_S05}} for the 0.5 mm pad. Drake’s follows its contact-surface faces, {{NC_DHY}} in the hold at 1 mm, and roughly doubles each time the mesh halves.
  • Stage: the stages at full pinch force (lift at 4 N; hold, transport and insert at 3 N) cost two to three times the brake. In the brake the force falls below 1 N and the patch shrinks.
