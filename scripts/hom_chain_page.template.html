Controller and chain

the paper’s generalized contact frames and least squares, a pad force through the servos, a closed-loop brake

Each fingertip gets a generalized contact frame on the tool: origin at the witness points, x along the tool’s signed-distance gradient, y the finger’s flexion axis projected on the tangent plane. The relative contact twist (spin, two roll, separation, two slide components; paper Eq. 1) is linear in the finger’s three joint rates. The joint-rate reference is the bounded least squares of Eq. 2, with damping and healthy-pose terms, integrated into the servo targets at 100 Hz. For a sphere against a cylinder the frames are exact in closed form and stay smooth through contact, so the paper’s geodesic filter on the hydroelastic centre of pressure is left out. The controller runs on a kinematic MuJoCo copy of the scene, so the same code drives the MuJoCo and the Drake plant.

The chain has seven steps.

  • Pick: the palm descends with thumb and index 8 mm open. They close along their contact normals (vsep = −15 mm/s, slide held at zero) and switch to force at contact. Pad force F enters as q = qtouch + JT(−F n)/kp + τg/kp, with τg the fingers’ own weight.
  • Lift: the palm rises 90 mm at F = 4 N.
  • Brake: the swing about the pinch axis runs open loop (4 → 0.5 N over 3 s) or closed loop. The closed-loop brake tracks a 1.8 s minimum-jerk profile to 90°. It inverts Iφ̈ = mgd cosφ − τf(N) through the rig’s brake law, with nominal mass and μ. Its force floor starts at twice the slip limit W/2μ, falls while the tool stalls off vertical, and rises 50 % when the tool slides along its axis. It accepts the swing within 2.5°.
  • Hold firm: F rises to {{F_HOLD}} N.
  • Transport and aim: the palm carries the tool’s lower end, aimed from its measured pose, 8 mm over the hole and corrects once.
  • Insert: the palm descends 30 mm at 15 mm/s.

The pinch line is the thumb–index mount line turned 45° about the palm normal, 50 mm below the mounts. There both fingers keep 0.55 rad of joint range from 4 mm squeeze to 12 mm open. The 2026-10-01 brake study’s line through the mount midpoint at 45 mm puts the index on its mcp and pip limits at contact, and the pinch cannot open there.

Experiment 1: contact-free tracking

the index steps each relative-velocity component at 40 °/s or 20 mm/s while the other five are held at zero

{{EXP1_TABLE}}

With all six components weighted equally, as in the paper’s protocol, a three-joint finger cannot follow a pure step. It translates its tip only by turning it, so the least squares trades each commanded component against the five held at zero. The gain is the median ratio of achieved to commanded on the stepped component. {{EXP1_PROSE}} The paper reports RMSE for five hands in a figure without stating the values in its text.

What this does not settle

  1. The pick under perturbation. Eight of ten perturbed picks fail before the brake. The next step is a per-seed trace of the close phase in hom_chain.run_chain: tip-to-surface distance, joint margins and tool displacement on the posts. Candidate fixes are position feedback on the slide references (the closing tilts the pinch line 1.3°) and a V-cradle instead of flat posts. Re-run seeds 1–10. The brake comparison counts only once the pick holds on at least 9 of 10 seeds.
  2. The closed-loop brake’s drop on seed 9. With a 9 % heavier tool and μ 1.17 the closed-loop brake lost the tool mid-swing in both MuJoCo models, where the open-loop schedule completed the chain. The brake inverts the law with the nominal mass, and its force floor of 2 W/2μ is set from it. That trace (hom_chain.py run --spec mj:point4s --seed 9) is the first to read: floor, commanded force and axial slip against time.
  3. Brake reliability. With picks fixed, run 20 seeds per brake in condim 4 and the sphere model, and 10 in Drake hydroelastic (about 5 min). Report the final-angle spread and drops. The open-loop schedule should be tuned on the nominal trial first, since the paper’s operator ended the force reduction by eye.
  4. Out-of-plane tilt. In MuJoCo the hanging tool leans 1.5–2.3° out of the swing plane, following the pinch line’s tilt; in Drake about 1°. A hold-phase correction moving the two tips about 0.8 mm vertically in opposite directions is written up in the session notes but not run.
  5. The three-pad hold. “Hold firm again” is a two-pad squeeze here. D8’s deploy-plan grasp, where the middle releases and re-closes on the hanging shaft (the 2026-09-16 swing), is the follow-up chain.
  6. The bench. The closed-loop brake reads the swing angle at 100 Hz from the tool pose. The AprilTag tracker’s rate and latency, and the pad modulus behind E = 10 MPa, are unmeasured.
