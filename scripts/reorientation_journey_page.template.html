Screwdriver reorientation across eight deployed hands

In {{N_ROLLOUTS}} simulated rollouts over {{N_SWEEPS}} sweeps on the eight deployed hands, D6 completed the whole chain open loop (grasp the lying screwdriver, lift it, turn it tip-down, seat it in a countersink and gait it) on 3 of 4 seeds at one setting: pivot 0.15, travel budget 0.50 rad. Of the 90° turn the fingers delivered 39.5° and the arm the rest. The pivot height chose which end of the tool went down (cos −0.988 at 0.05, +0.994 at 0.25), and the deployment grasp fitter left 0.44–1.26 mm of finger extension against the reference grasp’s 2.74 mm.

Simulation on the shipped servo model (kp 30 N·m/rad), no hardware run · scored on the signed cosine with a load test at the tool’s weight, 0.240 N

Terms and metrics

The task on a simulated UR5e: close on the screwdriver lying on the table, lift it, turn it tip-down in the air, set it into a countersink and gait it (8 cycles of re-grasps that rotate it about its own axis).

Three scoring defects and their replacement

Each produced a result that was reported and then withdrawn

1. The tilt angle folds an upside-down tool onto an upright one

tilt_deg is arccos|cos| when the tool has no modelled tip, so a screwdriver standing on its handle and one standing on its tip both read 0.00°. 952 of 967 scored stands on 2026-09-06 were upside down. Every result is now scored on the signed cosine.

2. Table-mode stands release the tool

In table mode the maneuver stands the tool up and lets go of it, so held_reorient in that mode measures a released tool. Of 177 recorded stands, 116 had zero pads on the tool and the median pad force was 0.000 N.

3. The success flag read the wrong checkpoint

reorient_ok reads the reoriented checkpoint. On the hand that later completed the chain the rotation is unfinished there: cos +0.568 at reoriented and +0.995 two checkpoints later. The completed chain was in the first pivot sweep of 2026-09-06 and was scored as a failure for the rest of that day.

Current scoring

Every rollout is scored at every checkpoint on the signed cosine, the pad count, the pad force against the tool’s 0.240 N weight and the height; a held turn needs two pads carrying the weight, floor-free. slide_mm shows whether the grasp kept its geometry, and of all the fields it agrees best with the films.

Pivot height and the end of the tool that goes down

The turn rotates the fingers about an axis at a fraction axis_k of the half-straddle above the contact plane. The fraction selects which end of the tool ends up pointing down before it changes how far the tool turns: the same hand reaches cos −0.988 at 0.05 and +0.994 at 0.25.

Finger extension left by the grasp fitter

A fixed-contact turn uses finger extension: the fingers reach further as the tool rotates under them. The stored CEM grasp closes at 62.6–65.4 mm of the 68.11 mm chain, leaving 2.74 mm. The deployment fitter _grip_from_fit closes at 66.0–67.7 mm, leaving 0.44–1.26 mm, at every squeeze from 2 to 12 mm, and no squeeze completes the chain.

Grip force is sufficient: the 4 mm cell lifts on three pads at 8.79 N, more than the reference, and the tool is on the floor at the next checkpoint. The fitter’s palm-height search is clamped to [depth − 8 mm, depth]; asked for 62, 58, 54, 50 and 46 mm on the reference hand it returns no pose at any of them, so its only feasible depth is the deepest.

Withdrawn

Two claims made during this work were wrong. Grip force at the lift was reported to predict the drop at Spearman −0.312 pooled; within each hand |ρ| is 0.03–0.36 with inconsistent sign, so the pooled value reflects which hand it is. And roll_deg was taken to show that the fingers were not holding the tool; it is measured against the palm, which is fixed through the turn, so it reads about 90° on a working rollout too.

Future work

  1. Turn made by the fingers alone. Rerun D6 at pivot 0.15 with transport_iters=1 and the staging rotation disabled, so that the arm cannot contribute, and read the cosine at staged. If it stays near +0.568, the finger-only reorientation is unsolved and the chain result is an arm result.
  2. Fitter as the cause of the seven failing hands. Add an extension-headroom constraint to _grip_from_fit (reject a pose leaving under 2.5 mm of the 68.11 mm chain) and widen its palm-height search past the [depth − 8 mm, depth] clamp. Accept it if a headroom-constrained fit reproduces the reference chain under tests/test_chain_reference.py, then rerun the pivot sweep.
  3. Width of D6’s pivot window. Sweep 0.12–0.20 in steps of 0.01 with eight seeds (D6 is sampled densely only at 0.15) and read the width of the band that completes.
  4. Pivot from geometry. Test the eight measured best pivots against the thumb moment arm and the grip depth; if neither correlates, the pivot must be screened per hand.
  5. The handover. The chain funnel of the earlier set-A run was 122 lifts, 102 held turns, 91 stands and 43 grip_ok. No test here addresses the 91→43 loss; D6 passes it.
  6. Pivot of the deployed plans. Measure the pivot for the hardware maneuver before re-flashing any plan to the CB1; the plans there keep their bench values, which are wrong for the chain.
