Screwdriver reorientation across eight deployed hands

{{N_ROLLOUTS}} simulated rollouts over {{N_SWEEPS}} sweeps, on the eight hardware-provenanced hands, asking one question: can a three-finger hand take a screwdriver from horizontal to vertical while holding it, and then seat and drive it. Four distinct configurations completed the whole chain, three of them the same hand at the same pivot height on different seeds. This is the record of what was tried, what each attempt cost, and what it eliminated.

Simulation only, no hardware run · scored on the signed cosine with a load test at the tool’s own 0.240 N · every number here is read back out of the sweeps’ own JSON by the builder named in the footer

The task, and the one configuration that has ever done it

results/phase1/real_v1/rv05_manual_stored · 169/169 on 2026-09-03

The chain is five moves: close on a screwdriver lying on the table, lift it, turn it tip-down in the air, set it into a countersink, and gait it round. Thirteen instrumented seams, and at each one the tool’s orientation, its pose in the palm frame, and how many pads are on it under how much load.

One configuration runs it end to end. It is not one of the eight deployed hands: it is rv05_manual_stored, on spherical pads, driven from a stored CEM grasp rather than the deployment fitter, on a floating palm.

It is pinned by tests/test_chain_reference.py, which asserts cos above 0.90 at reoriented, at least two pads carrying at least 0.240 N, floor-free, and 8 of 8 gait cycles. That test exists because on 2026-09-06 the reference would not execute at all — GantryPalm had no worst_pos, which the chain reads unconditionally on the floating-palm path, so the whole path had been raising AttributeError unnoticed since the arm work began.

The measurement was wrong three times

each of these produced a result that was reported and then withdrawn

Before any of the physics is worth reading, three scoring defects have to be named, because each one generated a positive result that did not exist.

1. The tilt angle folds a tool that is upside down onto one that is upright

tilt_deg is computed as arccos|cos| when the tool has no modelled tip, so a screwdriver standing on its handle and one standing on its tip both read 0.00°. 952 of 967 scored “stands” on 2026-09-06 were upside down. Everything is now scored on the signed cosine, where +1 is tip down and −1 is handle down.

2. A stand is not a hold

In table mode the maneuver stands the tool up and lets go of it by construction, so held_reorient in that mode is a measurement of a released tool. Of 177 recorded stands, 116 had zero pads on the tool and a median pad force of 0.000 N. The angle those runs reported is the angle of a tool the hand had already let go of.

3. The success flag read the wrong seam

reorient_ok reads the reoriented seam. On the hand that eventually completed the chain the rotation is not finished there — it reads +0.568 at reoriented and +0.995 two seams later. The completed chain was sitting in the first pivot sweep of 2026-09-06 and was scored as a failure for the rest of the day.

What replaced them

Every rollout is now scored on four fields at every seam: the signed cosine, the pad count, the pad force against the tool’s 0.240 N weight, and the height. A held turn requires two pads at or above the weight, floor-free. slide_mm — the tool’s position in the palm frame relative to closure — is what says whether the grasp kept its geometry, and it is the field that finally agreed with what the videos showed.

The pivot height decides which end goes down

scripts/real_v1_chain_ablate.py · open-loop rollouts only

The turn rotates the fingers about an axis a fixed fraction axis_k of a half-straddle above the contact plane. That fraction selects which end of the tool ends up pointing down, and it does that before it affects how far the tool turns: the same hand at 0.05 reaches cos −0.988 and at 0.25 reaches +0.994.

{{CHART_PIVOT}}

The eight deployed plans carry axis_k between 0.05 and 0.15, inherited from a bench screen that measured a different maneuver. Nothing in the deployment path had ever checked that a pivot height transfers between maneuvers, and it does not: for rv05_manual_b85, 0.05 is right for the free carry and 0.25 for the chain.

The grasp fitter spends the travel the turn is paid for in

closed-grip mount-to-pad distance against the 68.11 mm finger chain

A fixed-contact turn is paid for in finger extension: the fingers have to reach further as the tool rotates under them. The stored CEM grasp closes at 62.6–65.4 mm of a 68.11 mm chain, leaving 2.74 mm. The deployment fitter _grip_from_fit closes at 66.0–67.7 mm, leaving 0.44–1.26 mm, at every squeeze value from 2 to 12 mm. No squeeze value chains.

Grip force is not the missing quantity: the 4 mm cell lifts on three pads at 8.79 N — more than the reference — and is on the floor at the next seam. The fitter's palm-height search is clamped to [depth − 8 mm, depth], and asked for 62, 58, 54, 50 and 46 mm on the reference hand it returns no pose at any of them. Its only feasible depth is the deepest one, which is the problem.

Two closed loops, both measured, both worse

privileged simulator state, no sim-to-real constraint

The brief was explicit that closed-loop feedback and privileged information were allowed. Two loops were built and swept. Both make the turn worse at a pivot height that already works.

The force loop

Two implementation defects turned up first, and both are real bugs independent of the result. The regulator existed in chain() but the sweep tool exposed only the servo-load proxy, so every chain sweep ever run had been open loop. And _force_step slewed the trim at a hardcoded ±0.0006 rad per tick, which gain × error saturates at any realistic error — 0.066 rad of total correction available over a 550-step turn, against an authority band of 0.45. Fixing both made the loop reachable and effective, and then:

Withdrawn

Two claims made during this work were wrong and are not used above. Grip force at the lift was reported to predict the drop at Spearman −0.312 pooled; within each hand the relationship vanishes (|ρ| 0.03–0.36, inconsistent sign), so the pooled figure is carried by which hand, not by how hard it clamps. And roll_deg was read as evidence the fingers were not carrying the tool; it is measured against the palm, which is fixed through the turn, so it reads about 90° on a working rollout too.

The caveat, stated with the result

The fingers take the tool from 94.0° to 54.5°. The arm takes it from 54.5° to 6.0°, by an iterated rigid palm transfer against the measured object pose — the hand and tool rotate together and the fingers do not move. This is a held reorientation of the whole assembly, not a finger-only in-hand reorientation. Of the 90° asked for, the fingers deliver 39.5°.

What this does not settle

named measurements, with what would falsify each

  1. Whether the fingers can deliver more than 39.5°. Rerun D6 at pivot 0.15 with transport_iters=1 and the staging rotation disabled, so the arm cannot contribute, and read the cosine at staged. If it stays near +0.568 the finger-only reorientation is unsolved and the chain result is an arm result.
  2. Whether the fitter is the reason seven hands fail. Add an extension-headroom constraint to _grip_from_fit — reject a pose leaving under 2.5 mm of the 68.11 mm chain — and widen its palm-height search past the [depth − 8 mm, depth] clamp. Accept if a headroom-constrained fit reproduces the reference chain under tests/test_chain_reference.py. Then re-run the pivot sweep. Not before: the 432-rollout budget band already gave 0 chains against a fit with no travel left.
  3. Whether pivot 0.15 is D6’s optimum or the edge of a window. The grid is 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.25 / 0.35 and D6 is only sampled densely at 0.15. Sweep 0.12–0.20 in steps of 0.01, eight seeds, and read the width of the band that chains.
  4. Whether the per-hand pivot is predictable from geometry. Eight hands, each with a measured best pivot, is enough to test against thumb moment arm and grip depth, and not enough to fit anything. If neither correlates, the pivot has to be screened per hand and that cost belongs in the deployment budget.
  5. The handover. The chain funnel on the earlier set A run was 122 lifts, 102 held turns, 91 stands, and 43 grip_ok. That 91→43 loss is not touched by anything in this page; D6 passes it, and no test has isolated why.

None of this has been run on hardware. The deployed plans keep their bench pivot values, which this page shows are wrong for the chain; nothing should be re-flashed to the CB1 until the pivot is measured for the maneuver the hardware will actually run.
