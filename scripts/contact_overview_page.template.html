Components of a fingertip contact model

The SR2 hand pinches, turns, rolls and releases a 24.5 g screwdriver between two or three soft fingertips. Four contact quantities govern these motions: the normal force with the pad compliance behind it, Coulomb friction in the tangent plane, the friction torque about the contact normal, and the pressure distribution over the contact area, which sets that torque and the centre of pressure. The pinch brake and the wield of arXiv 2609.25619 depend on the friction torque.

The fingertip is a printed TPU block, 17.0 mm deep with a 14.8 mm wide palmar face and 2.7 mm fillets, and the planned prints have a gyroid infill; since 13 October the scenes model it as 1 mm pads. Its modulus, damping, shear compliance and friction are unmeasured; finite elements of the planned prints are on the printed-tip page (index below). The model is therefore judged by agreement with pressure-field references and analytic limits on task-level metrics, by consistency across step size and resolution, and by cost. A receding-horizon controller corrects a consistent model error from measured state, provided the model contains the friction torque and slip the task uses.

The task scenes with point-contact pads use MuJoCo’s elliptic friction cone, impratio 10 and a soft contact on pads, tool and floor (solref 0.006, solimp 0.97–0.995). On m05, the earlier simulation-only hand, a policy retrained with harder contact (solimp 0.985–0.999) kept the grasp and lost the turn, at an alignment reward of 13 against 48 (two finger arrangements).

Contact mechanics of a soft fingertip

1Friction torque of a contact patch

A pad pressed with normal force \(F\) spreads it over a patch \(A\) as a pressure \(p\). When the tool turns about the contact normal relative to the pad, each area element slides on a circle about the patch centre, and its friction \(\mu p\,dA\) acts at lever \(\rho\):

\[F=\int_A p\,dA,\qquad T=\mu\int_A p\,\rho\,dA=\mu F\,\bar r,\qquad \bar r=\frac{\int_A p\,\rho\,dA}{\int_A p\,dA}.\](1)

\(T\) is the largest torque the contact transmits before it spins. The friction arm \(\bar r\) is the force-weighted mean distance of the patch from its centre. Friction on opposite sides of the centre turns the tool the same way, so a patch whose centre of pressure lies at its centre still has an arm. For a disc of radius \(a\) under a uniform pressure \(p\), rings of area \(2\pi\rho\,d\rho\) give

\[F=\int_0^a p\,2\pi\rho\,d\rho=p\,\pi a^2,\qquad T=\mu\int_0^a p\,\rho\,2\pi\rho\,d\rho=\mu p\,\frac{2\pi a^3}{3},\qquad \bar r=\frac{2a}{3}.\]

The patch radius scales every lever and the shape of the pressure sets the weights. With \(u=\rho/a\), a cone of pressure \(1-u\) gives \(\bar r/a=1/2\); the paraboloid \(1-u^2\) of a spring bed (step 2) gives \(8/15=0.533\), the Hertz profile \(\sqrt{1-u^2}\) (step 3) \(3\pi/16=0.589\), a uniform pressure \(2/3\) and a ring at the rim 1.

A point contact has \(\rho=0\) and transmits no torque about its normal, so two point contacts pinching a tool form a free hinge about the line through them. The bed’s screwdriver, with its centre of mass \(d\) = {{HINGE_D}} mm from that line, exerts a gravity torque \(mgd\cos\varphi\) about it, with \(mgd\) = {{HINGE_MGD}} mN m and \(\varphi\) the tool’s angle below horizontal. Two pads hold it at rest where

\[2\mu F\,\bar r(F)\ \ge\ mgd\cos\varphi .\]

At 1 N per pad the arm of step 2 on the screwdriver, {{HINGE_ARM1}} mm, gives a capacity of {{HINGE_CAP1}} mN m with \(\mu=1\), so the tool comes to rest at \(\varphi\) = {{HINGE_PHI1}}°; it stays horizontal from {{HINGE_FH}} N per pad. At {{HINGE_N1}} N, the end of the braked swing of bed task 5, the pads still transmit {{HINGE_CAPN1}} mN m and the balance gives {{HINGE_PHIN1}}°. Point contacts hold the tool only hanging, at 90°.

2Winkler foundation and the hydroelastic pressure field

A Winkler foundation is a bed of independent springs, each pushing back in proportion to its own compression: \(p=k_w\delta\), with \(k_w=E/h\) in N/m³ for a layer of modulus \(E\) and thickness \(h\). Drake’s hydroelastic contact (Elandt et al., 2019) is a foundation of this kind: each compliant body has a pressure field that rises with depth below its surface, and the pressure at a point of the contact surface depends only on that point’s depth inside the undeformed body. For a sphere of radius \(R\) the field is \(p=E\,\delta/R\), a Winkler layer with \(h=R\). The sphere’s surface at distance \(\rho\) from its lowest point lies \(R-\sqrt{R^2-\rho^2}\approx\rho^2/2R\) higher, so a sphere pressed \(\delta_0\) into a flat face compresses the spring at \(\rho\) by \(\delta_0-\rho^2/2R\), and the patch ends at \(a=\sqrt{2R\delta_0}\). Summed over rings, with \(a^2=2R\delta_0\),

\[F=2\pi k_w\int_0^a\Bigl(\delta_0-\frac{\rho^2}{2R}\Bigr)\rho\,d\rho=2\pi k_w\Bigl(\frac{\delta_0a^2}{2}-\frac{a^4}{8R}\Bigr)=\pi k_wR\,\delta_0^2 .\](2)

The force grows as \(\delta_0^2\) because the patch area \(2\pi R\delta_0\) and the mean pressure \(k_w\delta_0/2\) each grow in proportion to \(\delta_0\). The pressure \(k_w\delta_0(1-\rho^2/a^2)\) is the paraboloid of step 1, so \(\bar r=8a/15\), and eliminating \(\delta_0\) with \(a^4=4R^2\delta_0^2=4RF/(\pi k_w)\) gives the arm as a power of the load:

\[\bar r_W=\frac{8}{15}\Bigl(\frac{4R}{\pi k_w}\Bigr)^{1/4}F^{1/4}=c\,F^{1/4},\qquad T=\mu c\,F^{5/4}.\](3)

For the real_v1 fingertip at Drake’s default \(E\) = 10 MPa, \(k_w=E/R\) = {{KW}} N/mm³: 1 N sinks the sphere {{BED_D0_UM}} µm into a flat face over a patch of radius {{BED_A_MM}} mm, with a peak pressure of {{BED_P0}} MPa and a contact stiffness \(dF/d\delta_0=2\pi k_wR\delta_0\) of {{BED_KC}} N/mm, and \(c={{C_FLAT}}\ \mathrm{mm\,N^{-1/4}}\). On the 12.5 mm screwdriver the patch is narrower across the tool axis. Integrating the same field over the sphere–cylinder overlap (scripts/hydroelastic_arm_integral.py) gives an arm of 0.820, 0.974 and 1.281 mm at 0.5, 1 and 3 N, \(c=0.974\ \mathrm{mm\,N^{-1/4}}\) with the exponent 1/4 to within 0.1 %. This integral is the reference of the twist task; Drake and the sphere pads are two discretizations of it. The springs are uncoupled, so the pressure is zero outside the geometric overlap.

3Hertz contact

A homogeneous elastic body transmits load sideways through shear, so its surface deflects outside the loaded region and the pressure takes the Hertz form. For a sphere on a rigid flat, with \(E^\ast=E/(1-\nu^2)\),

\[a^3=\frac{3FR}{4E^\ast},\qquad p(\rho)=\frac{3F}{2\pi a^2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho^2}{a^2}},\qquad \bar r_H=\frac{3\pi}{16}\,a\;\propto\;F^{1/3}.\](4)

At the same approach an elastic solid touches over a smaller patch, \(a^2=R\delta\) against the bed’s \(2R\delta\), and its mean pressure grows with the strain \(a/R\), so \(F\propto\delta^{3/2}\) and \(a\propto F^{1/3}\). Drake’s 10 MPa is a parameter of its pressure field, \(k_wR\); taken as a Young’s modulus with \(\nu=0.45\) (\(E^\ast\) = {{HZ_ESTAR}} MPa), (4) gives \(a\) = {{HZ_A}} mm and \(\bar r\) = {{HZ_RBAR}} mm at 1 N, half the bed’s {{C_FLAT}} mm. The two laws differ in how fast the arm grows with load. Between 0.5 and 3 N:

\[\frac{\bar r(3\,\mathrm{N})}{\bar r(0.5\,\mathrm{N})}=6^{1/4}=1.565\ \text{(foundation)},\qquad 6^{1/3}=1.817\ \text{(Hertz)}.\](5)

The modulus enters only the prefactor \(c\) of \(\bar r=c\,F^{\gamma}\), with \(\gamma\) = 1/4 on a foundation and 1/3 under Hertz, so the ratio \(\bar r(F_2)/\bar r(F_1)=(F_2/F_1)^{\gamma}\) does not depend on it. A bench measures torque, \(T=\mu F\bar r\propto F^{1+\gamma}\), which grows {{T_RATIO_BED}} times from 0.5 to 3 N on a bed and {{T_RATIO_HZ}} times under Hertz; dividing the spin torque by the pull force at slip under the same pinch, \(\bar r=T/F_\text{slip}\) for two pads, removes the friction coefficient. A tip whose arm grows as \(F^{0.15}\), inside the range of exponents from 0 to 1/3 that Xydas and Kao measured on soft fingertips, gives a ratio of {{RATIO_015}}, which neither law fits. The test assumes a spherical tip. On the printed TPU block the screwdriver presses a line across a flat face, and in finite elements of the planned prints the arm grows only {{FE_ARM_RATIO}} times from 0.5 to 3 N.

4Lateral coupling: the Pasternak foundation

A Pasternak foundation adds a shear layer that couples neighbouring springs,

\[p=k_w\,\delta-G\,\nabla^2\delta,\qquad \ell=\sqrt{G/k_w},\](6)

and load spreads a coupling length \(\ell\) beyond the overlap. A foundation suffices for \(\ell\ll a\); a coupled foundation is needed for \(\ell\sim a\), and a shell or modal model for \(\ell\gg a\). {{CSLC_MECH}} The asides page describes how \(\ell\) would be measured on the printed tip ({{ASIDES_REF}}).

Sphere-packed pads in MuJoCo

5Sphere discretization of the foundation

The pad replaces the field with \(n\) spheres of radius \(r_s\) whose outer envelope is the fingertip. Each sphere is an ordinary condim 3 MuJoCo contact, and the pad’s force and torque are sums over the spheres in contact:

\[F\approx\sum_i N_i,\qquad T\approx\sum_i \rho_i\,f_{t,i},\qquad \lVert f_{t,i}\rVert\le\mu N_i .\](7)

Each sphere gets the foundation’s stiffness over its share \(A_s\) of the pad. A sphere meets the tool under its centre, and the centres lie on radius \(R-r_s\), so the lattice projected onto the tool shrinks by \((R-r_s)/R\) in each direction and its contact points are \((R/(R-r_s))^2\) = {{PAD_DENS}} times as dense as on the fingertip surface:

\[k_s=\frac{E}{R}\,A_s\Bigl(\frac{R-r_s}{R}\Bigr)^2 .\](8)

The same geometry makes the pad a bed of curvature radius \(R-r_s\): sphere \(i\) overlaps the tool by \(\delta_0-\rho_i^2/2(R-r_s)\), so at equal force its arm is \(((R-r_s)/R)^{1/4}\) = {{PAD_ARMFAC}} of the bed’s. The bed’s 1 mm pad covers a {{BED_CAP}}° cap with \(n\) = {{BED_N}} spheres of \(k_s\) = {{BED_KS}} N/m; the chain’s thumb pad, which Figures 3 and 4 show, covers a {{CH_CAP}}° cap with {{CH_N}} spheres of {{CH_KS}} N/m. Pressed on a flat with 1 N, {{FLAT_N}} of the bed pad’s spheres touch and their arm is {{FLAT_ARM}} mm, against the bed’s {{FLAT_BED}} mm and the {{FLAT_PRED}} mm of a bed of curvature \(R-r_s\). Halving the spacing gives four times the spheres, each a quarter as stiff, so the stiffness per unit area stays \(k_w\). Figure 3 shows the sphere forces sampling the field’s pressure profile at 3 N, and Figure 4 the patches at 3 and 0.5 N.

The spacing has to stay small against the patch radius, {{PATCH_A}} mm at 0.5–3 N on a flat. At 0.5 N a 2 mm pad touches with {{PL2_N}} spheres, and tilting the lattice by 0–8° before pressing moves its arm {{PL2}} the bed’s; the 1 mm pad’s arm moves {{PL1}}, and the 0.5 mm pad’s {{PL05}}. In the bed’s twist at 0.5 N the 2 mm pad starts to spin at an arm of {{P2_ONSET}} mm and spins at {{P2_KIN}} mm, {{P2_ONSET_DEV}} and {{P2_KIN_DEV}} % below the integrated law, while the chain’s 2 mm thumb pad of Figure 4, pressed on the tool at rest with the same force, has an arm {{P2_FIG4}} the field’s.

6Static stiffness of a MuJoCo soft contact

MuJoCo solves each step for constraint forces \(f\) in the space of the contact rows:

\[\begin{aligned} (A+R)\,f&=a_\text{ref}-a_0,\qquad A=J M^{-1}J^\top,\qquad R=\operatorname{diag}\Bigl(\frac{1-d}{d}\,\hat\Lambda_i\Bigr),\\ a_\text{ref}&=-b\,v-k\,r,\qquad b=\frac{2}{d_\text{max}t_c},\qquad k=\frac{d}{d_\text{max}^2t_c^2}. \end{aligned}\](9)

Here \(a_0\) is the row acceleration without contact, \(r\) and \(v\) the penetration and its rate, \((t_c,1)\) the solref, \(d\) the impedance from solimp, and \(\hat\Lambda_i\) MuJoCo’s estimate of row \(i\)’s inverse inertia, the sum of the two bodies’ body_invweight0. \(R\) is diagonal because MuJoCo builds each \(R_i\) from its own \(\hat\Lambda_i\), while the Delassus matrix \(A\) couples every pair of rows on a shared body. With the constrained acceleration \(a_1=a_0+Af\), (9) reads row by row, for any \(A\) and any force inside its cone,

\[R_if_i=a_{\text{ref},i}-a_{1,i},\]

so the rows interact only through \(a_1\). At rest every row has \(a_1=v=0\), so \(R_if_i=-k\,r_i\); with a constant impedance \(d=d_\text{max}=d_0\), \(k=1/(d_0t_c^2)\) and \(R_i=(1-d_0)\hat\Lambda_i/d_0\),

\[f_i=k_s\,\lvert r_i\rvert,\qquad k_s=\frac{1}{t_c^2\,(1-d_0)\,\hat\Lambda_i},\qquad c_s=2t_c\,k_s .\](10)

Here \(k_s=k/R_i\) is the row’s stiffness and \(c_s=b/R_i\) its damping coefficient in N s/m, the force per unit penetration rate when \(a_1=0\). Each sphere is a spring of stiffness \(k_s\) whatever its neighbours carry, so in statics the pad is a discrete Winkler foundation. When one pad slides along the normal against a fixed plate, its spheres all measure the same motion: every entry of \(A\) is \(\hat\Lambda\), and \(A\) has rank one with the eigenvalue \(n\hat\Lambda\) along the common motion. While the pad moves, pressing one sphere harder accelerates the body and unloads the others; at rest \(a_1=0\) removes that coupling. Codex’s static audit measured the force per overlap equal to (10) to floating-point precision (docs/experiments/20261004-codex/20261004-distributed_contact.html). Away from rest one row with \(A=\hat\Lambda\) has \(a_1=d_0a_\text{ref}+(1-d_0)a_0\), and its penetration obeys

\[\ddot r+\frac{2}{t_c}\,\dot r+\frac{r}{t_c^2}=(1-d_0)\,a_0 ,\]

a critically damped motion with time constant \(t_c\) for any \(d_0\) and \(\hat\Lambda\): a sphere pushed from rest returns as \((1+t/t_c)\,e^{-t/t_c}\) and is within 5 % of rest after \({{REL_X}}\,t_c\), {{REL_MS}} ms at \(t_c\) = {{TC_MS}} ms. Under quasi-static loading the row’s damping over its rest stiffness, \(c_s/k_s=2t_c\) in (10), is {{TC2_MS}} ms against Drake’s dissipation time of 10 ms. In bed task 6 at 1 N, with \(t_c\) = {{ONSET_TCS}}, the slip force gives an effective \(\mu\) of {{MU_TC}} and the arm spinning at 1 rad/s is {{KIN_TC}} mm, while the arm at spin onset grows with \(t_c\) through the friction dampers of step 8: {{ONSET_TC}} mm.

7Inverse-inertia calibration

Solref’s \(k\) is an acceleration per metre, in s\(^{-2}\); dividing it by \(R_i\), in kg\(^{-1}\), gives a force per metre, and that is where the bodies’ masses enter (10). Solved for the impedance,

\[d_0=1-\frac{1}{k_s\hat\Lambda t_c^2},\qquad 0(k_s\hat\Lambda)^{-1/2}.\]

At a given \(t_c\) the softest spring MuJoCo can give is \(1/(\hat\Lambda t_c^2)\), reached as \(d_0\to0\), so a softer sphere needs a longer time constant. On the bed’s railed pads \(\hat\Lambda\) = {{BED_WP}} + {{W_TOOL}} = {{BED_LAM}} kg\(^{-1}\), a {{BED_PADG}} g pad and the 24.5 g tool: the 1 mm pad’s {{BED_KS}} N/m needs \(t_c\) above {{BED_TCMIN}} ms and takes \(d_0\) = {{BED_D0}} at {{TC_MS}} ms, and the 0.5 mm pad’s {{BED_KS05}} N/m needs {{BED_TCMIN05}} ms. On the chain’s thumb, \(\hat\Lambda\) = {{TH_WP}} + {{W_TOOL}} = {{TH_LAM}} kg\(^{-1}\), the 1 mm and 0.5 mm pads need {{TH_TCMIN}} and {{TH_TCMIN05}} ms, and the 1 mm pad takes \(d_0\) = {{TH_D0}} at {{TC_MS}} ms. \(\hat\Lambda\) is the pad’s inverse weight \(w_p\) plus the tool’s, so with \(d_0\) held, a tool \(s\) times heavier than the nominal \(m_0\) scales every sphere’s stiffness by

\[\frac{k_s(s\,m_0)}{k_s(m_0)}=\frac{w_p+1/m_0}{w_p+1/(s\,m_0)},\]

{{MASSF_BED}} on the bed and {{MASSF_TH}} on the thumb for a fourfold tool, whose arm then shortens by {{ARMF_BED}} and {{ARMF_TH}} % through \(\bar r\propto k_s^{-1/4}\): a tool-mass error matters most where the pad is heavy against the tool. {{MASS_TEXT}} The friction arm depends on the stiffness only through the patch radius:

\[\bar r\propto a\propto\Bigl(\frac{F}{k_s}\Bigr)^{1/4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\Delta\bar r}{\bar r}\approx-\frac14\,\frac{\Delta k_s}{k_s}.\](11)

8Creep under MuJoCo’s soft friction

Creep is the steady sliding of a contact under a tangential load below its friction limit; rigid Coulomb friction gives none. A sphere’s friction rows enter (9) with \(a_\text{ref}=-b\,v\) and no position term, and with a regulariser \(R_t=R_n/\kappa\) set by impratio \(\kappa\). At steady sliding \(a_1=0\), so each friction row obeys \(R_tf_t=-b\,v\); with \(b=2/(d_0t_c)\) and (10) it is a damper \(c_t\), in N s/m, with \(\kappa\) times the normal row’s coefficient \(c_s\):

\[f_t=-\frac{b}{R_t}\,v=-\frac{2\kappa}{(1-d_0)\,\hat\Lambda\,t_c}\,v=-c_t\,v,\qquad c_t=\kappa\,c_s=2\kappa\,k_s\,t_c .\]

Once \(d_0\) is chosen for \(k_s\) (step 7), \((1-d_0)\hat\Lambda=1/(k_st_c^2)\), and neither \(d_0\) nor \(\hat\Lambda\) remains. All touching spheres slide together, so with equal shares \(n\) spheres carry a load \(F_t\) at

\[v_\text{creep}=\frac{F_t}{n\,c_t}=\frac{F_t}{2\kappa\,n\,k_s\,t_c}.\](12)

Rigid Coulomb friction leaves the shares undetermined below slip. MuJoCo’s dampers make them equal, so the outer spheres, with the smallest normal forces \(N_i\), reach \(\mu N_i\) first and the inner ones carry the rest:

\[F_t=\sum_i\min\bigl(c_t\,v,\ \mu N_i\bigr).\]

9Step-size bound for a pad

Away from rest, the rows of one pad move together. For \(n\) rows of equal \(\hat\Lambda\) on one body, \(A\) has the eigenvalue \(n\hat\Lambda\) along their common motion, so (9) gives that motion an effective impedance \(d'\), a damping rate \(\beta\) in s\(^{-1}\) and a spring \(\omega^2\):

\[d'=\frac{n\,d_0}{n\,d_0+1-d_0},\qquad \beta=d'\,b=\frac{2}{q\,t_c},\qquad \omega^2=d'\,k=\frac{1}{q\,t_c^2},\qquad q=\frac{d_0}{d'}=d_0+\frac{1-d_0}{n}.\](13)

On a body that also turns, the largest eigenvalue \(\lambda_\text{max}\) of \(A\) takes the place of \(n\hat\Lambda\), and \(q=d_0+(1-d_0)\hat\Lambda/\lambda_\text{max}\). MuJoCo evaluates \(a_\text{ref}=-b\,v-k\,r\) at the state at the start of the step, so the spring term is as explicit as the damper. For \(\ddot x=-\beta\,\dot x-\omega^2x\), updating the velocity and then the position with the new velocity over a step \(\Delta t\) gives the step matrix

\[\begin{bmatrix}x\\ v\end{bmatrix}_{j+1}=\begin{bmatrix}1-\Delta t^2\omega^2 & \Delta t\,(1-\Delta t\,\beta)\\ -\Delta t\,\omega^2 & 1-\Delta t\,\beta\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\ v\end{bmatrix}_j ,\]

whose eigenvalues lie inside the unit circle when \(\Delta t\,\beta<2\) and \(\Delta t^2\omega^2+2\Delta t\,\beta<4\). With (13) the second condition reads \((\Delta t/t_c)^2+4\Delta t/t_c<4q\):

\[\Delta t<2t_c\bigl(\sqrt{1+q}-1\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;\begin{cases}2(\sqrt2-1)\,t_c, & n=1,\\ 2t_c\bigl(\sqrt{1+d_0}-1\bigr), & n\to\infty.\end{cases}\](14)

The damper condition alone, \(\Delta twxie_workspace/contact, script tutorial/09_fingertip_contact.py, row d23_step_threshold) bisects the spectral radius of MuJoCo’s one-step map for this pad on an undamped slide and finds it crossing 1 at the bound, to within \(10^{-4}t_c\) at 24 pairs of \(t_c\) and \(d_0\). For the 1 mm pads of these pages (\(t_c\) = {{TC_MS}} ms, \(d_0\) = {{SB_D0S}}) the bound falls from {{SB_ONE}} ms for one touching sphere to {{SB_LIM}} ms for many.

The pads gave the same task results from 50 µs to 10 ms steps in Codex’s 50 Hz sweep (Figure 8). Codex’s per-contact mapping set \(d_0=10^{-4}\) to impose each sphere’s spring and damper exactly. Then \(q\approx1/n\), and the damping rate \(\beta\approx nc_s\hat\Lambda\), with \(c_s\) = 24.5 N s/m per sphere, requires \(\Delta t<2/(nc_s\hat\Lambda)\): 60–90 µs for 20–30 spheres. Those runs diverged at every step from 0.5 to 10 ms and completed only at 50 µs. MuJoCo applies its clamp \(t_c\ge2\Delta t\) per row, which leaves this collective mode unbounded.

10condim 4 with a rescheduled torsional coefficient

condim 4 keeps one contact per pad and adds a torsional coefficient \(\mu_t\), limited together with the tangential force by an elliptic cone \((f_1^2+f_2^2)/\mu^2+\tau^2/\mu_t^2\le N^2\). Matching (3) requires \(\mu_t=\mu\bar r(N)\), which varies with load, so the plant rewrites it before every step from the last normal force:

\[\mu_t[j]=\mu\,c\,N[j-1]^{1/4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tau_\text{max}[j]\approx\mu\,c\,N^{5/4}.\](15)

The bed’s condim 4 takes \(c\) and the exponent from a fit to Drake’s torque on this rig (\(c=0.996\ \mathrm{mm\,N^{-1/4}}\), 0.2498), so it reproduces Drake’s torque while the tool spins; at spin onset it sits above the law (twist, in the comparison below). It costs one coefficient write per contact per step. With a single contact point, its centre of pressure is fixed, and under a pure pull or roll it behaves as condim 3. The chain page derives the brake that uses it ({{CHAIN_PATH}}, steps 3 and 5).

Drake and the spheres integrate the same pressure law, which is local and has no state. With the friction rows stiffened by impratio 1000, the sprung skin adds the shear compliance before slip and the rolling bristles its hysteresis, the bristles at {{BRISTLE_X}} times the batched cost; at the policy trainer’s impratio of 10 the friction rows’ creep swamps the skin’s springs. Neither model represents the lateral spread of load in an elastic tip, and on a spherical tip both give a contact radius growing as \(N^{1/4}\); measured soft fingertips give exponents from 0 to 1/3, depending on the material (Xydas and Kao). The spheres add the creep of MuJoCo’s soft friction rows and miss surface features finer than their 1 mm spacing.
