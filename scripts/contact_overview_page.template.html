What a fingertip contact model has to carry

normal compliance, Coulomb friction, friction torque, and the pressure distribution that sets the torque

The manipulation studied on the SR2 hand pinches, turns, rolls and lets slip a 24.5 g screwdriver between two or three soft fingertips. Four contact quantities decide those motions. The normal force and the pad’s give under it set the grasp. Coulomb friction in the tangent plane decides whether a contact sticks or slides. The friction torque about the contact normal resists a turn about the pinch axis, and the pinch brake and the wield of arXiv 2609.25619 depend on it. The pressure distribution over the contact area sets that torque and the centre of pressure.

No simulator models the fingertip that will be built. It will probably be printed TPU, possibly with a gyroid infill, and its modulus, damping, shear compliance and friction have not been measured. The target is therefore a model whose task-level behaviour agrees with pressure-field references and analytic limits and does not change with step size or resolution. It also has to be cheap enough for reinforcement learning and model-predictive control. A receding-horizon controller replans from measured state and absorbs a consistent model error, provided the model contains the friction torque and slip that the task uses.

Contact mechanics of a soft fingertip

the friction torque of a patch, and how it scales with load on a foundation and on an elastic half-space

1Friction torque of a contact patch

A pad pressed with normal force \(F\) spreads it over a patch \(A\) as a pressure \(p\). When the tool turns about the contact normal relative to the pad, each area element slides on a circle about the patch centre, and its friction \(\mu p\,dA\) acts at lever \(\rho\):

\[F=\int_A p\,dA,\qquad T=\mu\int_A p\,\rho\,dA=\mu F\,\bar r,\qquad \bar r=\frac{\int_A p\,\rho\,dA}{\int_A p\,dA}.\](1)

\(T\) is the largest torque the contact carries before it spins. The friction arm \(\bar r\) is the pressure-weighted mean distance from the patch centre, so it depends on the size of the patch and on the shape of the pressure over it. A point contact has \(\rho=0\) and transmits no torque about its normal, and two point contacts pinching a tool make a free hinge.

2An elastic foundation: Winkler springs and the hydroelastic field

The simplest compliant contact is a Winkler foundation, a bed of independent springs that each push back in proportion to their own compression, \(p=k_w\delta\) with \(k_w=E/h\) for a layer of modulus \(E\) and thickness \(h\). Drake’s hydroelastic contact (Elandt et al., 2019) is a foundation of this kind. A compliant body carries a pressure field that rises with depth below its surface, and for a sphere of radius \(R\) the field is \(p=E\,\delta/R\), a Winkler layer with \(h=R\). For a sphere pressed \(\delta_0\) into a flat face, the compression at distance \(\rho\) from the patch centre is \(\delta_0-\rho^2/2R\), the patch is a disc of radius \(a=\sqrt{2R\delta_0}\), and

\[\begin{aligned} F&=\int_0^a \frac{E}{R}\Bigl(\delta_0-\frac{\rho^2}{2R}\Bigr)\,2\pi\rho\,d\rho=\pi E\,\delta_0^2,\\ T&=\mu\int_0^a \frac{E}{R}\Bigl(\delta_0-\frac{\rho^2}{2R}\Bigr)\,2\pi\rho^2\,d\rho=\mu F\cdot\frac{8}{15}\,a . \end{aligned}\](2)

Eliminating \(\delta_0\) gives the arm as a power of the load:

\[\bar r_W=\frac{8}{15}\sqrt{2R}\,\Bigl(\frac{F}{\pi E}\Bigr)^{1/4}=c\,F^{1/4}.\](3)

For the real_v1 fingertip at Drake’s default \(E\) = 10 MPa a flat face gives \(c\) = 1.035 mm·N−1/4. On the 12.5 mm screwdriver the patch narrows across the tool axis, and a fit to Drake’s torque gives \(c\) = 0.996 mm·N−1/4 with exponent 0.2498: an arm of 0.84 mm at 0.5 N and 1.31 mm at 3 N. The springs do not shear against each other, so the pressure stops at the edge of the geometric overlap.

3An elastic half-space: Hertz

A homogeneous elastic body carries load sideways through shear, so its surface deflects outside the loaded region and the pressure takes the Hertz form. For a sphere on a rigid flat, with \(E^\ast=E/(1-\nu^2)\),

\[a^3=\frac{3FR}{4E^\ast},\qquad p(\rho)=\frac{3F}{2\pi a^2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho^2}{a^2}},\qquad \bar r_H=\frac{3\pi}{16}\,a\;\propto\;F^{1/3}.\](4)

The two laws differ in how fast the arm grows with load. Between 0.5 and 3 N:

\[\frac{\bar r(3\,\mathrm{N})}{\bar r(0.5\,\mathrm{N})}=6^{1/4}=1.565\ \text{(foundation)},\qquad 6^{1/3}=1.817\ \text{(Hertz)}.\](5)

The ratio needs no modulus. A torque sensor and two loads on a printed fingertip decide which family the tip belongs to, and with it whether a foundation model can represent it.

4Between the two: lateral coupling

A Pasternak foundation adds a shear layer that couples neighbouring springs,

\[p=k_w\,\delta-G\,\nabla^2\delta,\qquad \ell=\sqrt{G/k_w},\](6)

and load spreads over the coupling length \(\ell\) beyond the overlap. A foundation is accurate for \(\ell\ll a\), a coupled foundation is needed for \(\ell\sim a\), and a shell or modal model for \(\ell\gg a\). {{CSLC_MECH}} The asides page sets out how \(\ell\) would be identified on the printed tip ({{ASIDES_REF}}).

The sphere-packed pad in MuJoCo

a foundation sampled by ordinary soft contacts, what sets its stiffness, its friction, and its step size

5Sampling the foundation with spheres

The pad replaces the field with \(n\) spheres of radius \(r_s\) whose outer envelope is the fingertip. Each sphere is an ordinary condim 3 MuJoCo contact, and the pad’s force and torque are sums over the spheres that touch:

\[F\approx\sum_i N_i,\qquad T\approx\sum_i \rho_i\,f_{t,i},\qquad \lVert f_{t,i}\rVert\le\mu N_i .\](7)

Each sphere gets the foundation’s stiffness over its share \(A_s\) of the pad. The centres lie on radius \(R-r_s\), which packs them \((R/(R-r_s))^2\) more densely on the tool than on the fingertip:

\[k_s=\frac{E}{R}\,A_s\Bigl(\frac{R-r_s}{R}\Bigr)^2 .\](8)

The 1 mm pad on a 45° cap has \(n\) = 205 spheres of \(k_s\) = 817 N/m. Figure 3 shows the sphere forces sampling the field’s pressure profile at 3 N and Figure 4 the patches at 3 and 0.5 N. The spacing has to be fine compared with the patch. At 0.5 N a 2 mm pad touches with {{LO_N2}} spheres and its arm is {{LO_DEV2}} % long; the 1 and 0.5 mm pads stay within {{LO_DEV_FINE}} %.

6A soft contact at rest is a spring that ignores its neighbours

MuJoCo solves each step for constraint forces \(f\) in the space of the contact rows:

\[\begin{aligned} (A+R)\,f&=a_\text{ref}-a_0,\qquad A=J M^{-1}J^\top,\qquad R=\operatorname{diag}\Bigl(\frac{1-d}{d}\,\hat\Lambda_i\Bigr),\\ a_\text{ref}&=-b\,v-k\,r,\qquad b=\frac{2}{d_\text{max}t_c},\qquad k=\frac{d}{d_\text{max}^2t_c^2}. \end{aligned}\](9)

Here \(a_0\) is the row acceleration without contact, \(r\) and \(v\) the penetration and its rate, \((t_c,1)\) the solref, \(d\) the impedance from solimp and \(\hat\Lambda_i\) MuJoCo’s estimate of row \(i\)’s inverse inertia. The constrained acceleration is \(a=a_0+Af=a_\text{ref}-Rf\). At rest \(a=v=0\), so every row satisfies \(R_if_i=-k\,r_i\) by itself, and with a constant impedance \(d=d_\text{max}=d_0\)

\[f_i=k_s\,\lvert r_i\rvert,\qquad k_s=\frac{1}{t_c^2\,(1-d_0)\,\hat\Lambda_i},\qquad \frac{c_i}{k_s}=2t_c .\](10)

The coupling through \(A\), which is dense when many spheres press one body, drops out at rest. Each sphere is an exact spring of stiffness \(k_s\) however many of its neighbours touch, so the pad is a discrete Winkler foundation in statics. Codex’s static audit measured the force per overlap equal to (10) to floating-point precision (docs/experiments/20261004-codex/20261004-distributed_contact.html). Setting \(t_c=t_r/2\) from Drake’s relaxation time and solving (10) gives the impedance \(d_0=1-1/(t_c^2k_s\hat\Lambda)\), 0.741 for the 1 mm pad at \(t_c\) = 10 ms.

7The inverse inertia, and calibrating it once per object

\(\hat\Lambda_i\) is the sum of the two bodies’ inverse weights, so by (10) a sphere’s stiffness depends on the mass behind the contact. {{MASS_TEXT}} The friction arm is insensitive to the error because it follows the stiffness only through the patch radius:

\[\bar r\propto a\propto\Bigl(\frac{F}{k_s}\Bigr)^{1/4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\Delta\bar r}{\bar r}\approx-\frac14\,\frac{\Delta k_s}{k_s}.\](11)

8Friction rows, and why a MuJoCo grip creeps

A sphere’s friction rows enter (9) with \(a_\text{ref}=-b\,v\) and no position term, and with a regulariser \(R_t=R_n/\kappa\) set by impratio \(\kappa\). Under a steady tangential load below the friction limit, (9) at zero acceleration gives \(R_tf_t=-b\,v\): the contact slides at a constant creep speed instead of sticking. With \(n\) rows sharing a load \(F_t\),

\[v_\text{creep}\approx\frac{R_t\,F_t}{n\,b}=\frac{(1-d_0)\,\hat\Lambda\,t_c}{2\,\kappa\,n}\,F_t .\](12)

9Step size: the collective damping of many soft contacts

Away from rest the rows of one pad move together. For \(n\) rows of equal \(\hat\Lambda\) on one body, \(A\) has the eigenvalue \(n\hat\Lambda\) along their common motion, so (9) gives that motion an effective impedance \(d'\) and a damping rate \(\lambda\):

\[d'=\frac{n\,d_0}{n\,d_0+1-d_0},\qquad \lambda=d'\,b=\frac{2\,d'}{d_0\,t_c}.\](13)

For the 1 mm pads (\(d_0\) = 0.74–0.89, \(t_c\) = 10–15 ms) the bound is 7–13 ms and cannot fall with more spheres in contact, because \(d'\le1\). The pads held their task results from 50 µs to 10 ms steps in Codex’s 50 Hz sweep (Figure 7). Codex’s per-contact mapping set \(d_0=10^{-4}\) to impose each sphere’s spring and damper exactly. Then \(nd_0\ll1\), the summed contact damping \(nC\hat\Lambda\) with \(C\) = 24.5 N·s/m per sphere sets \(\Delta t<2/(nC\hat\Lambda)\), 60–90 µs for 20–30 spheres, and its runs diverged at every step from 0.5 to 10 ms and finished only at 50 µs. MuJoCo’s safety clamp \(t_c\ge2\Delta t\) covers one row and does not see the collective mode. {{STAB_TEXT}}

10The single-point alternative

condim 4 keeps one contact per pad and supplies the torque through a torsional coefficient \(\mu_t\), bounded with the tangential force in an elliptic cone \((f_1^2+f_2^2)/\mu^2+\tau^2/\mu_t^2\le N^2\). Matching (3) requires \(\mu_t=\mu\bar r(N)\), which changes with load, so the plant rewrites it before every step from the last normal force:

\[\mu_t[k]=\mu\,c\,N[k-1]^{1/4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tau_\text{max}[k]\approx\mu\,c\,N^{5/4}.\](15)

It matches the torque of the law it was fitted to, costs one Python write per contact per step on the CPU, and has no patch: the centre of pressure cannot move, and a pure pull or roll sees a condim 3 contact. The chain page derives the brake that uses it ({{CHAIN_PATH}}, steps 3 and 5).
