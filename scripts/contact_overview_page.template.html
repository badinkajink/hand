Components of a fingertip contact model

The SR2 hand pinches, turns, rolls and releases a 24.5 g screwdriver between two or three soft fingertips. Four contact quantities govern these motions: the normal force with the pad compliance behind it, Coulomb friction in the tangent plane, the friction torque about the contact normal, and the pressure distribution over the contact area, which sets that torque and the centre of pressure. The pinch brake and the wield of arXiv 2609.25619 depend on the friction torque.

The fingertip to be built will probably be printed TPU, possibly with a gyroid infill; its modulus, damping, shear compliance and friction are unmeasured. The model is therefore judged by agreement with pressure-field references and analytic limits on task-level metrics, by consistency across step size and resolution, and by cost. A receding-horizon controller corrects a consistent model error from measured state, provided the model contains the friction torque and slip the task uses.

Contact mechanics of a soft fingertip

1Friction torque of a contact patch

A pad pressed with normal force \(F\) spreads it over a patch \(A\) as a pressure \(p\). When the tool turns about the contact normal relative to the pad, each area element slides on a circle about the patch centre, and its friction \(\mu p\,dA\) acts at lever \(\rho\):

\[F=\int_A p\,dA,\qquad T=\mu\int_A p\,\rho\,dA=\mu F\,\bar r,\qquad \bar r=\frac{\int_A p\,\rho\,dA}{\int_A p\,dA}.\](1)

\(T\) is the largest torque the contact transmits before it spins. The friction arm \(\bar r\) depends on the size of the patch and on the shape of the pressure over it. A point contact has \(\rho=0\) and transmits no torque about its normal, so two point contacts pinching a tool form a free hinge.

2Winkler foundation and the hydroelastic pressure field

A Winkler foundation is a bed of independent springs, each pushing back in proportion to its own compression: \(p=k_w\delta\), with \(k_w=E/h\) for a layer of modulus \(E\) and thickness \(h\). Drake’s hydroelastic contact (Elandt et al., 2019) is a foundation of this kind. Each compliant body is given a pressure field that rises with depth below its surface; for a sphere of radius \(R\) the field is \(p=E\,\delta/R\), a Winkler layer with \(h=R\). For a sphere pressed \(\delta_0\) into a flat face, the compression at distance \(\rho\) from the patch centre is \(\delta_0-\rho^2/2R\), the patch is a disc of radius \(a=\sqrt{2R\delta_0}\), and

\[\begin{aligned} F&=\int_0^a \frac{E}{R}\Bigl(\delta_0-\frac{\rho^2}{2R}\Bigr)\,2\pi\rho\,d\rho=\pi E\,\delta_0^2,\\ T&=\mu\int_0^a \frac{E}{R}\Bigl(\delta_0-\frac{\rho^2}{2R}\Bigr)\,2\pi\rho^2\,d\rho=\mu F\cdot\frac{8}{15}\,a . \end{aligned}\](2)

Eliminating \(\delta_0\) gives the arm as a power of the load:

\[\bar r_W=\frac{8}{15}\sqrt{2R}\,\Bigl(\frac{F}{\pi E}\Bigr)^{1/4}=c\,F^{1/4}.\](3)

For the real_v1 fingertip at Drake’s default \(E\) = 10 MPa, a flat face gives \(c=1.035\ \mathrm{mm\,N^{-1/4}}\). On the 12.5 mm screwdriver the patch is narrower across the tool axis. Integrating the same field over the sphere–cylinder overlap (scripts/hydroelastic_arm_integral.py) gives an arm of 0.820, 0.974 and 1.281 mm at 0.5, 1 and 3 N, \(c=0.974\ \mathrm{mm\,N^{-1/4}}\) with the exponent 1/4 to within 0.1 %. This integral is the reference of the twist task; Drake and the sphere pads are two discretizations of it. The springs are uncoupled, so the pressure is zero outside the geometric overlap.

3Hertz contact

A homogeneous elastic body transmits load sideways through shear, so its surface deflects outside the loaded region and the pressure takes the Hertz form. For a sphere on a rigid flat, with \(E^\ast=E/(1-\nu^2)\),

\[a^3=\frac{3FR}{4E^\ast},\qquad p(\rho)=\frac{3F}{2\pi a^2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho^2}{a^2}},\qquad \bar r_H=\frac{3\pi}{16}\,a\;\propto\;F^{1/3}.\](4)

The two laws differ in how fast the arm grows with load. Between 0.5 and 3 N:

\[\frac{\bar r(3\,\mathrm{N})}{\bar r(0.5\,\mathrm{N})}=6^{1/4}=1.565\ \text{(foundation)},\qquad 6^{1/3}=1.817\ \text{(Hertz)}.\](5)

The ratio is independent of the modulus, so a torque measurement at two loads on a printed fingertip identifies which law the tip follows.

4Lateral coupling: the Pasternak foundation

A Pasternak foundation adds a shear layer that couples neighbouring springs,

\[p=k_w\,\delta-G\,\nabla^2\delta,\qquad \ell=\sqrt{G/k_w},\](6)

and load spreads a coupling length \(\ell\) beyond the overlap. A foundation suffices for \(\ell\ll a\); a coupled foundation is needed for \(\ell\sim a\), and a shell or modal model for \(\ell\gg a\). {{CSLC_MECH}} The asides page describes how \(\ell\) would be measured on the printed tip ({{ASIDES_REF}}).

Sphere-packed pads in MuJoCo

5Sphere discretization of the foundation

The pad replaces the field with \(n\) spheres of radius \(r_s\) whose outer envelope is the fingertip. Each sphere is an ordinary condim 3 MuJoCo contact, and the pad’s force and torque are sums over the spheres in contact:

\[F\approx\sum_i N_i,\qquad T\approx\sum_i \rho_i\,f_{t,i},\qquad \lVert f_{t,i}\rVert\le\mu N_i .\](7)

Each sphere gets the foundation’s stiffness over its share \(A_s\) of the pad. The centres lie on radius \(R-r_s\), which packs them \((R/(R-r_s))^2\) more densely on the tool than on the fingertip surface:

\[k_s=\frac{E}{R}\,A_s\Bigl(\frac{R-r_s}{R}\Bigr)^2 .\](8)

The 1 mm pad on a 45° cap has \(n\) = 205 spheres of \(k_s\) = 817 N/m. Figure 3 shows the sphere forces sampling the field’s pressure profile at 3 N, and Figure 4 the patches at 3 and 0.5 N. The spacing must be small compared with the patch: at 0.5 N a 2 mm pad touches with {{LO_N2}} spheres and its arm is {{LO_DEV2}} % long, while the 1 and 0.5 mm pads are within {{LO_DEV_FINE}} %.

6Static stiffness of a MuJoCo soft contact

MuJoCo solves each step for constraint forces \(f\) in the space of the contact rows:

\[\begin{aligned} (A+R)\,f&=a_\text{ref}-a_0,\qquad A=J M^{-1}J^\top,\qquad R=\operatorname{diag}\Bigl(\frac{1-d}{d}\,\hat\Lambda_i\Bigr),\\ a_\text{ref}&=-b\,v-k\,r,\qquad b=\frac{2}{d_\text{max}t_c},\qquad k=\frac{d}{d_\text{max}^2t_c^2}. \end{aligned}\](9)

Here \(a_0\) is the row acceleration without contact, \(r\) and \(v\) the penetration and its rate, \((t_c,1)\) the solref, \(d\) the impedance from solimp, and \(\hat\Lambda_i\) MuJoCo’s estimate of row \(i\)’s inverse inertia. The constrained acceleration is \(a=a_0+Af=a_\text{ref}-Rf\). At rest \(a=v=0\), so each row satisfies \(R_if_i=-k\,r_i\) independently, and with a constant impedance \(d=d_\text{max}=d_0\)

\[f_i=k_s\,\lvert r_i\rvert,\qquad k_s=\frac{1}{t_c^2\,(1-d_0)\,\hat\Lambda_i},\qquad \frac{c_i}{k_s}=2t_c .\](10)

The coupling through \(A\), dense when many spheres press one body, cancels at rest. Each sphere is a spring of stiffness \(k_s\) regardless of how many neighbours touch, so in statics the pad is a discrete Winkler foundation. Codex’s static audit measured the force per overlap equal to (10) to floating-point precision (docs/experiments/20261004-codex/20261004-distributed_contact.html). Solving (10) for the impedance gives \(d_0=1-1/(t_c^2k_s\hat\Lambda)\), which is positive only for \(t_c>(k_s\hat\Lambda)^{-1/2}\): 5.1 ms for the 1 mm pad and 10.2 ms for the 0.5 mm pad, so a softer sphere needs a longer time constant. The 1 mm pad uses \(t_c\) = 10 ms (\(d_0\) = 0.741) and relaxes in \(2t_c\) = 20 ms, twice Drake’s 10 ms on the bed; the slip force and the friction arm are measured in quasi-static ramps and do not depend on it.

7Inverse-inertia calibration

\(\hat\Lambda_i\) is the sum of the two bodies’ inverse weights, so by (10) a sphere’s stiffness depends on the mass behind the contact. {{MASS_TEXT}} The friction arm depends on the stiffness only through the patch radius:

\[\bar r\propto a\propto\Bigl(\frac{F}{k_s}\Bigr)^{1/4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\Delta\bar r}{\bar r}\approx-\frac14\,\frac{\Delta k_s}{k_s}.\](11)

8Creep under MuJoCo’s soft friction

Creep is the steady sliding of a contact under a tangential load below its friction limit; rigid Coulomb friction gives none. A sphere’s friction rows enter (9) with \(a_\text{ref}=-b\,v\) and no position term, and with a regulariser \(R_t=R_n/\kappa\) set by impratio \(\kappa\). At zero acceleration (9) gives \(R_tf_t=-b\,v\), a constant sliding speed. With \(n\) rows sharing a load \(F_t\),

\[v_\text{creep}\approx\frac{R_t\,F_t}{n\,b}=\frac{(1-d_0)\,\hat\Lambda\,t_c}{2\,\kappa\,n}\,F_t .\](12)

9Step-size bound for a pad

Away from rest, the rows of one pad move together. For \(n\) rows of equal \(\hat\Lambda\) on one body, \(A\) has the eigenvalue \(n\hat\Lambda\) along their common motion, so (9) gives that motion an effective impedance \(d'\) and a damping rate \(\lambda\):

\[d'=\frac{n\,d_0}{n\,d_0+1-d_0},\qquad \lambda=d'\,b=\frac{2\,d'}{d_0\,t_c}.\](13)

MuJoCo evaluates \(a_\text{ref}\) with the velocity at the start of the step, so the damping is explicit and stable for \(\lambda\,\Delta t<2\):

For the 1 mm pads (\(d_0\) = 0.74–0.89, \(t_c\) = 10–15 ms) the bound is 7–13 ms, and it cannot fall as more spheres touch because \(d'\le1\). The pads gave the same task results from 50 µs to 10 ms steps in Codex’s 50 Hz sweep (Figure 8). Codex’s per-contact mapping set \(d_0=10^{-4}\) to impose each sphere’s spring and damper exactly. Then \(nd_0\ll1\), and the summed contact damping \(nC\hat\Lambda\), with \(C\) = 24.5 N·s/m per sphere, requires \(\Delta t<2/(nC\hat\Lambda)\): 60–90 µs for 20–30 spheres. Those runs diverged at every step from 0.5 to 10 ms and completed only at 50 µs. MuJoCo applies its clamp \(t_c\ge2\Delta t\) per row, which leaves this collective mode unbounded. {{STAB_TEXT}}

10condim 4 with a rescheduled torsional coefficient

condim 4 keeps one contact per pad and adds a torsional coefficient \(\mu_t\), bounded with the tangential force in an elliptic cone \((f_1^2+f_2^2)/\mu^2+\tau^2/\mu_t^2\le N^2\). Matching (3) requires \(\mu_t=\mu\bar r(N)\), which varies with load, so the plant rewrites it before every step from the last normal force:

\[\mu_t[k]=\mu\,c\,N[k-1]^{1/4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tau_\text{max}[k]\approx\mu\,c\,N^{5/4}.\](15)

The bed’s condim 4 takes \(c\) and the exponent from a fit to Drake’s torque on this rig (\(c=0.996\ \mathrm{mm\,N^{-1/4}}\), 0.2498), so it reproduces Drake’s torque by construction, at the cost of one coefficient write per contact per step. With a single contact point, its centre of pressure is fixed, and under a pure pull or roll it behaves as condim 3. The chain page derives the brake that uses it ({{CHAIN_PATH}}, steps 3 and 5).

What this does not settle

{{OPEN_LIST}}

Drake serves as the reference because it integrates the same pressure law, which is local and has no state: neither Drake nor the spheres represent shear compliance before slip, hysteresis or the lateral spread of load in an elastic tip, and both give a contact radius growing as \(N^{1/4}\), where measured soft fingertips give exponents from 0 to 1/3 depending on the material (Xydas and Kao). The spheres add the creep of MuJoCo’s soft friction rows and miss surface features finer than their 1 mm spacing.
