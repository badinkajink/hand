SR² Hand · planning study · 1 October 2026

Inverse kinematics and morphology–posture optimization of the SR² Hand in Drake

A Drake model built from the real_v1 MJCF matches MuJoCo’s fingertip poses and Jacobians to {{FK_ERROR}} m over {{SAMPLES}} configurations, and solves {{IK_COUNT}} of {{IK_COUNT}} inverse-kinematics problems (three mount layouts, frozen and with locked gantries, and one joint morphology–posture solve) within the joint limits, with at least {{MIN_CLEARANCE}} mm between enabled collision pairs, in about 1 ms each. A raw MJCF import differs in actuator limits, derivative gains and contact, so an adapter rebuilds the actuators; unlocking a gantry during a live simulation fails in Drake 1.57. Drake fits as a planning component beside the MuJoCo evaluation pipeline.

Terms and metrics

Gantry offsets, morphology
The six XY positions of the three finger mounts relative to their nominal origins, in mm; the only hardware morphology variables.
Frozen and locked model
The nine finger joints with the mounts baked into the body transforms; the fifteen-coordinate model with the six gantry joints locked at a design.
IK problem
Joint angles that place the three fingertip centres in a ±1 μm box around world targets, within the source joint limits, with 1 mm requested clearance between enabled collision pairs.
Tip error, clearance
Largest fingertip distance from its target, in μm; smallest distance between enabled collision pairs, in mm.
Discrepancy
Largest absolute difference between Drake and MuJoCo in a fingertip coordinate, rotation entry, Jacobian entry or mass-matrix entry.
SNOPT, SAP
The nonlinear solver used for IK; Drake’s contact solver, whose assertion stops the live unlock.

Planning architecture

The measured geometry and the existing morphology records stay the source of truth, and Drake joins the MuJoCo evaluation pipeline as a planning and design-optimization component. The prototype reads the existing MJCF and resolves actuator defaults with MuJoCo, without duplicating the CAD dimensions.

UseRepresentationBehavior
Evaluate a chosen designFrozen model: 9 positionsBake the six mount offsets into body transforms and rebuild for each design; the first production target.
Many fixed-design IK problemsOne 15-position plant; a context per candidateSet and lock the six prismatic coordinates, then build IK with that context; the tested IK keeps all six exactly.
Joint morphology and posture search15-position model, gantries freeThe six gantry positions become decision variables, with their bounds and fingertip Jacobians.
Moving gantries during manipulation15-position dynamics with trajectory commandsNeeds motor and rail identification, collision geometry and transition handling; live unlocking fails in the tested build.

For a trajectory or several task poses, one six-coordinate mount vector is shared by all knots and the nine finger angles are optimized at each knot; otherwise the optimizer can change the hand during a task meant for one morphology. The problem minimizes the task cost of the fingertip positions at each knot plus motion and design costs, subject to the workspace bounds, the joint ranges, collision clearance and the task’s contact conditions. Generic mathematical programs do not inherit the IK constructor’s locked-coordinate constraints.

Future work

  1. Bench limits and plant. Replace the source ranges (±85° yaw, up to 92° flexion) with the driver’s (±70° yaw; index MCP to 64.75°), keeping the sign and unit conversion of manta_hand/plan.py and the ranges of servos.py, and the template gains (kp 30) with the calibrated ones (kp 0.5, kv 0.02, 0.35 N·m); the gantry damping of 2000 is a placeholder, and a 0.2 s unlocked rollout moved {{GANTRY_MOVE}} mm.
  2. Deployed geometry. Port the flat pads, rails and carriages, the tool, the table and the UR5e, and restore the self-contact pairs that the 21 source exclusions omit; until then, collision-free means only the enabled pairs of this simplified hand.
  3. Connection to the evaluator. Import a design record, solve task targets within the hardware limits, optimize the path with Drake’s kinematic trajectory optimization, export the named-joint trajectory, and run the MuJoCo clearance, retention and perturbation checks; compare D1–D8 after matching pad shape, plant and initialization.
  4. Contact. Test Drake’s hydroelastic contact for the pads with at least one compliant participant per pair, calibrated against the contact bed; a grasp needs force constraints beyond fingertip positions.

The backend factory and the RL stack target MuJoCo and Warp; the first deliverable is a planning interface that takes a morphology, task targets and limits and returns named-joint trajectories with feasibility checks.
