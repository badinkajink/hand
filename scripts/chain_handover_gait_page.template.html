What happens after the turn, stage by stage

Each change below was made against a measured failure; the seams record the apex’s offset from the socket, the palm’s tilt, the set-point error and the actuators on their force ceiling at every stage.

  1. Staging. The arm stands the tool up in three corrections over 3 s instead of one move over 0.6 s. In the single move D5 with the separation term lost the tool on two pads; in three it reaches the seat.
  2. Seat. Unchanged from 2026-09-20: the arm carries the tool’s apex over the socket and stands the tool up about the seated tip. After the single-move staging D6 clip’s tool sat on the countersink wall (apex 4.9 mm off-centre, 4 mm proud); after the three-step staging it seats (apex 0.6 mm). A tool left on the wall drops into the seat when released, or falls over (D5 clip, seed 1).
  3. Release. Each pad moves straight out from the tool to 6 mm clear, then the palm rises by the press depth plus 5 mm. The press leaves the palm plate bearing on the tool’s top (60 N on D5), and the middle pad, which cannot reach the open radius at its station, stays pinned at 36 N until that load is removed; lifted without the unload, it drags the tool over.
  4. Re-pose. The palm goes straight up until no fingertip can reach below the tool’s top, then levels over the gait pose. At the press the palm is 23–35° off level on the D5 and D6 policies. From there the relay handover’s best ring, on the pads’ own azimuths at the best height, is 5–24 mm out of reach at the grip radius and 12–21 mm at the open radius, and its gait turns the tool 0.00 rev (runs/relay/). A palm that moves while two pads hold the seated tool pulls it out of the seat (the slide handover of 2026-09-20 lifted it from 53 to 82–96 mm). The tool therefore stands alone in the countersink for 6.4 s between the release and the descent.
  5. Ring. Placed per hand by real_v1_gait_ring_screen.py (below), solved on the released tool and centred on its axis at the ring height. The first entry of each radius is solved from two IK seeds; the sequential warm start alone left D6’s approach ring 10 mm short, and its index pad came down on the tool’s rim.
  6. Descent. The fingers open to the approach ring (16 mm clear of the tool) at the top and the palm waits until every joint is within 2° of its command before it descends. With a fixed settle the fingers were up to 53° short of the approach pose and landed on the tool.
  7. Gait. Twist 2 s, release, return and regrasp 1.5 s each, 20° stroke, 5 mm squeeze, all three pads released together, and a host-side boost on the achieved joint angle (command = set-point + 2 × shortfall, rate-limited) armed at the ring grasp. Without the boost the pads move 0.1–1.2° of a 20–25° commanded twist and the regrasp turns the tool 7–8° back each cycle. At a 30° stroke the grip ring is 5.2–5.8 mm out of reach and the gait knocked the tool over on two of four policies; at 20° it is 4.5–4.8 mm and all four turned the tool. In the completed rollouts above the tool turns a median 11.8° per cycle (10th–90th percentile 5.6–21.3°) on two or three pads, against the 34° a slip-free 20° stroke delivers through the 1.68 gear of the pad ring.

The calibrated plant’s fingers respond in about one second

The calibrated plant sets the finger servos to kp 0.5 and kv 0.02 but keeps the joint damping of the template (assets/mjcf/real_v1/real_hand.xml, default class ctrl: damping 0.5 N·m·s/rad), which apply_measured_plant.py never changed. The finger time constant is (0.5 + 0.02) / 0.5 = 1.04 s. The 2026-09-16 page computed it as kv / kp and concluded 40 ms. The bench bounds the servo far below 1 s: on the 2026-08-29 stream run the flexion joints tracked a 1.08 s trajectory to under 1°, and in the stepped free-air run the pip follows 0.13 s steps to within 0.5°. Every calibrated-plant result since 2026-09-16, including the training of these policies, ran with the 1 s fingers.

{{TABLE_CALFAST}}

At damping 0 the chain fails before the seat on every hand whether or not the fingers turn the tool, including the case where the arm alone stands the gripped tool up, which the damped plant did on five hands on 2026-09-16. The damping is doing two things in the model: it slows the response to a command about 25× past the servo’s, and it resists the tool back-driving the fingers. The real gear train resists back-driving through friction that grows with the transmitted load (the 2026-09-02 sysid measured a 12.3° friction cone under a grasp and 0.7–1.5° hanging free), which a constant frictionloss cannot express. Neither plant reproduces both measurements, so the chain’s dynamics, and the policies trained on them, are not validated for hardware.

Corrections to the 2026-09-20 page

  • “All three keep the tool through the relay handover and the eight gait cycles”: D5 ended the gait with no pad on the tool, standing in the seat, and D6 clip’s tool sat on the countersink wall through the whole gait. The “within 1.5 mm of the socket” figures were measured at the end of the run, not at the set-down.
  • “The first non-zero turn on the calibrated plant” (+0.071 rev in 8 cycles): withdrawn; it is 2–6° per cycle in a gait that does not track its commands.
  • “The achieved-angle loop is falsified”: true of a loop armed while the servos sit on their force ceiling. Armed at the ring grasp, the same loop is what makes the gait above turn.
  • “The gait does not move the fingers on this plant”: the cause is the 1 s finger time constant against 0.12–0.24 s gait phases, not the load.
  • The calibrated plant described as kp 0.5, kv 0.02 on that page and on 2026-09-16 carries joint damping 0.5 as well.

What this does not settle

  1. The finger dynamics. On the bench, per joint: a 20° free-air step read at 111 Hz (time constant and no-load speed), and a pad pushed with a force gauge while the servo holds position (deflection against time and against force). The first sets the command response, the second the resistance to back-driving; fit joint damping and friction to both, and write a load-dependent friction if a constant one cannot match them. The present plant is falsified if the free-air step settles in under 0.2 s, which the 2026-08-29 logs already suggest.
  2. The reorientation on the corrected plant. Continue each 2026-09-20 checkpoint on the fitted plant (20 M steps, about 40 minutes per job) and rerun this chain. No policy on this page is a hardware candidate before that.
  3. Gait timing. The 2 s twist and 1.5 s phases are sized for the 1 s fingers; on a faster plant they shorten, and the boost gain has to be re-chosen.
  4. Rates. Five spawns per policy cannot separate a weak policy from an unlucky pose (seed 3 costs three policies their turn). Twenty spawns per policy for the D5/D6 policies, same setting.
  5. D1 and D2 after the turn. Both lose the tool on two pads in the regrip or the staging; the ring screen places their gait rings at 4.4 and 3.2 mm, so their handover and gait are untested.
  6. The screw. The countersink is a friction brake with no thread and no torsional load (screw_torque 0): the turns are the tool’s rotation in the seat.
