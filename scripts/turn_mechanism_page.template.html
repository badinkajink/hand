Reorientation mechanism on three plants

Every finger-driven reorientation the simulator has produced ran on a rigid, instantaneous actuator. On the actuator the bench calibrated, the fingers turn a gripped tool 1–8° on every hand at every setting tried; the bench maneuver the hardware has run 251 times is a 20–40° tip of a tool resting on the pads at its own weight; and the chain’s 90° is the arm’s, with the tool held on three pads at 1.3–3.5 N on eight of sixteen hands. The 2026-09-06 chain, the rv05 chain and the eight-hand ranking each measured a different plant.

What each plant does on each maneuver

the same hands, the same plans; only the actuator model changes

Three maneuvers have been called “reorientation” in this program. The bench maneuver is the deployed plan replayed on a fixed palm: grip, the plan’s own per-step trajectory, hold. The chain’s finger turn is a contact-fixed IK rotation of a lifted tool about a pivot between the driver pads. The chain’s arm turn rotates the whole hand with the tool gripped. The table gives, for each, the grip force at the start, how far the tool turns, and whether it is still carried at the end.

{{TABLE_THREE}}

The shipped plant and the hardware agree on the bench maneuver because that maneuver is carried by contact geometry at a load near the tool’s weight, where actuator stiffness hardly enters. They disagree on the chain because the chain’s grasp and finger turn load the actuators at 3–7 N, which the rigid plant supplies and the servo does not. The calibrated plant reverses the loss: it cannot roll a gripped tool with the fingers, and it holds one through the arm’s rotation at 1.3–3.5 N, where the rigid plant at the plans’ 10 mm squeeze either releases it (10 of 16) or cradles it at 0.3–0.7 N (6 of 16).

The bench maneuver is a cradle tip

rv05_manual_b85, deployed plan, tool on the 100 mm post, palm fixed

The per-step trace of the plan replay on the shipped and calibrated plants. Pad force is the sum over the three pads; the tool weighs 0.240 N.

{{CHART_TRACE}}

On both plants the grip pose drives the tool up off the post (23 mm on the shipped plant, 7 mm on the calibrated one) and the pad force is 2–3.5 N before the turn starts. Within the first quarter of the turn the pad force drops to 0.1–0.5 N on one or two pads, and the tool rides the fingers at its own weight for the rest of the maneuver. On the shipped plant the index pad leaves the tool at step 100 and never returns; the tool rests on the thumb and middle pads. The rotation is the fingers changing the shape of the cradle the tool rests in, and the “settle” after the last command is the same motion continuing under gravity.

Removing the post after the grip changes nothing (the post carries 0.00 N at every step). Removing gravity on the calibrated plant leaves the tool floating out of the fingers with zero pad contact at the grip: the grip pose does not pinch the tool, gravity holds it in.

{{TABLE_MECH}}

The hardware’s grip during this maneuver is in the same regime. The bench regrip walks each finger to a servo-load band (the station’s regulator at 250 units; the 2026-08-29 held run at 435 / 210 / 0), and the sysid’s 0.018° per load unit at kp 0.5 puts one load unit at 1.6×10−4 N·m, so 250 units is about 0.8 N at the pad and the plan’s own un-relieved grip (525 / 350 / 210) about 3.4 N in total. Under that un-relieved grip the middle yaw stalled at 4° of the 12.5° asked; relieved, it travelled 13° and the operator saw ~15° of tool rotation. The hardware turns the tool when the grip is light enough for the tool to move in it, as the simulator does on both plants.

The chain on the shipped plant jams the tool and tosses it

D3, D5, D6, D7 · 10 mm squeeze · pivot 0.15 and 0.25 · two seeds each

The chain’s grasp commands the pads 10 mm inside the shaft (the plans’ own value). At kp 30 the fingers reach that command: the shaft is wedged upward until it escapes over the pads and rests against the palm plate, and the lifted seam reads two or three pads at 0.3–0.9 N against 0.240 N of tool. The finger turn then slides the tool 10–24 mm along the pads (the hardware’s ejection separator is 20 mm) while turning it 24–36°. The staged seam, after the arm’s rotation, reads cos +0.98–1.00 with zero pads at zero force on 10 of the 16 rollouts: the tool has fallen tip-first into the countersink and is standing on its own. The other six read two or three pads at 0.3–0.7 N, a cradle at the tool’s weight. That is the rv05 result of 2026-09-03 and its “release and swing”.

{{TABLE_CHAINKV}}

The 2026-09-06 chain that completed on D6 used a 2 mm squeeze, which at kp 30 is a 6.9 N three-pad grip; the rigid fingers rolled that gripped tool 90° and gaited it. The hardware cannot do either half: under a comparable grip its yaw servos stall at 12.6° with the load register at 855–915 and the protective trip at ~800. The 2026-09-06 pivot and pole-flip mechanism is a property of kp 30.

The chain on the calibrated plant holds the tool through the arm’s turn

same cells; the fingers turn 1–8°; the arm does the 90°

At kp 0.5 the 10 mm squeeze is a spring preload rather than a position: the pads stop at the shaft and push with 3–4 N on three or four pads, the tool does not ride up, and it lifts flat (cos 0.00 ± 0.04). The finger turn moves it 1–8° with 1.4–3.7 mm of slide. The arm then rotates the hand and the staged seam reads cos +0.95–0.99 on three pads at 1.3–3.5 N on D3, D5, D6 and D7 at both pivots and both seeds. The damping (next section) changes none of these numbers by more than 0.03 in cos or 0.5 N.

Sixteen hands, and the knobs that were tried

The eight deployed hands and the eight set-B hands on the kp 0.5 / kv 0.02 plant, then the deployed eight under each variation: no finger turn at all (the arm alone), the driver pads relieved 4 and 8 mm during the turn (the bench’s mechanism, attempted inside the chain), each plan’s own screened turn (its angle, pivot and budget), and the kp 0.25 plant the settled re-fit implies. Cells count rollouts whose staged seam is above cos 0.85 on at least two pads at more than the tool’s weight.

{{TABLE_MATRIX}}

D2, the hardware’s 10-of-10 hand, loses a pad during the finger turn (3 pads at 3.65 N at the lift, 2 at 0.79 N after the turn) and drops at the arm’s rotation; with the finger turn removed it stages at cos +0.92 on three pads. D4 and D7 go the other way: without the 1–8° pre-turn they drop at the regrip. The relief never produces a cradle tip inside the chain; the turn stays at 1–8° at 4 and 8 mm on every hand. Each plan’s own screened turn inside the chain gives 4–15° and the same four carriers. On the kp 0.25 plant the lift force halves to 1.4–2.2 N, D2 and D7 slide 50–66 mm out of the grip during the finger turn, and only D6 reaches staged held.

The handover and gait remain the loss on every calibrated variant: the tool stands in the countersink held, the tripod never forms, and the gait turns 0.0–0.2 rev.

The calibrated plant’s speed was never measured

apply_measured_plant.py --kv defaults to 0.6; at kp 0.5 that is a 1.2 s time constant

apply_measured_plant.py documents where kp, forcerange, frictionloss and the masses come from. It does not mention kv, which defaults to 0.6. A position actuator with kp 0.5 and kv 0.6 driving a link of negligible inertia is a first-order lag with time constant kv / kp = 1.2 s. The hardware’s own logs bound the servo’s lag at under 40 ms: on the 2026-08-29 stream run the flexion joints tracked a 1.08 s trajectory to under 1°, and in the stepped free-air run the pip joint follows a ramp to within 0.5° at 0.13 s per step.

{{TABLE_HWLAG}}

The damping does not decide either maneuver. On the bench replay the turn is 29° at kv 0.6 and 24° at kv 0.01; on the chain the finger turn is 1–7° at kv 0.6 and 1–8° at kv 0.02, and the staged cosines differ by under 0.03. It does decide what the seam errors mean: at kv 0.6 the fingers sit 10–30° behind command at turned, at kv 0.02 they sit 8–21°, and the 8–21° is the spring deflection a 3 N grip produces at kp 0.5 (0.5 N·m/rad × 0.3 rad ≈ 0.15 N·m ≈ 3 N at a 50 mm pad). No actuator reaches the 0.35 N·m ceiling in any rollout.

{{TABLE_KV}}

The stiffness fit moves by 2× when the settle is real

The 2026-09-02 calibration matched the simulated ctrl − qpos after a 2 s hold to the bench’s settled deficits and found kp 0.5. At kv 0.6 a 2 s hold is 1.7 time constants, so 19 % of any step and the lag from the preceding ramp are still in the reading. Re-run with kv 0.02, kp 0.5 reproduces +5.8° of the measured +11.7° on middle_yaw, and the fit lands at kp 0.25.

{{TABLE_KPFIT}}

Both kp 0.25 and kp 0.5 at kv 0.02 reproduce the rv05 bench turn (40° and 22° against the bench’s 33°), so the bench maneuver cannot tell them apart. The chain can: the carry set is D3–D7 at kp 0.5 and D6 alone at kp 0.25. The plant’s stiffness is known to a factor of two, and the chain’s outcome sits inside that factor.

What this settles, and the next measurements

  • The finger-driven 90° turn is retired as a chain element. It exists only at kp ≥ 2 (the compliance band of 2026-09-02 says the same from the bench side). No relief, budget, pivot, angle or damping recovers it at kp 0.5 or 0.25.
  • The arm’s reorientation on the calibrated plant is the carry to test on hardware. D7 and D4 (the bench’s 10/10 and 9/10) and D3, D5, D6 hold it at kp 0.5; D2 holds it with the finger turn set to 0°. The bench measurement is a UR5e (or the gantry wrist) rotating a gripped tool 90° with the tag on the vane reading the tool’s axis; the prediction is retention on three pads and cos > 0.9 at the end of the move. Acceptance: five of these six hands retain across 5 trials. Falsification: D7 drops, which the kp 0.25 plant predicts and the kp 0.5 plant does not, and would fix the stiffness at the same time.
  • Measure kv. One free-air step response per joint at 111 Hz (the bus rate the 2026-09-02 latency fix allows), 20° step, read position until settled; fit the time constant and the no-load speed. Then re-fit kp with that kv via calibrate_plant_kp.py --kv. Until then the chain runs at kp 0.5 and kp 0.25 both, and a result that differs between them is not a result.
  • The handover and gait stay position sequences composed on the rigid plant. The achieved-angle loop named on the 2026-09-16 chain page is still the next control test, now with a specific regime: the tool stands in the countersink under its own weight, so the loads are below the grip’s and the ceiling is not the constraint it is during the turn. Run it on D6 at kp 0.5 and 0.25; accept if the tripod forms on 3 pads and the gait turns ≥ 0.5 rev.
  • Per-hand ranking on the bench maneuver is not a plant test. The seed spread under 4 mm of spawn jitter exceeds the between-hand spread on every plant. A ranking claim needs either the screened maneuver’s load regulator in the replay or a jitter matched to the bench’s placement repeatability (0.03 mm rms on the tag), and 20+ seeds.
