What changed in the plant, and why

The 2026-09-19 tranche (page) ended with every hand holding 64 of 64 in the benchmark and most of them dropping the tool inside the UR5e chain. Re-evaluating the same checkpoints with the tool spawned 3 mm and 10° off the trained pose gave the reason: training had spawned the tool at one pose (cube_spawn_xy_jitter 0), the evaluator inherited it, and the 64 rollouts were one rollout under 64 GPU contact solves. {{TABLE_CHAIN}}

After the turn: re-pose, set-down, relay handover, gait

The probe does not stop when the policy’s 5 s end: the fingers freeze at their last set-point and the chain continues with its own stages, the arm re-posing the palm so the shaft points down, lowering the shaft into the post’s hole, the relay handover (one finger re-grips at a time, the tool never released) and eight gait cycles. Each cell is the tool’s cos to vertical and its height at that seam; gaited is the last. The gait’s screw turns are the rotation the cycles put into the shaft; a chain that stands the tool and gaits 8 cycles at 0.00 turns is holding it without driving it.

The gait does not move the fingers on this plant. Finger commands and joint angles recorded every 60 sim steps through the D6 clip run at plate 0 (r_d6_clip_chain_pl0_ctrl_qpos.json): through the eight cycles the commanded angles vary by 5–27° per joint (the ring table is built around the tool’s actual axis, 31 mm of IK residual) and the achieved angles by 0.3–9°; the index pip sits 59° behind its command for the whole gait (commanded 88°, achieved 26–31°), the thumb and middle 3–15°. The tool stands in the hole under 20 N of pad force and turns −0.002 revolutions. This is the 2026-09-16 result on the calibrated chain (fingers 14–38° behind command through the gait, 0.00–0.02 rev) with the policy’s turn in front of it; the chain’s ok gate tests stance, grip and cycles run, not screw turns. The relay handover does move the fingers (commands change by 77–110° on the index, achieved 21–44°) and the tool stays at cos 0.99 through it.

Plate height: the chain scenes in assets/mjcf/experimental/20260904-chain_bothsets/ were generated with the palm plate at the mounting plane; the built hand carries it at +25 mm, which is where the training scenes put it. The probe moves the plate per run (plate_variant, a __plate25.xml beside the scene; only the plate geom moves), so the plate-25 rows are the built hand and the plate-0 rows the old geometry. The one film below is the plate-0 run; the plate-25 films are below it.

What decides the set-down is the angle the tool has when the arm has finished levelling it over the socket (staged). The tip sits 50 mm from the tool’s centre, so the tip’s lateral offset from the aimed centre is 50 mm × sin(angle) against a 6 mm capture radius: 8° is 7 mm and enters, 14° is 12 mm and lands on the annulus beside the hole. D6 clip at plate 0 staged at cos 0.99 (8°) and entered; at plate 25 the same policy staged at 0.97 (14°), the separation policy at 0.95 (18°) and D5 at 0.89 (27°), and in each film the tip comes down beside the hole. D6 clip then loses the tool at the handover grip (it tips off the post); D5 and the separation policy keep it in hand leaning on the post at 47–55° through the gait. The staging already iterates the palm against the measured tool pose (eight corrections), so the residual angle is what the compliant grip gives back under the arm’s re-pose.

{{CHAIN_VIDEOS}}

Aiming the tip at the seat instead of levelling the tool first

The residual angle does not have to close before the descent. The chain commands the tool’s pose and derives the palm’s from it (_rigid_palm_pose), and it already measures the tool’s low point every correction, so the staging and the descent can carry the apex over the socket rather than the body centre, and the lean can come out afterwards as a rotation about the seated tip with the floor carrying the weight (the _upright(about_foot) move the pivot order already uses). real_v1_chain_policy.py --seat-aim tip does that: _stage aims the measured apex at place_xy, _descend takes one aim on the apex at the top of its move, and a seated phase follows the set-down (eight corrections about the apex, walking it onto the socket centre). The shipped behaviour is --seat-aim centre, which is what every other chain row on this page ran.

On the three plate-25 policies that hold the turn, the set-down now lands the apex within 1.5 mm of the socket centre at the lean the grip gives it (15.5°, 30.5° and 12.9°), the seated correction takes the lean to 7.0°, 1.5° and 2.8°, and all three keep the tool through the relay handover and the eight gait cycles: D5 at 0.4°, D6 clip at 6.3° with 17.4 N on the pads, D6 with separation at 2.9° with 16.7 N. The chain’s ok passes at the built plate height for the first time. The turns column is unchanged at 0.00: the gait’s achieved-angle problem is the next item and this change does not touch it. Rows are tilt / tool centre z (mm; the seated centre is at 49.5) / pads on the tool at each seam.

Training pipeline: architecture and the decisions behind it

A policy on this page is a 9-dimensional residual on a scripted grasp-and-lift, trained by PPO on 3072 copies of one hand’s frozen scene in MuJoCo-Warp through mjlab, and warm-started from one D6 run of 2026-09-17. This section is the whole of that pipeline as it stands on 2026-09-20: the stages from the hardware model to a checkpoint, one episode, what the actor reads and writes, the reward and the terminations, the optimiser, the warm-start lineage, and a register of every setting with the reason on record and whether that reason is a measurement. The tables are read from the run configuration of the first finished job, so their numbers are the tranche’s own.

Stages, from the hardware model to a checkpoint

  1. Hand. assets/mjcf/real_v1/real_hand.xml is the CAD-matched three-finger hand; real_v1_design_search.py draws mount coordinates and link lengths from a Sobol sequence inside REAL_V1_WORKSPACE, filters on servo range, gantry travel and finger clearance, and the eight promoted hands (D1–D8) were built and run on the bench. The morphology is frozen into the scene (freeze_scene_for_eval) so no morphology joint drifts during a rollout.
  2. Grasp. The chain fitter places the three pads on the shaft at or below its equator and writes the open_ik keyframe (finger joints with the pads just off the shaft) and the grip anchor (the same pose closed by a 10 mm commanded squeeze). There is no CEM on these scenes; the anchor is the deployed plan’s own grip.
  3. Plant. apply_measured_plant.py rewrites the scene to the servo sysid of 2026-09-02 (finger kp 0.5, kv 0.02, forcerange 0.35 N·m, frictionloss 0.0035, measured link masses), the elliptic friction cone with impratio 10, and the +25 mm palm plate the bench carries. The result is the _cal morphology run (results/phase1/real_v1/20260916-<hand>_cal), which also holds the open-loop reference rollout of that grip and lift (best_rollout.npz) that the tracking terms and the ref_* observations use.
  4. Tool. make_tipmesh_morphology_run.py derives the _cal_tip run: the 25 mm screwdriver shaft with the screw-tip mesh the chain and the bench carry (25.6 g). This tranche is the first to train on it.
  5. Environment. morphohand.rl.env_build turns the frozen scene into an mjlab task: the hand as the robot entity, the tool as an object entity that keeps the scene’s contact class and mesh assets, the scene’s own floor (--scene-floor), 2 ms physics, control at 50 Hz. Observations, rewards, terminations and reset events are the managers listed below; the finger action term is the eased set-point plus the residual, the palm action term is the scripted lift.
  6. Optimiser. rl_train_cube.py --recipe b_liveA loads the blessed reorient recipe (the B10 reward block, the deploy-parity residual scale and easing, the contact gate, the relaxed slip guards) and the CLI adds the run-specific knobs; the trainer is rsl_rl PPO with the collapse watchdog attached. A warm start copies the parent’s actor and critic tensor by tensor.
  7. Evaluation. The checkpoint is scored on 64 nominal and 64 jittered GPU rollouts, replayed on the CPU solver under the transfer variants, run through the UR5e chain at both plates, and filmed; the numbers on this page are those, never the reward.

{{TABLE_EVAL}}

Inherited settings and the measurement that would settle each

  • Tip-lost 15 steps. One hand (D6), two 20 M continuations from the same checkpoint at 3 and 15 consecutive steps, jittered hold and chain at both plates. The 3-step value ends a turn in which a pad lifts for 0.1 s; the 15-step value lets a two-pad pinch persist for 0.3 s.
  • The B10 reward block (100 / 300 / 15 / −8). A one-factor sweep of the progress weight (100, 300, 900) on D6 at the same budget, judged on jittered hold and end cos; the progress term is the one that pays for turning past 60°.
  • Friction DR band [0.55, 1.15]. μ 0.6 sits inside the band and still drops D1/D2 64/64 (2026-09-19); a band [0.4, 1.4] on one hand tells whether width helps or whether the policy relocates to the band’s middle as the compliance DR did.
  • Observation noise (±0.01 rad, ±0.5 rad/s, ±5 mm). The bench servo telemetry and the tag tracker have their own error (0.03 mm rms on the tag; joint positions in commanded units against a 0.018°/load-unit spring): the noise should be set from those, and a run with the tracker’s measured latency in the observation delay slot is the deploy-parity check.
  • Residual scale 0.5 rad and clip 2.0. The bound is stated in policy units; a scale-1.0 clip-1.0 run gives the same ±1 rad bound with a different exploration std and would show whether the bound or the std carried the 2026-09-19 gain.
  • 20 M steps on the harder plant. One 60 M continuation of r_d6_clipsep (the first item under Next steps).

Lessons from the two tranches

  • Evaluate off the trained pose. A deterministic evaluator on a zero-jitter environment measures the GPU solver, not the policy. policy_eval_suite.py --spawn-jitter-mm 3 --spawn-yaw-deg 10 --friction-dr is now the number a policy is judged on, and the driver writes it for every job (*_eval_j3dr.json).
  • The training plant was not the calibrated plant. Three defects between the frozen scene and the mjlab environment (tool contact class, floor, tip) had been there since the first mjlab run; the CPU replay, the chain and the bench comparison all ran on the scene. Any earlier sim-to-sim gap is confounded by them.
  • Reading the arm. The 4.9 mm the palm sat below its command at the seam was the menagerie servo settling (slow pole kp/kv = 5 rad/s), not load: with the payload declared a UR5e holds ~0.1 mm, and the chain now runs the arm at ten times the position gain.
  • Two silent no-ops. The friction variants of the zero-shot table were copies of the base scene on every calibrated scene until 2026-09-20 02:00 (the variant matched the template’s μ 2.4), and --payload-gravcomp had been compensating the finger links as well as the hand. Both fixed.
  • The separation term. −10 below 30 mm buys 2–10 mm of index–middle clearance on D1/D3/D5/D6 and takes the turn away from D4 and D7, which turn by running the two fingers together; on the matched plant the continued policies sit at 16–29 mm without it.
  • The chain’s ok is a hold, not a gait. The gate tests stance, grip and cycles run; on the calibrated plant the gait’s commands move the fingers 0.3–9° (index pip 59° behind its command) and put −0.002 turns into the screw. A chain that reads ok has stood the tool and held it for eleven seconds.
  • The seat has a capture radius, and aiming the body centre spends it on the lean. 50 mm of tip lever turns 8° into 7 mm (enters) and 14° into 12 mm (misses); every plate-25 chain that held the turn staged at 14–27° and missed the hole when the body centre was the aim point. Aiming the measured apex and taking the lean out about the seated tip lands all three within 1.5 mm and hands over at 0.4–6.3°; the lean at the end of the turn costs nothing at the seat once the trajectory is written for the tip.
  • Unattended follow-ups. Two of three scheduled session wake-ups never fired; the page build has to live in the detached driver (or a watcher beside it), not in the session.

Next steps

  1. Finish the queue and rank on the jittered hold (remaining jobs listed in queue.json); the bench candidate is the hand whose continued policy holds ≥ 60 of 64 jittered and holds the chain at both plate heights.
  2. Turn quality on the matched plant. The continued policies end at cos 0.82–0.83 where their parents ended at 0.92–0.96: 20 M steps of continuation on a harder tool contact and a heavier tip is not yet converged, or the jitter costs turn. One 60 M continuation of the D6 separation job decides which.
  3. The achieved-joint-angle loop on the handover and gait commands (the 2026-09-16 measurement, still open): the gait commands 5–27° per joint and the calibrated servos deliver 0.3–9°, so the ring the gait plans is never the ring the fingers make. Close the loop on the measured joint angles (command = target + gain × (target − achieved), rate-limited) in probe_real_v1_chain’s gait and handover; accept q_err < 10° through handover_* and ≥ 0.5 rev in 8 cycles on the three tip-aimed plate-25 runs. The tip-aimed set-down (done above) is the precondition; the seat capture is no longer the blocker.
  4. Make --seat-aim tip the driver’s default for the next tranche (the remaining rows of this one keep centre so the table stays comparable), and run it across the spawn-jitter seeds (--jitter, --seed on the probe) to get a rate rather than three single rollouts.

  8. Clearance threshold from a caliper on the built index and middle housings, then the separation weight sweep (−3 … −10) on D4 and D7 only.
