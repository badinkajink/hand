Corrections, 2026-09-23

The calibrated plant keeps the template’s finger joint damping (0.5 N·m·s/rad), so at kp 0.5 the fingers respond with a 1.04 s time constant; the bench servo is far faster. Every policy on this page trained on it, and with the damping removed all ten drop the tool in their turn. The chain analysis this page carried after the turn (seat, handover, gait) is superseded by the 2026-09-23 page, which also lists the statements below that it corrects.

Training pipeline: architecture and the decisions behind it

A policy on this page is a 9-dimensional residual on a scripted grasp-and-lift, trained by PPO on 3072 copies of one hand’s frozen scene in MuJoCo-Warp through mjlab and warm-started from one D6 run of 2026-09-17. The tables below are read from the first finished job’s run configuration.

Stages, from the hardware model to a checkpoint

  1. Hand. assets/mjcf/real_v1/real_hand.xml is the CAD-matched three-finger hand; real_v1_design_search.py draws mount coordinates and link lengths from a Sobol sequence inside REAL_V1_WORKSPACE, filters on servo range, gantry travel and finger clearance, and the eight promoted hands (D1–D8) were built and run on the bench. The morphology is frozen into the scene (freeze_scene_for_eval) so no morphology joint drifts during a rollout.
  2. Grasp. The chain fitter places the three pads on the shaft at or below its equator and writes the open_ik keyframe (finger joints with the pads just off the shaft) and the grip anchor (the same pose closed by a 10 mm commanded squeeze). There is no CEM on these scenes; the anchor is the deployed plan’s own grip.
  3. Plant. apply_measured_plant.py rewrites the scene to the servo sysid of 2026-09-02 (finger kp 0.5, kv 0.02, forcerange 0.35 N·m, frictionloss 0.0035, measured link masses; the template’s joint damping 0.5 is left in place, a 1 s finger time constant), the elliptic friction cone with impratio 10, and the +25 mm palm plate the bench carries. The result is the _cal morphology run (results/phase1/real_v1/20260916-<hand>_cal), which also holds the open-loop reference rollout of that grip and lift (best_rollout.npz) that the tracking terms and the ref_* observations use.
  4. Tool. make_tipmesh_morphology_run.py derives the _cal_tip run: the 25 mm screwdriver shaft with the screw-tip mesh the chain and the bench carry (25.6 g). This tranche is the first to train on it.
  5. Environment. morphohand.rl.env_build turns the frozen scene into an mjlab task: the hand as the robot entity, the tool as an object entity that keeps the scene’s contact class and mesh assets, the scene’s own floor (--scene-floor), 2 ms physics, control at 50 Hz. Observations, rewards, terminations and reset events are the managers listed below; the finger action term is the eased set-point plus the residual, the palm action term is the scripted lift.
  6. Optimiser. rl_train_cube.py --recipe b_liveA loads the blessed reorient recipe (the B10 reward block, the deploy-parity residual scale and easing, the contact gate, the relaxed slip guards) and the CLI adds the run-specific knobs; the trainer is rsl_rl PPO with the collapse watchdog attached. A warm start copies the parent’s actor and critic tensor by tensor.
  7. Evaluation. The checkpoint is scored on 64 nominal and 64 jittered GPU rollouts, replayed on the CPU solver under the transfer variants, run through the UR5e chain at both plates, and filmed; the numbers on this page are those, never the reward.

{{TABLE_EVAL}}

Inherited settings and the measurement that would settle each

  • Tip-lost 15 steps. One hand (D6), two 20 M continuations from the same checkpoint at 3 and 15 consecutive steps, jittered hold and chain at both plates. The 3-step value ends a turn in which a pad lifts for 0.1 s; the 15-step value lets a two-pad pinch persist for 0.3 s.
  • The B10 reward block (100 / 300 / 15 / −8). A one-factor sweep of the progress weight (100, 300, 900) on D6 at the same budget, judged on jittered hold and end cos; the progress term is the one that pays for turning past 60°.
  • Friction DR band [0.55, 1.15]. μ 0.6 sits inside the band and still drops D1/D2 64/64 (2026-09-19); a band [0.4, 1.4] on one hand tells whether width helps or whether the policy relocates to the band’s middle as the compliance DR did.
  • Observation noise (±0.01 rad, ±0.5 rad/s, ±5 mm). The bench servo telemetry and the tag tracker have their own error (0.03 mm rms on the tag; joint positions in commanded units against a 0.018°/load-unit spring): the noise should be set from those, and a run with the tracker’s measured latency in the observation delay slot is the deploy-parity check.
  • Residual scale 0.5 rad and clip 2.0. The bound is stated in policy units; a scale-1.0 clip-1.0 run gives the same ±1 rad bound with a different exploration std and would show whether the bound or the std carried the 2026-09-19 gain.
  • 20 M steps on the harder plant. One 60 M continuation of r_d6_clipsep (the first item under Next steps).

Lessons from the two tranches

  • Evaluate off the trained pose. A deterministic evaluator on a zero-jitter environment measures the GPU solver. policy_eval_suite.py --spawn-jitter-mm 3 --spawn-yaw-deg 10 --friction-dr is the number a policy is judged on, and the driver writes it for every job (*_eval_j3dr.json).
  • Four differences between the training plant and the bench. The tool’s contact class, the floor and the tip had differed between the mjlab environment and the scene since the first mjlab run. The finger joint damping is shared by both and differs from the servo: a 1 s time constant that the policies rely on.
  • The arm’s settling. The 4.9 mm the palm sat below its command at the turn was the menagerie servo settling (kp/kv = 5 rad/s). A UR5e told its payload holds about 0.1 mm, and the chain runs the arm at ten times the position gain.
  • Two silent no-ops. The friction variants of the zero-shot table were copies of the base scene on every calibrated scene until 2026-09-20 02:00 (the variant matched the template’s μ 2.4), and --payload-gravcomp compensated the finger links as well as the hand. Both fixed.
  • The separation term. −10 below 30 mm buys 2–10 mm of index–middle clearance on D1, D3, D5 and D6 and removes the turn on D4 and D7, which turn by running the two fingers together.
  • Unattended follow-ups. Two of three scheduled session wake-ups never fired; the page build lives in a watcher beside the detached driver.

Next steps

  1. Measure the finger dynamics on the bench (free-air step and a loaded back-drive per joint; the 2026-09-23 page names both), fit the plant to them, and continue these checkpoints on it. No policy here is a hardware candidate before that.
  2. Turn quality. The continued policies end their turns at cos 0.80–0.96. One 60 M continuation of the D6 separation job on the fitted plant separates convergence from what the jitter costs.
  3. D4. Both draws stopped on NaN observations (iterations 9 and 63) on the g12 tip-mesh scene; inspect the D4 pads against the tip mesh at spawn before another D4 job.
  4. Driver hygiene. Apply --seed in rl_train_cube.py (a dead flag: every run is an unseeded draw) and pass --video to the driver’s chain call.
  5. The bench controller. Servo telemetry and the AprilTag pose at 50 Hz into the 66-dim observation, the residual onto the finger set-points, after the tracker’s x/y heading calibration.
  6. The privileged-critic blind actor (proprioception, motor load and history; critic with the pose), once a sighted policy transfers.
  7. Clearance from a caliper on the built index and middle housings, then the separation weight (−3 … −10) on D4 and D7 only.
  8. The friction rows of the 2026-09-17 D6 page re-run under the fixed variant.
