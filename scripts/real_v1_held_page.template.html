So the 89° of turn that a previous version of this page reported as the result is the tilt of a tool the hand has let go of. Adding the load test — two pads carrying more than 1 N — leaves {{HELD}} of {{NALL}}. This is the same defect as reading a peak alignment without a final height, and as a dropped shaft reading vertical because it fell into a countersink; the guard is now in chain() as held_reorient, and reorient_ok requires it.

Three things the maneuver does not do

  1. It does not put a tip down. tip_len_mm = 0.0 on every run, so the object is a plain cylinder and the tool axis is folded — standing on either end scores tilt_deg = 0. Which end finishes downward was never measured.
  2. It does not use the countersink. place_xy = None on every run. The tool stands on flat table, and {{BELOW}} of the {{NSTOOD}} stands end below the table top — knocked off it.
  3. It does not relay. reindex = "full" sets the tool down, releases it, moves the palm, and closes again. There is no transfer from the reorient grasp to the gait grasp; reindex = "relay" exists and was not what ran.

The two losses pull in opposite directions. A 2 mm squeeze puts the tool where it was and loses 24 runs in the carry; a 6 mm squeeze carries the tool and loses 32 at the handover. The chain rate peaks at 2 mm ({{OK2}}) and 4 mm ({{OK4}}), and choosing the squeeze per hand rather than for the population is worth {{BEST}}. Two sweeps looked for the handover: scanning the gait palm height against the open ring instead of the closed one is worse (21 chains against 44 over 128 runs per arm), and opening the pads wider before the descent changes the handover count by exactly zero at 6, 11, 16 and 22 mm — the open ring's IK residual grows one-for-one with the target, so the fingers are already as open as they go.

Why 10 mm of squeeze ejects the tool

Every deployed plan carries squeeze_mm = 10.0. That drives the pad centres to r_obj + r_pad + gap − squeeze = 12.5 + 10.55 + 1 − 10 = 14.05 mm from the shaft axis, where contact needs 23.05 — nine millimetres inside the surface. The number was fitted on a bench scene where the shaft floats at 100 mm on a post that takes the reaction. On a table nothing takes it, so the pinch drives the tool up and out.

Measured with the palm held still and the tool lying on the table, g12_b095 rises +3.8, +8.9, +12.8 and +18.6 mm at 2, 4, 6 and 10 mm of squeeze while its pad force falls 10.4, 5.4, 1.8, 0.5 N — the force falls because the shaft has escaped over the top of the pads. Nine of sixteen hands rise more than their own 12.5 mm radius at 10 mm and finish riding on thumb_pip_frame.

The fitter's own hold probe cannot catch this. It measures retained lift against the object's height before the close, so a tool thrown 18 mm upward reads as 18 mm of successful lift — held ≈ lift_probe + dz is an identity. Grasps “passing” a 20 mm gate at 65–75 mm on a 50 mm probe passed by the amount they ejected the tool. _grip_from_fit then passes hold_min=−1.0 and discards the gate anyway.

What would have to change

  1. The turn has to be carried by the fingers, not the table. Requiring two pads above 1 N at upright is now held_reorient in chain() and reorient_ok depends on it. Any maneuver proposed for this task has to clear that gate before its tilt is quoted; on this one it is {{HELD}}/{{NALL}}, so the maneuver is not a candidate.
  2. The close probe is a gate that is not being applied. close_nonpad ≥ 2 selects exactly the hands that eject the tool (sv1_w6689_b050, _b060 at every squeeze, sv1_u5855_b050 at 2 mm), and those hands account for 0/16 and 0/4. Applying it in prepare() removes them from the denominator on evidence rather than by hand.
  3. The squeeze optimum is bracketed but not resolved. The carry wants more and the handover wants less; 2 mm and 4 mm are within two runs of each other over 64. Per-hand selection is worth {{BEST}}/{{N}} against {{OK2}}/{{N}}, on n=4 per cell, which does not separate a 3-run difference from noise. Eight seeds per cell on the surviving hands would.
  4. The task needs a tip, a socket and a relay, and none of the three is in these runs. tip_len_mm = 0 makes the axis foldable so either end down scores the same; place_xy = None removes the countersink; and reindex = "full" releases rather than transferring the grasp. The relay path (reindex = "relay", 0.4% of the task uncontacted against 49%) already exists and has never been run against this maneuver.
  5. The gait gain is not guarded against the gear ceiling. The pad ring is a 1.684 gear, so a 30° stroke cannot exceed 50.5° per cycle; gain_mean_deg reads −67 to −86 on g12 and +188 on u5860. Those are the shaft being spun or thrown, and they currently read as fast gaits.
