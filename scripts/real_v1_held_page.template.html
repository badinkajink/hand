The maneuver

scripts/real_v1_chain_hands.py --stand table

The fingers are never asked to turn the tool. The hand closes on the screwdriver lying on the table, lifts it, puts it back down lying, and then stands it up by pivoting it about its own foot: eight rigid palm transfers, each computed against the measured tool pose, each rotating the tool about the point it is resting on. The table carries the weight throughout. Once the tool is standing it supports itself, so the palm can leave, re-index to the pose the gait study validated, close on the tripod ring, and gait.

On g12_b095, seed 0: stood at 0.09°, pressed to 0.00°, ring taken on three pads at 41.1 N, six gait cycles at −36.4° each — 72% of the 1.684-gear ceiling — and the tool 11.7° off vertical when the chain ends.

Why the table and not the arm

A 90° rigid reorientation in mid-air drops the tool in every variant measured: the fingers turning it, the arm turning it, tip-up, tip-down, on the UR5e and on a floating palm. The cause is statics rather than control. Steady-state grip on a free object held by position servos is whatever force balance with the object's weight demands, and it scales with mass on an identical command — 0.33 N on the 24.5 g tool, 9.84 N at 393 g, 83.6 N at 1571 g. The 41 N at closure is a transient. Nothing in the controller reaches this: commanded squeeze, re-squeeze, a servo-load loop, a contact-force loop and a station-referenced re-grasp all move a quantity that statics fixes.

So giving up the reorientation-by-rolling does not by itself help. The roll was not what was failing; the hold was, and only a reaction surface changes it — which is what every maneuver that works in this program already has.

It is a guided pivot, not a rigid carry

The tool rotates 70.6° inside the grasp during the stand-up. That is measured directly now: every seam carries roll_deg and slide_mm, the tool's rotation and translation in the palm's frame since the grasp settled, which separates alignment the hand delivered from alignment the tool helped itself to. A rigid carry reads zero on both however far the arm travels. The floor does the work; the hand guides; the feedback lands it.

What the probe was reporting wrong

four defects, all surfaced by taking the finger turn out

A screwdriver standing on its handle read as a perfect 0.00° stand. Every tilt in the chain probe was arccos|cos| and every axis was folded to point up — right for a plain cylinder, wrong for a tool whose tip is one specific end. With the rotation left to the arm, the staging transfer picks the end to raise from the sign of R[2,2], which on a horizontal tool is numerical noise. The run then reported tilt 0.00°, z 0.0500 m, stood_ok True for a tool upside down on the annulus beside the socket.

Rigid transfers were interpolated in joint space. For the small corrections the published chain makes that is the same path. For a 90° reorientation it is not: the arm swings the hand through an arc the command never named, and reports an 0.008 mm IK residual saying it went exactly where it was told.

The gait's ring was solved on fixed world bearings. Thumb at π, index at π/3 is the tripod a palm looking down at a standing tool makes. A palm that stood the tool up by pivoting is a quarter turn from there, and the canonical ring is 14.0 mm outside the fingers' reach; the pads then either hover at 0.02 N or arrive with that residual commanded as interference, at 57–91 N, and shove the tool 17–26° over.

The gait's palm height was the fit's grip depth. The fit reports the height that pinches a tool lying down — 64.5 mm on this hand. The gait needs the height from which three fingers close on a ring around a standing one, 45 mm. At 64.5 the ring solves 13.0 mm out of reach and the close knocks the tool over; scanned per hand, the residual is 0.43 mm.

What this does not settle

  1. The handover is the entire remaining failure. Thirty-six stands, two chains. The tool is standing free and stable when the palm move starts and on the floor by the time the ring closes. The next measurement is which phase of the re-index — release, palm move, or close — loses it, per seed, on the ten hands that stand on every seed. Two variants are already excluded: closing one finger at a time after the palm move (--reindex regrip) gives 0/4 against 1/4, because three Cartesian approaches past a free-standing tool are three chances to knock it down; and reducing the ring's commanded interference (--squeeze 0.5 / 1.0 / 2.0 mm over five hands × four seeds) leaves every hand at its own value.
  2. The countersink, and the tool's heading on the bench. A screwdriver cannot stand tip-down on a flat table, so the seated version has to arrive at the socket. On the heading the scenes ship with, pivoting the foot into the socket stalls at 34° — the same tip-down rotation the mid-air stage could not carry. Laid down the other way round (one quaternion on the object body, prepare(h, yaw_deg=180)) the same move stands the tool tip-down at 8.79° with only 10.8° of roll in the grasp. It then sits 10 mm proud of its seat and the handover topples it. Seat it the last 10 mm and re-run the handover there: a tool in a 45° cone is not free-standing, and continuous contact has already been measured to buy everything in a seat and nothing on a plane.
  3. Five hands fail upstream of all of this. sv1_w6689_b060, sv1_w6689_b050, sv1_u5860_b070, sv1_w7583_b130 and sv1_w2360_b075 drop the tool at the grasp, with 147–167° of roll and no pad on it for the whole run. Their fitted grasp does not hold a tool lying on a table; check the fit before reading anything else about them.
  4. The press is a cliff, not a knob. Same hand, same seed, 2 / 6 / 10 mm: the tool is thrown, then six cycles at 15.6°, then six cycles at 12.5°. The gait study's own window was −6 to +6 mm for a shaft already standing. Where the top of that range comes from, and whether it is the same on hardware where the table is not a rigid plane, is unmeasured.
