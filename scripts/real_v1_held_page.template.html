The reorientation is not where the runs are lost

Splitting the chain's gates apart puts the 90° turn on one side and everything downstream of it on the other. When a hand stands the tool it stands it at {{REO}}° of the 90, with a standard deviation of {{REOSD}}° across every hand and every squeeze. The attrition is at the two ends: the carry that delivers the tool to the table, and the handover that gives the standing tool back to the gait ring.

The two losses pull in opposite directions. A 2 mm squeeze puts the tool where it was and loses 24 runs in the carry; a 6 mm squeeze carries the tool and loses 32 at the handover. The chain rate peaks at 2 mm ({{OK2}}) and 4 mm ({{OK4}}), and choosing the squeeze per hand rather than for the population is worth {{BEST}}.

Why 10 mm of squeeze ejects the tool

Every deployed plan carries squeeze_mm = 10.0. That drives the pad centres to r_obj + r_pad + gap − squeeze = 12.5 + 10.55 + 1 − 10 = 14.05 mm from the shaft axis, where contact needs 23.05 — nine millimetres inside the surface. The number was fitted on a bench scene where the shaft floats at 100 mm on a post that takes the reaction. On a table nothing takes it, so the pinch drives the tool up and out.

Measured with the palm held still and the tool lying on the table, g12_b095 rises +3.8, +8.9, +12.8 and +18.6 mm at 2, 4, 6 and 10 mm of squeeze while its pad force falls 10.4, 5.4, 1.8, 0.5 N — the force falls because the shaft has escaped over the top of the pads. Nine of sixteen hands rise more than their own 12.5 mm radius at 10 mm and finish riding on thumb_pip_frame.

The fitter's own hold probe cannot catch this. It measures retained lift against the object's height before the close, so a tool thrown 18 mm upward reads as 18 mm of successful lift — held ≈ lift_probe + dz is an identity. Grasps “passing” a 20 mm gate at 65–75 mm on a 50 mm probe passed by the amount they ejected the tool. _grip_from_fit then passes hold_min=−1.0 and discards the gate anyway.

The three failure modes, as they are filmed

What is not settled, and what would settle it

  1. The gait palm height is scanned against the wrong ring. probe_real_v1_chain.py minimises the closed-ring IK residual over palm heights 30–72 mm, but the descent that topples the tool happens on the open ring. --gait-scan grip|open|both is in the chain now; the 384-cell A/B over 16 hands × {2, 4} mm × 4 seeds decides it, and it is the only change that addresses the dominant loss.
  2. The close probe is a gate that is not being applied. close_nonpad ≥ 2 selects exactly the hands that eject the tool (sv1_w6689_b050, _b060 at every squeeze, sv1_u5855_b050 at 2 mm), and those hands account for 0/16 and 0/4. Applying it in prepare() removes them from the denominator on evidence rather than by hand.
  3. The squeeze optimum is bracketed but not resolved. The carry wants more and the handover wants less; 2 mm and 4 mm are within two runs of each other over 64. Per-hand selection is worth {{BEST}}/{{N}} against {{OK2}}/{{N}}, on n=4 per cell, which does not separate a 3-run difference from noise. Eight seeds per cell on the surviving hands would.
  4. The tip-down variant has no measurement at all here. Every run on this page stands the tool on its handle on a flat table. Standing it tip-down into the 45° countersink needs --stand-order pivot, which stalls at 34° on the shipped heading and reaches 8.79° when the tool is laid down rotated 180°. The tool's heading on the bench is therefore a spec, and the seated version is unmeasured across the population.
  5. The gait gain is not guarded against the gear ceiling. The pad ring is a 1.684 gear, so a 30° stroke cannot exceed 50.5° per cycle; gain_mean_deg reads −67 to −86 on g12 and +188 on u5860. Those are the shaft being spun or thrown, and they currently read as fast gaits.
