D6 reorientation policy on the calibrated plant at 60 M steps

A finger-only policy on the calibrated plant turns the screwdriver from flat to cos {{COS60}} ± {{SD60}} ({{DEG60}}° from vertical) and holds it there on three pads in 64 of 64 deterministic rollouts. The tool rolls across the pads between steps 80 and 200 with no finger leaving the shaft. The same recipe at 20 M steps reads cos {{COS20}}: the plateau reported on 2026-09-16 was an under-trained snapshot, not a workspace ceiling. A second 60 M run of the same recipe and seed, differing only in a grip term that contributed 0.3 % of the return, stops at cos {{COS60M}} ({{DEG60M}}°), also held 64 of 64: the recipe’s run-to-run band on this hand is at least 0.29 wide. Both 60 M policies clamp at 17–18 N over the turn; a 20 M finetune of the better one with the grip term sized to the reward manager’s dt (weight −5 above 4 N per pad) takes the grip to {{FT_TOT}} N and the turn to cos {{COSFT}} ± {{SDFT}}, held 64 of 64, crossing 0.9 forty steps sooner. Two defects found on the way are recorded here: the evaluator applied the policy’s residual from step 0 while training masked it until step 58, which turned a held tool into a dropped one standing on its end; and the grip-force term was sized without the reward manager’s dt scaling, so at ±0.25 it did nothing in either direction.

The 2026-09-16 page read the 20 M policy’s cos 0.43 as the contact-fixed workspace ceiling of D6’s fitted grasp (the rigid-IK map put it at 20°). The 60 M run passes through that value at iteration 330 and continues to 0.93. The map was for a turn with fixed contacts; the policy rolls the tool on the pads, which the map does not describe. The ceiling claim is withdrawn.

A stopping rule for the ACCESS queue has to come from a per-checkpoint evaluation, because the episode reward keeps rising after the deterministic held-cos has stopped moving (the reward sums cos over the steps after 58, so it grows as the turn gets earlier). Checkpoints are saved every 50 iterations; evaluating models 300–800 under the corrected evaluator gives the held-cos curve the rule is set from. That evaluation is queued behind the run in progress.

The evaluator applied the residual through the lift

morphohand.rl.deploy.make_env_cfg · finger_residual_active_from_step default 0, trained value 58 · fixed 2026-09-17 by reading the run’s config.yaml

{{TABLE_EVAL}}

The recipe masks the policy’s residual until step 58, after the scripted lift has finished, and the runs train that way. make_env_cfg, which policy_eval_suite.py, rl_render_rollout.py and four other scripts build their environment from, did not take that parameter and left it at 0. Under that evaluator the model-812 policy acts on the tool from the first step, during a lift it never controlled. Its residual pushes the grip before the tool is off the post, and in {{R0_LOST}} of 64 rollouts the tool leaves the hand at step {{R0_DROP}} and lands on the floor, often standing on its end (z = 50 mm, the half-length), where the alignment score reads 0.99 with no pad touching it. The evaluator reported {{R0_ALIGNED}} of 64 aligned and {{R0_HELD}} of 64 held; with sampled actions {{R0S_ALIGNED}} aligned and {{R0S_HELD}} held. Training logged episode lengths of 249.9 of 250 and a mean object height of 131 mm at the same iteration, which is what exposed the mismatch.

make_env_cfg now takes finger_residual_active_from_step, reorient_start_step and lift_phase_start_step; run_env_overrides(checkpoint) reads them (with finger_residual_scale and finger_close_easing) from the config.yaml beside the checkpoint, and the eval suite and render script apply them and print what they applied. policy_healthcheck.py, probe_obs_ablation.py, probe_joint_envelope.py and rl_demo_handoff_continuous.py still call the builder with the default and need the same change before they are used on a b_liveA-style checkpoint. Every evaluation of a residual-from-58 policy made before this date with these tools carries the same defect; the only such evaluations are the two D6 runs on this page.

The grip-force term at ±0.25 did nothing, and a second run of the recipe stops at 50°

grip_force_excess: quadratic in the per-pad force above 4 N, scale 4 N, mean over pads · returns a positive magnitude, so the weight must be negative · the reward manager multiplies every term by the step dt (0.02 s) · pad forces from the deterministic render, mean over the steps named

{{TABLE_RUNS}}

The first 60 M run was launched with the weight at +0.25 (a bonus for force above 4 N per pad); the second with −0.25 and nothing else changed. Neither mattered: at 0.25 × 0.02 = 0.005 per unit of squared excess per step, against 100 × 0.02 = 2.0 per unit of alignment, the term summed to +0.09 and {{GM812}} per episode against 54 and {{ALM812}} for alignment. The two runs are therefore two draws of the same recipe and seed on a non-deterministic GPU solver, and they end at cos {{COS60}} and {{COS60M}}. Both hold 64 of 64 and both roll the tool the same way; the second stops at {{DEG60M}}° from step 140 and stays there. The turn-quality band of this recipe on D6 at 60 M steps is at least that wide, which is the seed-dominance result of the m05 program repeated on the calibrated plant, and it means one run per arm cannot rank a change to the recipe.

Grip: the 20 M policy averages 12.5 N over the active phase; both 60 M policies 17–18 N, rising to 20–23 N in the last 50 steps, with the thumb at 11 N. The chain runs D6 at 6.9 N total and the bench servos trip their overload protection well below 11 N on one pad. A weight that can move this in the units the manager applies is −5: an 11 N thumb then costs 0.10 per step, a third of the alignment term’s 0.28, and a 6 N thumb 0.008. That run is a finetune of the cos-{{COS60}} checkpoint (actor and critic warm-started, terminations unchanged, 20 M steps, results/rl/20260917-1844-d6_cal_reorient_ft_gpm5_20M_s0) rather than a fresh draw, so that the band above does not swallow the effect.

What this does not settle

  • The grip. {{FT_TOT}} N total with a {{FT_THUMB_MAX}} N thumb peak, against the 5–6 N per pad the bench servos held at μ 1.0 on the cap grasp: whether the thumb peak trips the overload protection, and whether the softer grip survives the real pads’ friction, are bench questions. A threshold at 3 N, or the spread term, is the next step down in simulation.
  • The chain. No benchmark checkpoint has run inside the chain environment. The adapter (the 66-dim observation from the CPU state, act_b, the residual on the chain’s finger ctrl during its reorient phase, inside probe_real_v1_chain.py) tests the arm lift, the 100 mm wrist stack, the screw-tip mesh, the plate at 0 and the CPU solver in one rollout. Until it runs, this page says nothing about the UR5e chain.
  • Seeds and hands. Two draws of one seed on one hand, 0.29 apart. Three more D6 seeds at this budget give the band properly, and any comparison between recipes needs either that many draws per arm or a finetune from a shared checkpoint; the other seven hands need a cal morphology run each at the squeeze the chain’s arm lift held (D8 at 12 mm; D1 and D2 pop the tool out at 10 mm on cal) and the evaluator’s lift as the gate.
  • What the policy reads. The observation carries the tool pose. The input-replay test (probe_obs_ablation.py, replay mode, after it takes the run’s timing) says whether the turn depends on it; if it does, the next run blinds the actor to those terms (actor_blind_terms).
  • A stopping rule. Held-cos at checkpoints 300–800 under the corrected evaluator, then stop when it has not moved by 0.02 over the last 150 iterations. Not yet measured.
