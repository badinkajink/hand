In-hand screwdriver reorientation on the calibrated plant by pinch and swing

On the bench-calibrated plant, the contact-fixed turn of every earlier result reaches only 0–20° from any grasp the fitter accepts. Releasing the middle finger instead lets the screwdriver swing tip-down about the thumb–index pinch under its own weight, ending at cos +0.92–0.98 on two pads. D8 does it 6 of 6 at a 12 mm squeeze, D6 with the thumb at the index’s station, and D2 and D4, the bench’s 10-of-10 and 9-of-10 hands, with the thumb at 30 mm. The swing fails at kp 0.25 and above a pad torsional friction of 0.003–0.01.

Contact-fixed turn on a compliant hand

D6, calibrated plant, the chain’s own turn: per-step joint, tip and contact record

The chain’s turn rotates the fingertips about a pivot between the driver pads, with joint angles from a rigid IK model. On D6 the yaw joints move 18–40 % of their command (thumb 2.5° of 13.7, index 5.0° of 21.1, middle 11.4° of 28.5): the actuator torque, about 0.15 N·m, is balanced by friction at the pads, which creep over the shaft at 6–10 mm/s, and the tool turns 4.7°.

The rigid IK never reached the arcs: carry_ik is 42 mm short of the tips a 90° rotation needs. Under the elliptic cone at impratio 10 the yaws move even less (thumb +2.3° of −13.2), the pads hold, and the tool leaves the hand when the index pad loses contact. The kp 30 plant forced this motion against friction; the servo cannot.

Reachable rotation from the fitted grasp

From the lifted state the index is 6 mm short of its arc at 10°: it sits nearly straight (MCP 8°, PIP 13°) and a tip-down rotation asks it to extend. The fitter places the grasp 62.5 mm below the mounts, 95 % of the 68 mm chain, and rejects shallower depths at this straddle because a flexed pad would sit inside the shaft.

Pinch and swing

release the middle, keep thumb and index, let gravity turn the tool about the pinch line

A rotation about the line through two contacts needs no finger motion. With thumb and index pinching the shaft and the middle pad off it, the tool’s centre of mass lies 15–30 mm from the pinch line and gravity turns the long end down; the tool is laid with its tip on that side (--yaws 180). On D6 the middle finger has room to release (MCP 27° against the index’s 8°): pulling its command 20 mm out moves it 2–5 mm off the shaft.

The contact model decides whether the swing appears: under the template’s pyramidal cone at impratio 1 the released pad still reads 0.26 N and the tool stays; under the elliptic cone at impratio 10 it swings. At the flat pads’ friction of 2.4 the swing stops at 0.3 N of residual contact; at 1.0 it goes; at 0.6 the pinch loses the tool on D6 and holds it on D8.

Swing across the eight hands

released finger, thumb station, pinch squeeze · tip-down heading · kp 0.5 and 0.25

The swing succeeds when the released pad actually leaves the shaft and the remaining pinch holds the swinging tool. Both are grasp properties the fitter never scored. With the plans’ grasp (thumb 10 mm from the shaft centre, index at 32–40, middle at −32 to −43) the middle releases on D3, D6, D7 and D8 and stays on the shaft on D1, D2, D4 and D5; the pinch holds on D6 and D8, holds D3 once squeezed 4–8 mm, and drops on D7 (1.0 N per pad). Moving the thumb to the index’s station makes a pinch line perpendicular to the shaft: D7 then holds, D2 and D4 swing, and D6 keeps the tool through the whole chain.

{{TABLE_HANDS}}

Seeds, squeeze and plant

Six seeds at 0.5 mm of spawn jitter on the three hands that swing at the plans’ thumb station. D8 needs the 12 mm squeeze to lift at all and then swings 6 of 6; D6 swings 4–5 of 6 at 8–10 mm and the extra pinch squeeze hurts it (the tool is pushed onto the middle pad, which then cannot leave); D3 swings partially. At kp 0.25, the plant the settled re-fit implies, no hand swings: the pinch force halves to ~1 N per pad and the tool slides out during the swing on every hand. The pinch force is commandable (flexion is the direction these fingers have room in), so this margin can be sized on the bench.

{{TABLE_SEEDS}}

Friction and torsional friction at the pinch

D8 at 12 mm squeeze. Pad friction 0.6–1.0 swings fully; 1.2 swings to +0.72; 1.5 does not release. Torsional friction at the contacts (condim 4, coefficient in metres of effective patch radius) of 0.003 halves the swing and 0.01 stops it; the simulator’s default point contact has none. The real pads are flat 21 mm faces on a 25 mm shaft and their torsional resistance is unmeasured. This number decides whether the swing transfers, and a two-finger pinch with the middle opened, read by the vane tag, measures it.

Future work

  1. Bench swing test. Grip as the plan does, open the middle finger and read the vane tag, on D8 and D6 at 10 and 12–14 mm of squeeze, then on D2, D4 and D7 with the thumb re-fitted to 20–30 mm (real_v1_export_plan.py, thumb_axial). Accept a swing past 60° kept in 4 of 5 trials on any hand; no swing at any squeeze puts the pad’s torsional friction above 0.005 and calls for a rounder pad.
  2. Pad torsional friction and μ. From that test, the squeeze at which the swing starts, against the 0.0036 N·m gravity torque, gives the torsional coefficient, and the squeeze at which the pinch drops the tool gives μ; set cal from them.
  3. Grasp fitted for the swing. Score in fit_real_v1_pose.py the radial room for the middle pad to leave the shaft and the thumb at the index’s station (now set by hand with --thumb-axials).
  4. Re-close after the swing. Close the middle finger on the vertical shaft at the pinch station, turning the two-pad hang into a three-pad hold before the carry; the chain’s 0.3 mm regrip helps D2 and D7 and hurts D6.
  5. Policy on this plant. Train a reorientation residual on cal over the pinch-and-release sequence with the tag pose observed; the plant drops the tool at kp 0.25, μ 0.6 or torsional friction 0.01, which gives the policy a disturbance to correct.
