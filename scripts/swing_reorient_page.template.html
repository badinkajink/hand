In-hand screwdriver reorientation on the calibrated plant by pinch and swing

On the bench-calibrated actuator with Coulomb-like contact, the fingers stand the screwdriver up tip-down without the arm: one finger releases and the tool swings about the thumb–index pinch under its own weight. D8 does it 6 of 6 at a 12 mm squeeze, D6 2 of 2 with the thumb at the index’s station, and D2 and D4, the bench’s 10-of-10 and 9-of-10 hands, with the thumb at 30 mm. The contact-fixed rotation every earlier result used is unreachable from the fitter’s grasp on this hand, 0–20° for any grasp it accepts, and the rigid plant had been executing it by force.

Why the contact-fixed turn stops on a compliant hand

D6, calibrated plant, the chain’s own turn: per-step joint, tip and contact record

The chain’s turn rotates the achieved fingertips about a pivot between the driver pads and solves the joint angles that put the tips on those arcs with a rigid IK model. The per-step record on D6 shows where the command goes. The yaw joints, which carry the turn, move 18–40 % of what they are told: thumb 2.5° of 13.7, index 5.0° of 21.1, middle 11.4° of 28.5. The error grows in proportion to the command, the actuator torque (0.5 N·m/rad × 0.3 rad ≈ 0.15 N·m, matching the recorded maximum) is balanced by tangential force at the pads, and the pads creep over the shaft at 6–10 mm/s. The tool turns 4.7°.

Two things in the record say why. The rigid IK never reached the arcs: carry_ik is 42 mm, the distance by which the fingers fall short of the tips a 90° contact-fixed rotation needs. And under a Coulomb-like contact model (elliptic cone, impratio 10) the yaws move even less (thumb +2.3° against −13.2 asked), the pads hold, and the tool leaves the hand when the index pad loses contact at u = 0.91. The commanded motion is not a rigid rotation the fingers can make; it is executed only by force against friction, which the kp 30 plant had and the servo does not.

The reachable rotation from the grasp is 0–20°

Per-finger IK residual of the contact-fixed rotation from the lifted state, over angle and pivot height, D6. At 10° the index is already 6 mm short of its arc: it sits at mcp 8°, pip 13°, nearly straight, and a tip-down rotation asks it to extend. The fitter places the grasp at 62.5 mm below the mounts, 95 % of the 68 mm mount-to-pad chain, and refuses every shallower depth at this straddle because a flexed finger’s pad lands inside the shaft. The pads can move up and along the shaft from this grasp, nothing else.

Pinch and swing

release the middle, keep thumb and index, let gravity turn the tool about the pinch line

A rotation about the line through two contact points needs no finger motion at all. With the thumb and index pinching the shaft at one station and the middle pad off the shaft, the tool’s centre of mass is 15–30 mm from the pinch line and gravity turns it until the long end hangs below. Which end drops is set by which side of the pinch the centre lies on, and the tool is laid on the table with its tip on that side (--yaws 180). On D6 the middle is the finger with room to release (27° of mcp against the index’s 8°); pulling its command 20 mm out takes it 2–5 mm off the shaft, and that is enough.

The contact model decides whether any of this is visible. Under the template’s pyramidal cone at impratio 1 the released middle pad still reads 0.26 N and the tool does not move; under the elliptic cone at impratio 10 it swings. The pad friction of 2.4 the flat pads ship with brakes the swing at 0.3 N of residual contact; at 1.0 it goes, at 0.6 the two-pad pinch loses the tool on D6 and holds it on D8.

Eight hands, three knobs

released finger, thumb station, pinch squeeze · tip-down heading · kp 0.5 and 0.25

The swing succeeds when the released pad actually leaves the shaft and the remaining pinch holds the swinging tool. Both are grasp properties the fitter never scored. With the plans’ grasp (thumb 10 mm from the shaft centre, index at 32–40, middle at −32 to −43) the middle releases on D3, D6, D7 and D8 and stays on the shaft on D1, D2, D4 and D5; the pinch holds on D6 and D8, holds D3 once squeezed 4–8 mm, and drops on D7 (1.0 N per pad). Moving the thumb to the index’s station makes a pinch line perpendicular to the shaft: D7 then holds, D2 and D4 swing, and D6 carries the tool through the whole chain.

Seeds, squeeze and plant

Six seeds at 0.5 mm of spawn jitter on the three hands that swing at the plans’ thumb station. D8 needs the 12 mm squeeze to lift at all and then swings 6 of 6; D6 swings 4–5 of 6 at 8–10 mm and the extra pinch squeeze hurts it (the tool is pushed onto the middle pad, which then cannot leave); D3 swings partially. At kp 0.25, the plant the settled re-fit implies, no hand swings: the pinch force halves to ~1 N per pad and the tool slides out during the swing on every hand. The pinch force is commandable (flexion is the direction these fingers have room in), so this is a control margin to size on the bench, not a wall.

Friction and torsional friction at the pinch

D8 at 12 mm squeeze. Pad friction 0.6–1.0 swings fully; 1.2 swings to +0.72; 1.5 does not release. Torsional friction at the contacts (condim 4, coefficient in metres of effective patch radius) of 0.003 halves the swing and 0.01 stops it; the simulator’s default point contact has none. The real pads are flat 21 mm faces on a 25 mm shaft and their torsional resistance is unmeasured. It is the one number that decides whether the swing transfers, and a two-finger pinch with the middle opened, read by the vane tag, measures it directly.

What this settles, and the next measurements

  • The finger-only reorientation is recovered on the calibrated plant, as a release-and-swing about a two-pad pinch, with the tool ending vertical tip-down in the hand at cos +0.92–0.98 on two pads at 1.8–3.2 N. It runs on the hands the bench holds best once the thumb station is set per hand. It does not run at kp 0.25 at the plans’ squeeze.
  • Bench test, first. Grip as the plan does, then open the middle finger to its open pose and read the vane tag: does the tool swing, how far, and does the pinch keep it. Do it on D8 and D6 at the plans’ 10 mm squeeze and at 12–14, then on D2, D4 and D7 with the thumb re-fitted to 20–30 mm (real_v1_export_plan.py takes thumb_axial). Acceptance: a swing past 60° retained on 4 of 5 trials on any hand. Falsification: no swing at any squeeze, which puts the pads’ torsional friction above 0.005 and sends the design to a rounder pad. This is the same station command sequence as the bench regrip; no new software.
  • Measure the pad’s torsional friction and μ from that test: the squeeze at which the swing starts, against the 0.0036 N·m gravity torque, gives the torsional coefficient; the squeeze at which the pinch drops the tool gives μ. Then set cal from measurements instead of the 1.0 / 0 assumed here.
  • Re-fit the grasp for the swing. fit_real_v1_pose.py should score two things it does not: radial room for the middle pad to leave the shaft, and the thumb at the index’s station. --thumb-axials in the chain driver is the manual version; the fitter is where it belongs.
  • The re-close is the next element. After the swing the tool hangs from two pads. The chain’s regrip (0.3 mm on all three, from the lying-tool command) helps on D2 and D7 and hurts on D6; a middle re-close targeted at the now-vertical shaft at the pinch station is the primitive that turns the hang into a three-pad hold before the carry.
  • An RL policy on this plant is now a well-posed problem. The plant drops the tool (kp 0.25, μ 0.6, torsional 0.01), so there is a disturbance to learn, and the physics says the feasible in-hand turn is release-and-catch, not a contact-fixed roll. A reorient policy trained on cal with the tag pose in its observation and a residual on the pinch-and-release sequence is the training run to make; the 2026-08 policies were trained on the rigid plant against a roll the servo cannot produce.
