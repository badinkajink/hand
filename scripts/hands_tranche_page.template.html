The two arms, the observation, and why the residual is bounded

Every policy here is a sighted actor: the 66-dim observation carries the tool pose in the palm frame (object_pos, object_pose_actual, target_axis_misalign), which on the bench the AprilTag tracker has to supply to the controller at 50 Hz. That dependence is real, not nominal: with those three terms zeroed at run time in CPU MuJoCo, both sighted 60 M D6 checkpoints drop the tool in 4 of 4 rollouts and the grip finetune turns it to the wrong pole (cos −0.7, held). A blind actor (asymmetric actor-critic, --actor-blind-terms: the critic keeps the pose, the actor reads servo position and velocity, the last action and the reference clock) warm-started from the sighted checkpoint did not recover the hold in its first 22 iterations (episodes ending at step 74 with the tool on the floor) and was stopped; the proprioceptive actor, with a motor-load term and an observation history in the RMA style, is a later item.

The unbounded D6 finetune of 2026-09-17 commands joint targets up to 384° beyond the joint range and, after clamping to the range, runs up to 100° ahead of the achieved joint: on the kp 0.5 plant that is a request for full force, on a real servo it is a stall into the overload cliff. Every job here therefore trains with the policy’s raw actions clipped to ±2, which with the 0.5 rad scale bounds the residual to ±1 rad around the grasp anchor (--clip-actions 2.0, applied in the rsl_rl wrapper and recorded in the run’s config so evaluation applies the same bound). The second arm adds a penalty on the index–middle link-chain clearance below 30 mm (surface to surface, three capsules of 10.55 mm radius per finger; weight −10, quadratic in the shortfall), because the built index and middle carry servo housings and cabling the capsules do not model and policies that run those two fingers close together have failed on the bench. The unbounded D6 policies are kept as the reference; the index–middle clearance of the D6 grip finetune is 18 mm at its minimum and 25 mm at the end.

The policy inside the UR5e chain

real_v1_chain_policy.py: the UR5e carries the hand through its 100 mm wrist stack; the chain’s grasp and lift are the training environment’s (--stages policy: ease-out closure from the open keyframe to the grasp anchor over 240 sim steps, the arm lift of 100 mm over 80, 260 of settle, the policy active from sim step 580 and evaluated in shadow from step 0 so its actions input is its own previous output as in training); the policy then drives the fingers at 50 Hz for 5 s · plant cal, plate at the built 25 mm and at the 0 every chain scene was generated with · one nominal rollout each · held = tool above 60 mm with ≥ 0.5 N on a pad when the 5 s end

{{TABLE_CHAIN}}

The arm holds its command. The menagerie UR5e servo (kp 2000 / kv 400 on the large joints, 500 / 100 on the wrist) is overdamped with its slow pole at kp/kv = 5 rad/s: 0.52 s after the 80-step lift the palm is still 4.9 mm below its command (shoulder 5.3 mrad, elbow 4.7, wrist_1 −10.4) and reaches 0.04 mm only a second later, so every chain rollout before 2026-09-20 handed the policy an arm that was still settling. That is not sag under load: the arm and the hand are gravity-compensated and a UR5e told its payload holds ~0.1 mm at the end of a move. The chain now runs the arm at ten times the position gain (arm_kp_scale 10, kv unchanged, slow pole 50 rad/s): 0.004 mm below command at the seam and through the turn. The finger bodies carry their own weight on the finger servos again (finger_gravcomp 0; the payload declaration had compensated the whole hand subtree, fingers included, which the training scene and the bench hand do not do).

With those three changes the observation the policy receives at its first active step in the chain matches the benchmark’s to within 1° on every finger joint, 1.3 mm on the tool position and 0.015 on the relative quaternion (D6, separation arm, obs_at_active in both JSONs); the only remaining scene difference is the screw-tip mesh on the chain’s tool, the “tip mesh” column of the zero-shot table. What this does not settle

  • The bench. The chain table is the sim-to-sim test; no policy here has run on the hardware. The station needs a policy-driven controller (the 66-dim observation from the servo telemetry and the AprilTag pose at 50 Hz, the actor, the residual on the finger set-points), and the tracker’s x/y heading has to be calibrated first, since the policy reads the tool position in the palm frame.
  • The tip mesh. The training scene’s tool is a plain 25 mm cylinder; the chain’s and the real screwdriver carry the tip. Its column in the zero-shot table is the cost; a morphology run built with the tip mesh (build_screw_scene.py on the flat scene before make_cal_morphology_run.py) removes it from the next tranche.
  • Solver-side fragility. A draw that holds on the GPU and drops on the CPU at the same friction (D6 clip) is a policy on a knife edge, and the bench is a third solver. The CPU probe’s held fraction across the perturbation columns is the ranking to use for bench candidates, not the GPU hold rate alone.
  • The clearance threshold. 30 mm between modelled capsule surfaces is a guess at the width the servo housings and cabling add; a caliper on the built index and middle at their closest approach during a bench turn gives the number.
  • D8. Its fitter grasp drops the tool in the evaluator’s palm lift at 4–14 mm of squeeze, with the plate at 0 or 25 mm and with a 200-step ramp; the chain’s arm lift held it at 12 mm. A grasp search for D8 on the calibrated plant (or the arm-lift reset) comes before it trains.
  • One draw per cell. The three D6 clip seeds end at 0.834 (56 of 64 held), 0.965 and 0.962: one of three draws of the same recipe is the fragile one, so a hand’s row here is one draw and a hand-versus-hand difference smaller than 0.13 in cos or 8 of 64 in the hold is not resolved. Three seeds per cell is the minimum for a ranking.
  • The separation arm. It turns D3, D5 and D1 (0.95, 0.93, 0.95) at 5.7–11.7 mm of clearance, below the 30 mm target, and takes the turn away from D4 (0.81, 4 of 64 aligned, 21 mm) and D7 (0.81, 0 aligned, 8 mm): those two turn by running the index and middle together. A weight between −3 and −10 and a threshold at the calipered clearance are the next two sweeps, on D4 and D7 only.
  • The CPU-side reconstruction. Whether the six policies the two solvers disagree on are fragile or the CPU reconstruction is wrong for them is decided by one run: the GPU evaluator on the CPU-replayed initial state, or the CPU rollout started from the GPU’s step-0 state, for D1. Until then the chain column for those six is not evidence either way.
