Chain on the bench-calibrated plant

On the plant fitted to the bench on 2026-09-02 (finger gain kp 0.5, about 60× softer than the template’s), the chain’s 2 mm squeeze leaves 0–1.43 N of grip at the lift, and the plans’ own 10 mm lifts every hand at 2.5–4.5 N. The fingers then turn the tool only 0–10° at any travel budget, lagging their commands by 25–36°. The arm stands the tool up, and five hands set it into the countersink in 17–20 of 20 rollouts. The hand-over loses 2–8 of 20 and the gait turns the tool at most 0.02 revolutions, from the same lag.

Grip at the lift on both plants

scripts/real_v1_chain_hands.py --plant shipped,corrected

The chain scenes are generated from the base template and inherit its finger actuators: kp 30, forcerange ±10, no friction, capsule-volume masses. The servo system identification fitted the real gain at kp 0.5 against the bench’s own hold deficits and cross-validated it on retention (0.94 sim, 0.92 bench) and alignment (0.638, 0.593). The 2,214-rollout record of 2026-09-08 never went through that model. --plant corrected rewrites each hand’s arm scene through apply_measured_plant.py, cached beside the shipped one.

Grip force at the lift falls from 4–25 N to 0.16–1.43 N. D8 does not close on the tool. D7, the hand that holds 10 of 10 on hardware, lifts at 0.16 N — below the tool’s 0.240 N. Force on a position servo is gain times position error, and the chain closes 2 mm past contact because at kp 30 the plans’ 10 mm produced ~30 N and ejected the tool. At kp 0.5 the same 2 mm is ~1 N.

Joint lag through the chain

Two new seam fields: the largest commanded-minus-achieved angle per finger, and the number of that finger’s actuators on the force ceiling. D6, 10 mm squeeze, pivot 0.15, budget 0.8, calibrated plant:

Through the turn the fingers sit 25–36° behind their commands and no actuator is on its ceiling. They are not tripping the torque limit; at kp 0.5 they do not develop the torque to roll the tool against the thumb. The bench recorded the same state on 2026-08-29 (“yaws stall; index yaw does not move at all”) and +11.7° of middle-yaw deficit at a static hold. The shipped plant reads 0.0° on every finger at every seam, which is why it never showed this.

The pivot-height result of 2026-09-06 was therefore a rigid-actuator artifact. Which pole the tool turns to is decided by a finger motion that does not happen on the real plant.

The arm’s iterated rigid transfer stands the held tool up: D3, D4, D5, D6 and D7 reach staged at cos +0.999–1.000 on three pads at 1.3–2.1 N, and set it into the countersink 17–20 of 20. D7 and D4 are the hardware 10/10 and 9/10 hands. D2, the other 10/10 hand, loses the tool between regripped and staged; D1 and D8 lose it at the regrip. The handover then costs each surviving hand 2–8 of 20, and the gait turns the tool 0.00–0.02 revolutions and leaves it at 8–75°.

The handover and the gait fail the way the turn does. At handover_thumb the thumb is 27° behind its command; at handover_index the index is 42° behind; through the gait the middle finger runs 14–38° behind. Both maneuvers are position-command sequences composed on the rigid plant, and on the calibrated plant the fingers execute a fraction of each move, so the tripod never forms and the stroke turns air.

Future work

The grasp is set at the plans’ 10 mm squeeze, and on this plant the turn is an arm reorientation; the open loss is the hand-over and the gait, caused by 14–42° of commanded-minus-achieved joint angle, which the servos report at 111 Hz. Later pages corrected this plant: the fingers have a 1.04 s time constant (2026-09-23), and the 2026-10-06 refit gives kp 4.

  1. Joint-angle loop. Command target + k(target − achieved) on the host, the integral term the SCS0009 lacks, and re-run the hand-over and gait; accept if q_err_deg through handover_* falls under 10° on D6 and the gait turns at least 0.5 revolutions in 8 cycles, reject if the loop saturates the actuators (sat > 0) first.
  2. D2’s regrip. D2 holds 10 of 10 on hardware and loses the tool at the arm transfer here on every seed; instrument its 0.3 mm re-squeeze from the commanded pose.
  3. Plant validated on the chain. The plant was fitted on the bench maneuver; its 0.35 N·m ceiling is the protective-torque plateau, which the servo reaches only after sustained overload.
