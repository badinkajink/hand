Pinch-brake loading of the screwdriver on the SR2 hand in Drake and MuJoCo

On the SR2 hand (D8 gantry, calibrated position servos), thumb and index lower the real_v1 screwdriver from horizontal to hanging by relaxing their pinch, the load step of Wang, Oh and Pollard. Drake with hydroelastic fingertips at a 0.01 s relaxation passes 45° at {{H45_D}} N and 80° at {{H80_D}} N of pad force; MuJoCo with condim 4 and a rescheduled μt passes them at {{H45_C4}} and {{H80_C4}} N, and MuJoCo with sphere-packed fingertips at {{H45_SP}} and {{H80_SP}} N. The two-pad rig’s quasi-static law predicts {{Q45}} and {{Q80}} N. At Drake’s default 0.1 s relaxation the tool falls through the lower half of the swing once it moves, and with point contact in either simulator it swings away at release.

D8 gantry from sv1_w0099_b100_plan.json baked into real_hand.xml, palm fixed 250 mm up · fingers: kp 0.5 N·m/rad, kv 0.02, effort 0.35 N·m, no joint damping or friction loss · tool 24.5 g, r 12.5 mm, centre of mass d from the pinch line · μ 1.0, 1 ms step · Drake {{DRAKE_V}} SAP, MuJoCo {{MUJOCO_V}} impratio 100 · scripts/hom_hand_brake.py, scripts/hom_hand_drake.py · the contact models are characterized on the two-pad rig, page ({{RIG_PATH}})

Setup

one grasp, one force schedule, eight contact models, two centre-of-mass offsets

The pinch point sits 45 mm below the gantry plane, midway between the thumb and index mounts, with the tool axis horizontal and perpendicular to the thumb–index line; the middle finger is parked straight and tilted back. IK places the thumb and index tip spheres touching the tool. The pinch force is commanded through the position servos as q = qtouch + JT(F û) / kp, so each pad pushes along the pinch axis; a target placed inside the tool by IK instead pushes along the servo’s joint-space stiffness, and in a first run that yawed the tool 20° out of the swing plane while the fingers closed. The fingers close on the tool at F = 4 N with its weight compensated, the weight is released, F is held for 0.6 s and then lowered geometrically to 0.5 N over 4 s.

The calibrated plant’s joint friction loss (0.0035 N·m) is left out of both simulators because Drake’s SAP has none. On this hand it costs about 0.2 N of pad force at low commands, and with it a 0.27 N command drops the tool.

What this does not settle

  1. The grasp. The tool is placed in the pinch by IK in the air. On the 2026-09-16 swing grasps, which pick the tool off the table, it already hangs on two pads when the lift ends, so the brake has to be set before the lift. The next run is this force schedule inside probe_real_v1_chain.chain() on the D8 swing grasp, in MuJoCo with condim 4 rescheduled at impratio 100.
  2. The servo. The force command assumes the bench servo is the calibrated kp 0.5 with no friction loss. In MuJoCo the calibrated friction loss lowers the low end of the pad force by about 0.2 N. A bench step-down of the commanded force with the tag reading the angle measures both it and μt/μ (open since 2026-09-16).
  3. The pusher. The paper sets the final angle with a third finger pushing the tool about the pinch axis. Here the tool’s short end points toward the parked middle finger; whether the middle can reach and turn it is the paper’s Experiment 3 and is not run.
  4. The controller. The schedule is open loop. The paper’s relative-contact-velocity controller with generalized contact frames is not implemented.
