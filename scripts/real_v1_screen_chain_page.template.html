Screened plans in the chained task

Eight morphologies were exported to hardware trajectories and evaluated on the bench, then the same eight plans were run inside the chained task. The screen and the chain disagree, and the disagreement is not one effect: the grasp specification does not survive the change of support, one hand releases the tool after a successful lift, and half the runs that stand the tool upright lose it at the handover.

Axis alignment is the wrong acceptance test

docs/experiments/20260902-cb1-log-archive · {{NTRIAL}} trials carrying both a tag angle and an operator verdict

Retention is the operator's verdict that the cylinder is still in the hand at trial end. Net turn angle is object_track.deg_turned, the cylinder's net rotation about the pinch axis from an AprilTag on a rigid vane. Axis alignment is object_track.cos_hold, the signed cosine between the cylinder's long axis and world vertical over the terminal hold window.

{{T_BENCH}}
The gate that would fail

No hand reaches mean cos_hold 0.95, so a 0.95 gate admits nothing, and the single hand above 0.90 has the second-worst retention at 0.30. Alignment carries no information about retention in either direction: on the pre-registered analysis (paper/figures/ranking.json, dropped trials scored 0 for parity with the simulator) per-hand retention rate ranks against mean alignment at Spearman {{RHO_PAPER}} and against peak alignment at {{RHO_PEAK}}; on the per-trial table here, taking the median alignment over retained trials only, it is {{RHO_TRIAL}}. Both are within noise of zero at n = 8, and they do not agree on sign. Retention has to be measured, not inferred from how far the tool turned.

Pooled over all eight hands, {{BANDH}} of {{NHELD}} retained trials and {{BANDD}} of {{NDROP}} non-retained trials have a net turn angle between 20° and 60°. That is a descriptive interval over the pooled sample, not a per-hand threshold: three hands have no non-retained trials at all, and on two the non-retained trials turned less than the retained median, not more. There is no single angle above which the tool is lost.

The consequence for the chain is fixed by the same table. Skills 3 and 4 inherit the residual angle, 90° minus the median retained turn, and it runs from 24.5° to 56.5°.

Correction

An earlier report of “2 of 256 held reorientations” read held_reorient at the upright seam in table mode, where the maneuver stands the tool on the table and lets go by construction. It is the released-tool measurement. held_turn, measured while the fingers carry, is {{NTURN}}/{{NCHAIN}}.

Loss one: the plan's squeeze ejects a tool that is lying on a table

scripts/real_v1_grasp_refit.py · closure with the palm stationary

Every deployed plan carries squeeze_mm 10.0. That drives the pad centres to r_obj + r_pad + gap − squeeze = 14.05 mm from the shaft axis when contact needs 23.05 mm — 9 mm inside the surface. On the screen's post the post takes the reaction. On a table nothing is behind the shaft, so the pinch drives it out. close_dz_mm is the tool's rise during closure with the palm held still:

{{T_SQUEEZE}}

Six of eight rise 14.5–19.6 mm, more than the shaft's own 12.5 mm radius. Substituting 2.0 mm holds every hand to 4.0 mm or less, which is what the chain already does.

Not the depth

_grip_from_fit clamps its palm-height search to [depth − 8 mm, depth], so overriding squeeze without re-fitting depth is a plausible cause and is not the cause. Re-fitting with the depth search free at squeeze 2 mm returns the plan's own depth on six of eight hands, moves sv1_w6689 by 6.0 mm and two others by 1.0 mm, and changes contact count on none.

Loss two: one hand lifts the tool and releases it at the next seam

sv1_w6689_b060 · 24/24 retained on hardware, 0/16 carried in the chain

sv1_w6689_b060 is the best hand on the bench and the worst in the chain. It is not failing at the grasp: it closes on the tool with 3 pads at 28.20 N and lifts it to 100.8 mm. Its seam trace:

{{T_SEAM}}

Every later seam repeats the second row. rv05_manual_b85 on the same schedule reads 4 pads at 5.22 N lifted and 4 pads at 1.04 N turned, and completes the chain.

Three properties separate sv1_w6689 from the six that work and they are confounded in one hand: it is the only set-A hand at straddle_mm 32.0 (the rest are 40.0), the shallowest at grip_depth_mm 52.5 (the rest 56.5–66.5), and the only one with two non-pad contacts at closure.

What this does not settle

  • Which of the three differences releases the tool on sv1_w6689. The test is a 2×2 on that hand alone: straddle {32, 40} × depth {52.5, 58.5}, n ≥ 6, scored on pad count and pad force at the turned seam.
  • sv1_w0099_b100 at 6/16 has not been examined.
  • Whether the chain's turn is the screened turn. The chain runs in table mode with angle_deg 0, so the fingers never turn the tool. The screened maneuver's turn has not been run inside the chain on any hand.
  • The handover, {{NGRIP}}/{{NSTOOD}}. This is skills 3 and 4 and it is the largest single loss in the funnel. Nothing here measures it.
