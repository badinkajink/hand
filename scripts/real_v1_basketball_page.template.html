Palm plate height and the mount ring of a seated ball

assets/mjcf/real_v1 · PALM_PLATE_Z in build_real_v1_scenes.py

The palm plate of the physical hand sits 25 mm above the plane of the yaw servos. Since 2026-09-16 the simulated plate does too. Scenes generated before that date have the plate on the servo plane, 25 mm too close to the fingertips.

A ball pressed against the plate cannot sag. Seated, a size-7 ball has its centre {{SEATED_DEPTH_7}} mm below the mounting plane, where its cross-section is wider than the mount ring of every layout; the table gives the ring each size needs.

Reachable wrap angle per layout and ball size

forward-kinematic reach map on a 69 × 55 × 38 joint grid per finger, links clear of the ball

A pad at wrap angle θ pushes the ball down by \(N\cos\theta\) and lifts it by at most \(\mu N\sin\theta\), so it supports weight only when \(\tan\theta > 1/\mu\): beyond 45° at μ 1.0 and beyond 59° at μ 0.6. A finger reaches outboard of its mount by at most its 15° of MCP extension. On the wide layout the contacts on a size-7 ball pass outboard of the mounts at θ = {{THETA_OUTBOARD_7}}°, and beyond that the palm climbs and the plate gap grows. The table gives the reachable θ with the thumb on its meridian and the pair near ±60°, and the fitted grasp at the largest θ.

Every real_v1 scene runs the default. The screwdriver pinch is insensitive to it, because its normals oppose each other. In a cap grasp friction must also cancel the normals’ downward component, and at the default the pads lose their 0.5–1 mm of penetration in the first millimetre of lift. The hold results above use the grasp setting.

Future work

  1. Pad–ball friction. Drag a pad across an inflated ball under a known normal load on the bench scale. A size-7 hold needs μ ≥ {{MU_MIN_7}} on the measured servo; a lower value rules the grasp out on this hand.
  2. Pad force. Press a pad on the scale at a saturating command (joint error over 0.7 rad). The model gives {{N_PAD_MEASURED}} N; a larger force lowers the friction a hold needs.
  3. Gantry travel. Measure the travel of each gantry axis; 6 mm more on each would give the {{RING_REQ_7}} mm ring that seats a size-7 ball.
  4. Approach from above. Lower the palm onto the ball from 50 mm in the UR5e scene of scripts/build_real_v1_arm_scene.py (these rollouts start with the pads 8 mm off the ball); if the thumb reaches the cap before the pair closes, the fitted grasp is unreachable.
  5. Compliant pads. Repeat real_v1_basketball_grasp.py sweep with the 1 mm sphere-pad fingertips; a hold at lower μ would show that pad compliance helps the cap grasp.
