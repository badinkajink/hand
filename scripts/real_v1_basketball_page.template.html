The palm plate moved 25 mm up, and what that buys

assets/mjcf/real_v1 · PALM_PLATE_Z in build_real_v1_scenes.py

The flat palm of the physical hand sits 25 mm above the plane the three yaw servos are mounted on; until today the simulated plate was drawn on the mounting plane itself, 25 mm too close to the fingertips. The plate geom and the workspace sites now sit at +25 mm in the palm frame. Nothing else moved: mounts, link lengths, ROM and the 21 contact excludes are the CAD numbers, and the fitted open_ik keys of the screwdriver scene were carried through the rebuild (--keep-keyframes). Scenes generated before 2026-09-16 still carry the old plate.

For a ball the plate matters because a ball pressed against it cannot sag. A ball seated on the plate's underside puts its centre {{SEATED_DEPTH_7}} mm below the mounting plane for size 7, and its cross-section at the foot of the yaw links is then wider than the ring the three mounts sit on: the links need a ring of at least {{RING_REQ_7}} mm for size 7, {{RING_REQ_6}} mm for size 6, {{RING_REQ_5}} mm for size 5 and {{RING_REQ_3}} mm for size 3. The widest legal layout, every gantry at the end of its travel, rings the ball at {{RING_WIDE}} mm. The nominal layout rings it at {{RING_NOMINAL}} mm and the bench's best-retaining deployed hand, g12, at {{RING_G12}} mm.

What the fingers can reach on the ball

forward-kinematic reach map on a 69 × 55 × 38 joint grid per finger, links clear of the ball

A pad on the upper cap at polar angle θ from the top pole pushes the ball down by N cosθ and its friction lifts by at most μN sinθ, so a pad carries weight only once tanθ > 1/μ: 45° at μ 1.0, 59° at μ 0.6. The finger reaches outboard of its own mount by at most the 15° of MCP extension, and the contact ring at θ sits outboard of the mounts once (R + 10.55 mm) sinθ exceeds the mount ring, which on the wide layout is θ = {{THETA_OUTBOARD_7}}° for size 7. Past that the palm has to climb until the fingers are near full extension, and the plate gap grows. The table gives, per layout and size, the largest wrap angle at which all three pads land with the thumb on its meridian and the pair mirrored near ±60°, and the palm height that puts the ball nearest the plate at that angle.

Every real_v1 scene in this repo runs the default. The pinch grasps it was tuned on do not care, because their normals oppose each other and friction carries only the weight. A cap grasp carries the normals’ downward component on friction as well, so the frictional impedance sets whether the pads keep their 0.5–1 mm of penetration through the first millimetre of lift. The rows above are the grasp setting; the default is reported here as the sensitivity, and the bench decides which one the pads follow.

What this does not settle

  • The pad–ball friction coefficient. The hold on a size-7 ball needs μ ≥ {{MU_MIN_7}} at the measured servo torque; rubber on the TPU pad is probably 0.8–1.5 and has never been measured. Drag a pad across an inflated ball under a known normal load with the bench scale; one number moves this from feasible to infeasible.
  • The servo’s force at the pad. The measured plant delivers {{N_PAD_MEASURED}} N per pad here, from kp 0.5 and the 0.35 N m cliff fitted on the screwdriver deficits. Press a pad on the scale at a saturating command (any joint error over 0.7 rad) and read it directly.
  • The approach. Every rollout starts with the pads 8 mm off the ball at the fitted pose. Descending onto the ball from above lands the thumb on the cap before the pair are round it; that sequence needs the arm and is not simulated.
  • Pad and ball compliance. Rigid pads on a rigid sphere with MuJoCo’s soft constraints. A TPU pad flattening on an inflated ball adds contact area and torsional resistance the model does not have; both help a cap grasp.
  • Seating a size-7 ball. Needs a mount ring of {{RING_REQ_7}} mm against the {{RING_WIDE}} mm the gantries reach, 5 mm short on each finger. Size 5 clears the links by 0.2 mm on paper and no fitted grasp seated it; size 3 seats at θ 90°. The next measurement is the gantry travel itself: 6 mm more on each axis would seat a size-7 ball, and the CAD workspace boxes are the only record of the limit.
